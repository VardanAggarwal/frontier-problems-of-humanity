# India's impact capital stack — who writes what first cheque

Written 2026-09-11. A finding, not a plan; no action decided.

Produced by a deliberate test, not a reading pass. The question was a founder's:
*can you reach an Indian social-impact investor and actually raise a first cheque,
and how hard is that?* The test was run because an earlier round of claims in this
project's conversation turned out to be an artifact of how `../problems/actors/` was
built. The falsification trail is kept in §5 — it is the most useful part of the file.

## 1. Method

Eleven investors, two arms, blind to arm, at application-page level:

- **Impact arm (6):** Villgro, Social Alpha, UnLtd India, Upaya, Menterra, Ankur Capital
- **Commercial control (5):** 100X.VC, Antler India, Titan Capital, Blume, All In Capital

Five questions per investor: (1) is there an unsolicited inbound door at all;
(2) open now or window-gated; (3) published response commitment; (4) implied
acceptance rate; (5) first-cheque size and what it demands.

Two commercial fetches failed — Blume returned HTTP 403, All In Capital returned
nothing — so the control arm is thinner than the impact arm.

## 2. The ladder, by first cheque

| Tier | First cheque | Who | Instrument |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Grant / prize** | ₹1L–₹60L (~$1K–$73K) | UnLtd India (free incubation + small grant); Social Alpha (₹15L TechTonic → ₹60L Agritech Challenge); Villgro (prize/grant, avg $73.7K); Tata Social Enterprise Challenge (₹10L cash, ₹1Cr pitch) | Non-dilutive, milestone-gated, usually won via a challenge rather than a round |
| **1 — Micro-seed** | $44K–$200K (₹4L–₹1.7Cr) | Upaya (most recent deal $43.8K, Feb 2026); Villgro equity/quasi-equity (up to ₹60L / $0.1M) | Recoverable grant → loan (Upaya); quasi-equity |
| **2 — Seed** | $120K–$2M (₹1Cr–₹17Cr) | Menterra (₹1–4 Cr, from a ₹40Cr Fund I); Ankur Capital ($500K–$2M); Unitus Ventures ($50M AUM, 30–40 startups) | Equity / SAFE |
| **3 — Series A / early growth** | $3M–$25M | Elevar Equity ($3M–$10M first cheque); Lok Capital ($3M–$18M); Aavishkaar Capital ($5M–$25M) | Venture equity |
| **4 — Growth / debt** | $10M–$47M | Northern Arc; responsAbility; Triodos; Maanaveeya; Caspian | Structured debt, minority stake |

Data note — round size is not ticket size. The Tracxn "average seed round" figures
($792K, quoted for both Menterra and Lok Capital) are round totals including
co-investors, not what that fund wrote. Menterra's own stated ticket is ₹1–4 Cr.
The identical $792K for two different funds may be a garbled source; verify before
re-quoting either.

Data note — Villgro runs two instruments, not two estimates of one: "up to ₹60 lakh
($0.1M) equity/quasi-equity" and "grant (prize money), avg $73.7K per round".

Data caution — tiers 3 and 4 lean on `../problems/actors/`, which is funder-seeded
and over-samples large, visible funds. Tiers 0–1 are the best-evidenced, being
fetched directly during the test.

## 3. What the test found

**Door existence — no difference between arms; the impact arm is slightly better.**
6/6 impact funds have an open cold door with a form or a published email. Villgro,
UnLtd India, Menterra and Ankur publish an address and a phone number. Ankur states
the position outright — *"write to us directly. We read everything."* In the control
arm, Titan Capital publishes only a generic `info@` with no process disclosure and
Blume was unreachable. There is no evidence here that Indian impact capital is
relationship-gated relative to commercial capital.

**Selectivity — no difference either, where it is measurable.**
100X.VC Class 12: 1,900 applications → 304 shortlisted → 18 funded (0.95%).
Class 8: 4,748 → 669 → 25 (0.53%). Antler India: 9,200 founders / 7,700
applications → 110 joined the residency (1.4%), then ~50–75% of joined teams are
funded at ₹4 Cr for 11%. Villgro: 746 applications in FY2014-15, and 119 enterprises
incubated since 2001 (~5/yr) — order 0.7%.

Data caution — the Villgro 0.7% is not comparable to the 100X figures. Numerator and
denominator come from different years, and "applications across all stages of
incubation" is a wider object than a funding application. Order of magnitude only.

**The ladder is continuous in rupees and discontinuous in instrument.**
₹15L → ₹60L → ₹1–4Cr → $500K–$2M → $3M → $25M. No rung is missing by cheque size.
This cuts against the standard "missing middle" telling: Upaya's own definition of
the gap is enterprises seeking $20K–$2M, and that is the band India's impact stack
covers *best*.

The break is in instrument. The sequence is grant → grant → equity → *venture*
equity. Elevar, Lok and Aavishkaar underwrite venture-scale outcomes. Per Upaya's
own four-way segmentation, only 1% of the missing-middle segment is "High Growth"
tech-led; the rest are Niche, Dynamic Enterprises, and Livelihood Sustaining. A
business growing 15%/year and employing 200 people reaches ₹4Cr and finds the next
cheque up exists but is priced for a different asset class. Upaya states the general
form: *"the typical venture style equity investment doesn't fit well with any of
these groups"* — the $930bn gap is a mismatch in funding structure, not an absence
of capital.

So the shortfall is a **missing instrument in the ₹4Cr–₹25Cr band** — revenue-based
finance, long-tenor debt, mezzanine. Northern Arc, Caspian and Maanaveeya are the
beginning of an answer on the debt side, and the "from grants to debt" rethink is
live in the sector now.

**"Impact investor" spans 1,000× in cheque size under one label.**
₹15L to $25M. A founder needing ₹2Cr and a founder needing $15M are pointed at the
same category of people. No one publishes this ladder; it was assembled here from
six websites, two Tracxn profiles and a Lemelson press release, and two of the
numbers are still not trusted. Against this, every commercial fund publishes its
full funnel as marketing — applications → shortlist → selected, per cohort, in press
releases the trade press picks up — and Antler publishes a 3-week decision
commitment. Not one impact fund in the sample publishes a current funnel or any
response time. The only impact funnel figure findable anywhere was eleven years old,
in a PDF annual report.

The consequence is narrow and real: a founder can compute expected value before
applying in the commercial arm and cannot in the impact arm. This is a *legibility*
defect, not a reachability one.

## 4. Follow-on: the network works for the 1%, and that is survivorship evidence

Ecozen, Villgro's flagship: $76.4M across 16 rounds from 29 investors
(`../problems/actors/ecozen-solutions.md`), seeded by an incubator writing ~₹40L
cheques. Ten operator records in the corpus show multi-round chains. The pipe from
impact seed to commercial-scale growth capital demonstrably works.

It is also exactly the 1%. The corpus is funder-seeded, so it over-samples funded
winners; this is survivorship evidence, not a base rate. What cannot be concluded
from it is that follow-on capital is generally available to impact-seeded companies,
and what cannot be concluded from the missing middle is that follow-on capital does
not exist. Both readings were tried in conversation and both overshoot.

## 5. What this test falsified

Kept because the errors were structural, not incidental.

- **"India's impact ecosystem is relationship-gated / low accessibility."** Dead.
  The evidence offered for it was a funder:operator ratio drawn from a crawl seeded
  on funders and sampling portfolio edges at ≤4 per parent — an artifact of the
  crawler's design, not a fact about the world. A second figure ("12% of actors
  publish an email") measured what `actor-channel-finder` was briefed to look for,
  which was follow channels, not contact addresses.
- **"India cannot count its social enterprises."** False as stated. NGO Darpan holds
  94,684 registered NGOs with registration number, FCRA status, district, funding
  sources and named funding agencies — public and queryable.
- **"The UK can count its social enterprises."** Also false. The 100,000 figure is a
  survey extrapolation; other estimates of the same population are 131,000 (SEUK
  2023, after adding large co-ops and building societies) and a ~67,000 maximum
  (NCVO, including non-trading orgs). The only hard count is the CIC register,
  ~32,500 as of March 2024.

  Data caution — those three UK counts measure different populations under different
  definitions. Do not average them or read a trend across them.

- **What survives, narrowed:** India indexes the non-profit leg (Darpan) and not the
  for-profit leg. SEBI's Social Stock Exchange was built for that gap and has ~92
  NPOs registered on BSE and ~84 on NSE as of May 2026, of which only 11 have listed
  a ZCZP instrument. FPE registration numbers are not published. India has no legal
  form marking a for-profit as social, so it has no equivalent of the CIC register as
  a form-based filter.

The general lesson, which applies to the rest of this repo: a network crawl seeded on
one layer returns that layer, and a finding drawn from its topology describes the
seed. `../CLAUDE.md` already names the institutional-source bias for the tier files;
this is the same failure in the actor corpus.

## 6. Relation to `lens.md`

Not promoted to an axis. Logged as a candidate fourth axis: **instrument fit to
growth shape** — whether the capital on offer is priced for the growth curve the
enterprise actually has, independent of whether the enterprise is good or the capital
is plentiful. It is adjacent to axis 2 (ownership vs. payment) but not the same
question: axis 2 asks who owns the surplus, this asks whether any instrument exists
that will fund a 15%/year business at ₹10Cr. One domain is not enough to promote it;
per this repo's pace, it waits for a second.

## 7. What this cannot tell you

The decisive number is **cold-inbound response rate**, and homepage disclosure is not
behaviour. An open form nobody reads scores identically here to one answered in 48
hours. Getting that requires sending matched cold pitches to both arms and waiting
4–6 weeks. Until then, "the doors are open" means the doors exist, and nothing more.

Also: n=11; both arms are well-known names with the long tail untested; the control
arm is thinner than the impact arm because two fetches failed.
