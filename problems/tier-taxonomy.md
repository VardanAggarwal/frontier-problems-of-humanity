# Maslow's Hierarchy, Rederived — Tier Definitions & Topic Taxonomy

*Rederived from first principles (not cited from Maslow's original wording), then cross-checked against Maslow's later revisions, Max-Neef's Human Scale Development, Alderfer's ERG theory, Self-Determination Theory, and Kenrick et al.'s "Renovating the Pyramid of Needs."*

## The single underlying drive

One drive — **persist/propagate** — operating on progressively more abstract containers: cell/body → gene lineage/kin group → tribe/group → symbolic/abstract self (legacy, ideology, identity). Each layer inherits the anxiety structure of the one below it; a threat to reputation triggers the same stress response as a threat to the body because the mechanism never changed, only the object. The tiers below are not five different needs — they're one need recursing outward through nested objects.

They form a hierarchy not because higher needs matter less, but because of an **attention/resource-allocation rule under scarcity**: when a lower tier is acutely threatened, cognition gets captured by threat-monitoring, crowding out the bandwidth higher tiers need. Not strictly sequential in practice — people risk lower tiers for higher ones (hunger strikes, martyrdom) — so tiers 1–2 simply have the highest *override frequency*, not the highest importance.

## Tier 1 — Physiological (homeostasis)

**Definition:** short-horizon cellular/bodily survival. An organism dies within minutes to days without these; no cognitive process can override cell death.

1. Food/caloric intake
2. Water/fluid balance
3. Air/breathable environment
4. Shelter & thermoregulation
5. Sanitation & disease (external pathogen exposure)
6. Sleep/circadian regulation

## Tier 2 — Safety (threat-buffering projected over time)

**Definition:** surviving *today* isn't enough if you can be destroyed tomorrow — a buffer against unpredictable future threats to body, claims, and resources.

1. Violence protection
2. Rule of law & property/contract rights
3. Financial/economic continuity (currency, savings, banking)
4. Employment/livelihood security
5. Health safety net (injury/illness recovery infrastructure)
6. Disaster/institutional protection (population-scale shocks)
7. Information/epistemic security (ability to accurately predict threats — attacked by propaganda/censorship, distinct from the threats themselves)

## Tier 3 — Belonging (bonding as reproductive strategy + group cohesion)

**Definition:** offspring require years of care beyond what a solitary strategy justifies — bonding isn't preference, it's a precondition for this species' reproductive strategy. Group cohesion is the same mechanism scaled up.

1. Mate acquisition/courtship (competing-for phase, distinct from bond maintenance)
2. Pair-bonding/marriage (bond maintenance)
3. Parent-child attachment & child-rearing support
4. Kinship/lineage network
5. Peer/non-kin community bonds
6. Intergenerational transmission (language/skill/role passed forward)
7. In-group identity/cohesion (tribal/national "we-feeling," independent of blood ties)

## Tier 4 — Esteem (status as the resource-access mechanism within a group)

**Definition:** once inside a group, resource/mating access is gated by relative rank — esteem-seeking is instrumental, converting membership into actual access.

1. Hereditary/caste status
2. Ascribed-identity status (race/gender/immigrant status)
3. Economic/class status
4. Occupational/skill status
5. Political/power rank
6. Age/generational rank (elder vs. junior privilege)
7. In-group vs. out-group standing (one's whole group's rank against other groups)
8. Participation/voice (having a say in group decisions — distinct from relative rank itself)

## Tier 5 — Meaning (persistence of abstract/symbolic extensions of self)

**Definition:** once tiers 1–4 are met, the drive to persist/propagate points at constructs with no biological substrate — legacy, ideology, identity, cause. This doesn't escape mortality; it relocates the survival problem onto a slower-decaying but still finite substrate.

1. Religious/cosmological meaning
2. Political/national ideology
3. Occupational/vocational identity
4. Ethnic/cultural identity
5. Personal legacy/individual purpose
6. Collective memory/ancestor-continuity (being remembered / continuing a chain, distinct from group-membership identity)
7. Aesthetic/creative expression (meaning through making — art, craft, ritual)
8. Understanding/comprehension (curiosity/sense-making for its own sake, distinct from Tier 2's threat-focused information security)

## Cross-cutting axes (not tier-bound — check for these within every topic, not as separate topics)

- **Freedom/autonomy** — irreducible to safety or status per Self-Determination Theory and Max-Neef; shows up inside any tier (bodily, economic, political autonomy).
- **Leisure/play** — Max-Neef flags as fundamental, not derivative of rest; doesn't nest cleanly under homeostasis or meaning.
