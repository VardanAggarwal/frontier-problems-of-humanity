## Tier 1 — Physiological (food/water/shelter)

1. **Bronze Age Collapse, ~1200–1150 BCE.** Egyptian/Hittite records of crop failure and famine; multiple palace-states collapsed. Cause debated: drought (Kaniewski et al.) + trade-network breakdown.
2. **Fall of Rome, siege of 408–410 CE.** Alaric's blockade cut the annona grain fleet feeding ~800,000. Zosimus records starvation, cannibalism rumors.
3. **Great Famine, 1315–1317, N. Europe.** Rain/cold destroyed harvests; 10–15% mortality in places. Jordan: climate shift + no-surplus feudal agrarian system.
4. **Irish Famine, 1845–1852.** Potato blight; ~1M deaths, 1–2M emigrated. Ó Gráda: monocrop dependency from land-tenure system + continued food exports.
5. **Bengal Famine, 1943.** 2–3M deaths. Sen: not absolute shortage — wartime inflation, hoarding, British "denial policy," grain diverted to war effort.
6. **Soviet collectivization famines, 1930–33** (Kazakh famine + Holodomor). Kazakhstan ~1.5M deaths (38% of ethnic Kazakhs) from forced sedentarization; Ukraine 3.5–5M from requisition quotas exceeding harvests, borders sealed against flight.
7. **Great Leap Forward, China, 1959–1961.** 15–45M excess deaths (Dikötter, Yang Jisheng). Forced collectivization, backyard-furnace campaign, inflated output stats forcing over-requisition, suppressed reporting.
8. **Weimar hyperinflation, 1922–23.** Wages worthless faster than spendable; barter replaced markets despite food existing.
9. **Siege of Leningrad, 1941–44.** 1–1.5M civilian deaths, mostly starvation; rationing fell to 125g bread/day at worst.
10. **Yemen, 2016–present.** Near-famine from blockade + currency collapse; Yemen imports ~90% of staples.

## Tier 2 — Safety/Security (rule of law, institutions, savings, physical protection)

1. **Weimar hyperinflation, 1921–23.** Reparations + unbacked money printing; mark 4.2/USD → 4.2 trillion/USD. Middle-class savings/pensions wiped out.
2. **Weimar rule-of-law collapse, 1930–33.** Article 48 emergency rule bypassing Reichstag; unprosecuted paramilitary violence; Reichstag Fire Decree + Enabling Act (1933) ended constitutional constraint.
3. **Fall of Western Rome, 376–476 CE.** Legions withdrawn from frontiers as tax base collapsed; state fiscally unable to fund the army needed to hold borders.
4. **US banking collapse, 1930–33.** 9,000+ bank failures, no deposit insurance, cascading runs until the 1933 bank holiday.
5. **Post-Soviet Russia, 1992–98.** Price liberalization caused ~2,500% inflation (1992) destroying savings; voucher/loans-for-shares privatization concentrated assets; organized crime filled the enforcement vacuum.
6. **Yugoslav hyperinflation + Bosnian War, 1992–95.** Serbia hit ~313,000,000% monthly inflation (Jan 1994); Srebrenica massacre (July 1995, ~8,000 killed) in a UN "safe area."
7. **Zimbabwe hyperinflation, 2007–09.** Land reform disrupted export earnings; peak monthly inflation ~79.6 billion% (Nov 2008); currency abandoned 2009.
8. **Libya, post-2011.** No unified army/police after Gaddafi's fall; power devolved to militias; open slave markets documented by 2017 (CNN).
9. **Syria, 2011–present.** Security forces fractured along sectarian lines; 500,000+ deaths, 12M displaced.
10. **Partition of India, 1947.** Boundary announced after independence with no transition security force; police/army split communally; 200,000–2M deaths, ~14M displaced in weeks.

## Tier 3 — Belonging (family/kinship/community)

1. **Trans-Atlantic slave trade, ~1500–1866.** ~12.5M transported; enslavers systematically split family units at capture, embarkation, sale; US slave codes (from 1662) denied enslaved marriages legal standing.
2. **US/Canada residential schools, 1831/1879–1996.** Hundreds of thousands of Indigenous children removed from families; languages banned; TRC (2015) documents deliberate policy; mortality at some schools exceeded 40%.
3. **Australian Stolen Generations, ~1905–1970s.** ~1 in 3 Aboriginal children forcibly removed under state protection-board laws (Bringing Them Home report, 1997).
4. **British Industrial Revolution, ~1780–1850.** Enclosure + factory labor drew families from village kin networks into cities; Engels (1845), parliamentary Blue Books document multi-generational household breakdown.
5. **WWI, 1914–18.** France: ~600,000 war widows, ~1.1M orphans; mass conscription death (10M military deaths) removed a generation of fathers.
6. **Holodomor/collectivization, 1929–33, Ukraine.** 3.5–5M famine deaths; 1.8M+ kulak households deported; peasant household economies dissolved.
7. **China's one-child policy, 1980–2015.** Forced sterilizations/abortions documented; restructured kinship terms and elder-care obligations (4-2-1 structure per census data).
8. **Rwandan genocide, 1994.** 800,000–1M killed in ~100 days; gacaca/ICTR records show neighbor-organized killing destroying intermarried village trust networks; 400,000+ orphans/widow-headed households after.
9. **Post-Soviet collapse, 1991–late 1990s.** Loss of state-provided housing/childcare/pensions removed extended-family supports; marriage rates fell, divorce and child-institutionalization rose through the decade (Rosstat).

## Tier 4 — Esteem/Status (rank, hierarchy, mobility)

1. **French Revolution, 1789–94.** Aug 4, 1789: feudal privileges/noble exemptions abolished in one session; 1790: hereditary titles abolished; ~2,600 guillotined in Paris during the Terror, disproportionately nobility/clergy.
2. **Haitian Revolution, 1791–1804.** ~500,000 enslaved overturned the colonial racial caste system (Code Noir, 1685); planter class killed/expelled by 1804.
3. **US Reconstruction and reversal, 1865–1900s.** 13th–15th Amendments granted citizenship/suffrage; reversed via 1877 troop withdrawal, Black Codes, Jim Crow (Plessy v. Ferguson, 1896), sharecropping debt peonage.
4. **Russian Revolution, 1917–30s.** Oct 1917 abolished nobility/Table of Ranks overnight; 1929–33 dekulakization deported 1.8M "kulaks," tied to Holodomor famine.
5. **Weimar hyperinflation, 1919–23.** Fixed-income Bürgertum (pensioners, civil servants) lost rank-based security while mobile-asset holders were insulated — later linked to this class's Nazi Party membership skew.
6. **Chinese Cultural Revolution, 1966–76.** "Five Black Categories" reclassification; struggle sessions, job/education loss, rural exile; est. 500,000–2M deaths in the broader upheaval.
7. **Decolonization of Algeria, 1954–62.** Pied-noir settlers (~1M) held disproportionate status under French colonial code; 900,000+ emigrated within months of the 1962 Évian Accords.
8. **Partition of India, 1947.** Zamindari/princely-state ranks collapsed with Partition; 10–20M displaced; 565 princely states abolished as sovereign units by 1949–56.
9. **Fall of Berlin Wall/Soviet collapse, 1989–91.** East German Nomenklatura/factory-management ranks lost standing after reunification; regional unemployment reached ~35% in the 1990s (Treuhandanstalt records).

## Tier 5 — Meaning/Identity/Legacy

1. **Industrializing France, 1870s–1897.** Durkheim's *Suicide* (1897): rising rates tied to urbanization and weakened religious/occupational bodies — "anomic suicide."
2. **Meiji Japan, 1868–77.** Samurai class abolished (1876 sword ban); ~1.9M lost a 700-year identity structure; Satsuma Rebellion (1877) killed 12,000–30,000; survivors formed ultranationalist societies (Gen'yōsha, 1881).
3. **Post-WWI Europe, 1918–29.** "Lost Generation" literature (Remarque, Hemingway); Germany's NSDAP grew from ~27,000 (1925) to ~850,000 (1933), recruitment concentrated among status-displaced veterans.
4. **Post-Soviet Russia, 1991–94.** Male life expectancy fell 63.8→57.6 years; suicide rate 26→42/100,000 (Rosstat); Walberg et al. (BMJ 2001) tie this to collapse of state-guaranteed ideological identity, alongside alcohol deaths.
5. **Jonestown, 1978.** 918 deaths; membership drawn from urban Black communities experiencing civil-rights-era community/church breakdown; Peoples Temple offered substitute identity that later enforced mass suicide.
6. **Aum Shinrikyo, Japan, 1980s–95.** ~10,000 members amid post-bubble social dislocation; Tokyo subway sarin attack (1995), 13 deaths, ~6,000 injured.
7. **Algerian harkis, 1962 onward.** ~200,000 Algerians who served France left with no accepted identity in either state; reprisal killings 1962, decades of marginalization in France.
8. **US Rust Belt "deaths of despair," 1999–2017.** Case & Deaton: rising mortality (suicide, opioids, alcoholic liver disease) among non-college whites 45–54, tied to deindustrialization and loss of work-based identity.
9. **Western Europe secularization, 1960s–2000s.** UK weekly church attendance ~30%→under 10%; parallel decline in associational membership (Putnam); direct causal link to suicide contested in the literature.
