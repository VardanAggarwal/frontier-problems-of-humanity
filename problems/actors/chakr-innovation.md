# Chakr Innovation

**What they do.** Delhi/Gurugram cleantech firm founded 2016 by three IIT-Delhi graduates. Flagship product **Chakr Shield** is a retrofit emission control device (RECD) that bolts onto an existing diesel generator, captures ~80–90% of the particulate exhaust and converts the collected soot into a pigment for ink and paint — the **first RECD to receive CPCB type-approval** in India. The market it sells into was **constituted by a mandate**: CPCB IV+ (July 2023) requires certified RECDs on in-use diesel gensets up to 800 kW. That mandate reaches *permit-holding, inspectable* genset owners — commercial buildings, hospitals, telecom, formal industry — which is Chakr's customer base, and **not** the ~1,000 informal Loni units or ~27,000 Ghaziabad units running cheap uncertified sets, which are the ones producing the small-town PM2.5 load. A clean second instance (after CEMS) of the tier's "when a mandate constitutes a market" pattern — and of that market being keyed to the formal segment only.

## Scope
- `small-industrial-town-air` — the enterprise leg is **not absent**, as the leaf first read it: a mandate (CPCB IV+ / RECD) manufactured a genset-emission-control market and Chakr is its leader. But the market serves formal gensets; the informal industrial cluster and the exposed resident remain outside it. Unit/segment mismatch, not a void.

## Status
- **Funding** — ~$43.6M total across 12 rounds; Series C **$23M, Sep 2025**, led by Iron Pillar (manufacturing expansion + deeptech R&D, incl. non-lithium energy storage).
- **Scale metric** — **5,000+ RECD installations, 2,000+ customers** (company figures, 2025); FY25 revenue ₹189 Cr (up 51.5% YoY from ₹124.8 Cr FY24).

## What they need
A demand path into the sub-threshold / informal genset base — either enforcement reaching it or a subsidised retrofit line — is the missing piece; today that segment has no procurement trigger.

## What they can offer
Proven RECD hardware and installation network; a soot-to-pigment downstream that partly offsets device cost.
