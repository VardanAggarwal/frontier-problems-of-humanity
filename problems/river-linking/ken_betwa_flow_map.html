Ken–Betwa Link Project

Canal systems and command areas — schematic, grouped by supply line

Not a map — a schematic, but oriented to rough real compass direction (N up, E right): Daudhan Dam sits as the central hub; Jhansi/Mahoba/Tikamgarh/Lalitpur bow west and north of it; Banda/Bariarpur sit northeast; Damoh sits south; Lower Orr Dam/Datia sit far northwest; Kotha Barrage/Bina Complex sit southwest. Each canal/lift system is one straight/bowed line in its own color, with the command areas it feeds shown as blobs in that same color, sized by CCA (√ha scale). For exact lat/lon, see command_area_geoboundaries.md.

Irrigation water required, by demand zone (MCM/yr)

Required = KBLP's own irrigation duty (5,079 m³/ha/yr, from kblp_salient_features.pdf §2.3.4's Command Area & Water Utilization table: 4,614.56 MCM ÷ 9,08,428 ha) × each command's CCA. No command-level flow-metering data exists, so demand is grouped to the finest resolution the source actually supports — WHERE water is drawn relative to Daudhan, not per structure: upstream at Daudhan (reservoir pump/lift, never leaves the Ken), en-route on the K-B Link canal (drawn off before it reaches Betwa), downstream Ken (Ken LBC + Bariarpur RBC, further along the Ken's own course), and the Betwa-side standalone group (not fed by the transfer at all).

Downstream Ken (Ken LBC + Bariarpur R.B.C.) alone requires 1,865 MCM/yr. The K-B Link diverts 1,377 MCM/yr away from the Ken side to the Betwa instead (see water-split panel below) — 74% of what downstream Ken needs on its own. Kept in-basin rather than transferred west, that volume would go a long way toward meeting downstream Ken's own requirement first.

Cost Efficiency by System (₹ per ha)

Cost per ha = system cost ÷ total CCA that system's commands cover (computed live from the numbers above, not separately typed in). Costs are from kblp_salient_features.pdf's "Abstract Of Cost" table (₹ crore, 2020-21 price level) — this DPR component sub-total is ₹37,778.75 cr, part of the full ₹44,605 cr Cabinet-approved project cost, not equal to it. Notable: the K-B Link main canal — the project's namesake transfer to Betwa — is the most expensive per hectare of the group, pricier than the Ken LBC or reservoir pump/lift schemes that just move Ken's own water around within its own basin.

Water split at Daudhan (MCM/yr)

Ken's own basin use is ~3x the entire K-B transfer, and only ~4% of the transfer (≈0.8% of Ken's total yield) ever actually reaches the Betwa river.
