# The toxic-exposure class

## The object

**Not a policy object** — a **class**: one mechanism (long-latency toxic exposure), three carriers (asbestos, lead, silica), recurring across tier-1 (lead through food, silica and asbestos through air, asbestos also reaching water and shelter). Kept in the register because the payoff is a node's payoff: one class of intervention (exposure registries, phase-out schedules, occupational monitoring) addresses all three carriers at once.

**Shared structure.** Latency between exposure and measurable harm; no exposure registry in India; controlled or banned in most other jurisdictions; installed into poor households and worksites as a development/construction product — asbestos roofing, leaded paint and pipes, unmonitored silica dust in mining and stone-cutting.

**Where lead diverges — the reason `authority-mismatched-to-harm` was added to this node (2026-09-07).** For asbestos and silica the class fails through mechanism 7: a compensation layer built without a detection layer. Lead is the carrier where the state has built *neither* — no blood-lead surveillance programme *and* no compensation scheme for lead-poisoned children. All three carriers also show the same jurisdictional split: regulation divided across factory, mine, environment and consumer-product law with no body holding the exposed person as its reporting unit (FSSAI counts the food sample, CPCB the facility, DGMS the registered mine). See leaves `residual-childhood-lead`, `silicosis-stone-industry`, `asbestos-import-legal`, `ambient-asbestos-demolition-dust`.

## The one intervention

A statutory exposure registry keyed to the person, not the site or the product — the detection layer that compensation schemes were built without. Plus dated phase-out schedules per carrier, aligned to the jurisdictions that have already banned them.
