
# Construction

## The object

The construction sector as a provision system — housing and infrastructure output on one side, and on the other a standing source of dust, effluent, land conversion, noise, and precarious migrant labour. No single regulator holds it: it spans state PWDs, pollution control boards, labour departments and municipal building-plan authorities, none of which owns the full failure chain.

## Cross-need chain
- *Air:* construction and demolition dust, a large share of urban PM in Indian cities — and the fraction NCAP abatement money is preferentially spent on (68% of ~₹9,929 cr to road-dust management), because the C&D-dust sub-lever is where clean-air funds enter the municipal chain. See leaf `north-india-winter-smog` §B/§C (the NCAP spend-misallocation analysis was folded there and into `small-industrial-town-air` on 2026-09-08).
- *Water:* sand mining from riverbeds; unpermitted groundwater draw at sites.
- *Shelter:* build quality and the informal-housing stock the sector produces at the low end.
- *Sleep:* site noise; the labour schedules imposed on on-site migrant workers.
- *Food:* conversion of agricultural land to built use.

## The one intervention

No clean single lever — this is a sector node, so the work is mapping where the sub-levers sit (dust rules, C&D waste rules, sand-mining leases, BOCW cess and welfare boards, building bye-laws) and which actors already work each. Candidate consolidation point: the state-level construction-worker welfare boards, which already levy a cess across the sector.
