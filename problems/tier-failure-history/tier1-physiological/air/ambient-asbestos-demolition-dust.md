# Ambient asbestos from weathering, renovation and demolition of the in-place roofing stock

This is distinct from [`asbestos-import-legal`](/leaf/asbestos-import-legal), not a duplicate. That leaf: the source is *fresh* chrysotile imports fabricated into new asbestos-cement (AC) sheeting; the harmed party is AC-industry workers and the families who buy the roof. This leaf: the source is the *already-installed* stock re-entering the air as it weathers, is renovated and is demolished; the harmed party is the occupant and the bystander, who never bought anything and never worked with the material. Same node, same carrier, different exposure route — and, decisively, a different evidence base, because the second one does not exist.

## A · Classification

- **Channel — ambient-accidental.** A hazard with a source but no intent and no system design. The tension is worth naming: the *product* is designed (BIS IS 11769 governs installation and handling) but the *release path* is not — the Construction and Demolition Waste Management Rules 2016 contain no asbestos stream, no pre-demolition survey requirement and no wet-removal protocol, so nothing in the system is aimed at this pathway at all. Where a control exists and the arrangement of work defeats it, the value is `structural` (as on [`silicosis-stone-industry`](/leaf/silicosis-stone-industry)); here no control was ever keyed to the pathway.
- **Satisfier relation — violator.** AC sheeting is the cheapest durable roof in India and it does satisfy the shelter need; the provision damages the air need over the life of the roof and, hardest, at its end. This is the within-tier loop in its cleanest form — shelter provision degrading air.
- **Onset — latent.** Mesothelioma latency is 20–40 years and India reports essentially no mesothelioma to the WHO, so no part of this exposure is visible in mortality data. Harm incurred, not yet realised.
- **Agent — industrial-pollution**, deliberately diverging from the sibling leaf's `industrial-exposure`. There is no worksite and no employment relation: an industrial material degrades into shared air. The exposed party's relation to the source is residence, not work.

## B · Evidence

- **The stock.** India has incorporated on the order of **7 million tonnes of asbestos into its building stock since 1960**, almost all of it as AC corrugated roofing, with an advocacy estimate of **more than 100 million people — disproportionately Dalit — living under asbestos roofs**. That stock is now aging into its renovation-and-demolition window.
- **The release mechanism is conceded on the record.** In the NGT proceeding the **MoEF&CC admitted** that asbestos fibres "can enter the air, water and soil from the weathering, renovation or demolition of manufactured asbestos products and people are likely to be exposed … through inhalation of asbestos airborne fibres." The tribunal used that admission to reject the manufacturers' association's contrary claim that fibres stay locked in the cement matrix (judgment ¶121). This route is no longer speculative; a central ministry has conceded it in a judicial proceeding.
- **Measurement state — no Indian ambient measurement exists, and in 2025 that absence decided a case.** Asked to assess *non-occupational* asbestos hazard, the expert committee constituted by CPCB on 7 November 2024 — 12 experts, two meetings, **no study of its own** — had three measurement sources available. Two were Indian and both occupational: ICMR-NIOH's 2012 study (427 observations in or near asbestos industry; 18–19% above the 0.1 f/cc work-environment limit) and DGFASLI's 2019 study of 50 AC plants (35 within 0.1 f/ml, 15 in the range 0.2–0.4 f/ml). The third, and the only household-ambient one, was **Sri Lankan** — the National Building Research Organisation's 2021 air-quality study, reporting household concentrations of 0.00016–0.00034 f/cc average and 0.00071–0.00077 f/cc maximum. There is no Indian equivalent to substitute for it.
- **The threshold was keyed to the wrong population.** The committee benchmarked non-occupational exposure against **0.1 fibres/cc, the industrial occupational limit**, while the Sri Lankan study it relied on states a **non-occupational standard of 0.0009 fibres/cc** — a factor of roughly 111 — and itself recommends including asbestos in air-quality standards and monitoring programmes. The applicant also placed on record that the Indian soil standard is 10,000 mg/kg where several countries use 100 mg/kg, and that the ICMR-NICPR representative did not sign the committee's additional report of 21 February 2025.
- **The consequence.** The tribunal held that "in the absence of any positive specific scientific evidence/material" an immediate discontinuance direction was not warranted, declining to apply the precautionary principle and citing *Kalyaneshwari v. Union of India* (2011) 3 SCC 287. The missing denominator produced the ruling. This is the strongest instance in tier 1 of absence of measurement being load-bearing rather than merely a gap in the research.
- **The one Indian TEM measurement is near-source, not urban.** Singh, Fitzgerald, Dada and Frank (IJERPH, February 2025; medRxiv preprint October 2024) analysed dust from a marble waste slurry dump-yard at Rajsamand, Rajasthan by **transmission electron microscopy** and found abundant **tremolite** asbestos — an equivalent quantity of the tested Kelwa marble dust containing on the order of **37,000 countable fibrous structures** of a regulated asbestos mineral. Contractors dispose of slurry on open land; it spreads in the rains and becomes airborne when dry. The site is open to the public and has become a tourist destination. This confirms the analytical route and a second, previously unmapped Indian source (asbestos as a contaminant of *non*-asbestos mining), but it is dump-yard dust — the urban ambient measurement still does not exist.
- **Feasible and routine elsewhere.** US EPA ran more than 10,000 TEM samples after 9/11; Denmark monitors its four largest cities. India has no ambient programme for a hazard sitting on ~100 million roofs.
- **Differential vulnerability.** (a) Hit hardest: households under aging AC roofs — the population is poor and disproportionately Dalit, the roof is the cheapest durable option, and its most dangerous phase is its last one, which arrives without an owner having done anything; plus informal demolition and C&D-waste handlers, who work the debris stream with no separation, no wetting and no PPE. (b) Why they cannot exit or defend: **no information** (the exposure is invisible and the disease is 20–40 years out), **no resources** (replacing the roof costs more than keeping it), and **no standing** (there is no measurement, so there is no claim to make and nothing to be a claimant *of*). That profile routes representation to a body acting *for* the exposed — and, uniquely on this leaf, the first thing such a body must do is commission a measurement, not file a claim.
- Data note — the ~7 Mt stock figure is a 2010 *Lancet* vintage and the ">100 million under asbestos roofs" figure is an advocacy estimate, not a survey. `scale: 8` is set from the second of these and is a **stock-exposed** count, not a count of anyone measured, diagnosed or shown to be above any threshold — the whole point of this leaf is that no such count exists. Do not read it as a harm magnitude.
- Data note — the only public copy of the NGT judgment found is hosted on the website of **FCPMA, respondent no. 4**, the manufacturers' association that lost on the fibre-release point. The text is the tribunal's; the hosting is the industry's.
- Data note — non-asbestos sheet pricing below (₹18–23/sq ft) is from trade listings and a market price list, not a procurement dataset. Treat as an order of magnitude for the substitution cost, not a verified parity figure.

## C · Diagnosis

- **Mechanism — `instrument-keyed-to-wrong-object` (primary).** Every asbestos instrument India owns is keyed to the worker: the 0.1 f/cc permissible exposure limit is an occupational limit, the two Indian studies are occupational, the notification regimes sit under the Factories and Mines Acts, and NIOH and DGFASLI are occupational-health bodies. The exposed resident is adjacent to all of it and covered by none, so when a tribunal asked the non-occupational question, the only available instrument answered a different one. The remedy attaches next to the harm and excludes a class by design.
- **Mechanism — `authority-mismatched-to-harm`.** Asbestos is not among India's 12 NAAQS parameters, so CPCB's ambient-air mandate does not contain the pollutant; the C&D Waste Management Rules 2016 give the urban local body a demolition-waste duty with no asbestos stream inside it; hazardous-waste rules cover disposal but not re-entrainment. No body's reporting unit is the exposed resident because no instrument makes that person a unit.
- **Mechanism — `solution-at-hand-blocked`.** A TEM ambient survey is settled technique, routine in the US and Denmark, and India has at least four NABL-accredited laboratories that already run asbestos air monitoring. Nothing technical is missing. What is missing is a buyer, and the mandate that would create one was ordered in October 2025 and has not arrived.
- **Mechanism — `within-tier-loop` (secondary).** Shelter provision degrades air within the same tier: the cheapest way to satisfy the shelter need is the thing that fails the air need twenty years later. The loop is why the harmed population cannot simply be told to stop — the alternative sheet is the same object at a higher price.
- **Primary vs derivative burden.** The visible claim-fight is about *schools*, because a school is a legible unit with a named authority and a sympathetic occupant. The tribunal itself noticed the mismatch, observing that the prayer for schools "ignores/overlooks the adverse impact of asbestos cement roofing sheets in residential houses or other buildings likely to be frequented by the children/students, where their stay may also be for equal or longer periods." The load-bearing exposure is residential and the litigated unit is institutional.
- **Nodes.** Downstream of [`toxic-exposure-class`](/node/toxic-exposure-class) — the long-latency toxic exposure with no person-keyed registry, banned elsewhere, reaching the poor as a building input; and of [`construction`](/node/construction) — the sector node, where the specific sub-lever is the C&D Waste Management Rules' missing asbestos stream and pre-demolition survey.
- **Blocker.** Two, in sequence. First, no ambient measurement exists, so the precautionary principle was declined for want of "positive specific scientific evidence" — an evidence standard applied to a body of evidence nobody is obliged to generate. Second, the directions that would break the loop were issued and the deadline passed: MoEF&CC had six months from 30 October 2025 to review the evidence and frame policy, and MoEF&CC, MoHUA, MoE and CPCB were to file Action Taken Reports within one month after that. **The ATR was due around 31 May 2026; as of this leaf's date, 9 September 2026, no compliance is on the public record.** Paragraph 126 makes the tribunal's Registrar General responsible for relisting the matter if the reports do not come.

## D · Who is working on this

**Activism — present, and one individual is carrying both halves of it.**
- **Dr. Raja Singh** — visiting faculty at the School of Planning and Architecture, New Delhi, and Department of Environmental and Occupational Health, Dornsife School of Public Health, Drexel University. He is simultaneously the **applicant** in *Dr. Raja Singh v. Union of India*, O.A. 298/2023, argued **as party-in-person** against three ministries, CPCB and the manufacturers' association's three counsel; and the **first author** of the only Indian TEM asbestos-dust measurement of the last decade (IJERPH 2025). The same person is both litigating the absence of evidence and personally generating the evidence. That combination does not appear anywhere else in this tier, and it is the reason this leaf is no longer a stub.
- **Sean Fitzgerald** (Scientific Analytical Institute, US) — the TEM analytical capability behind the Rajasthan finding. **Arthur L. Frank** (Drexel) — asbestos epidemiologist with a long India engagement. **Rima Dada** (AIIMS New Delhi) — the Indian institutional co-author, and the plausible in-country host for a survey. All three are evidence-base authors: `registry` under the ground test, not monitored.
- **BANI**, **India Asbestos Victims Association**, **Toxics Watch Alliance** and **Gopal Krishna** — named in the judgment record and the reference archive on the case; twenty-five years of campaign, all of it aimed at the import trade and the ban rather than at the installed stock. **OEHNI** convenes the occupational-health network. Their unit is the central advocacy organisation, not the exposed household.
- **Nobody is affected-led here, and the reason is structural.** An affected-led body needs an affected population that knows it is affected. With no measurement, a 20–40 year latency and no diagnosis, the exposed cannot identify themselves — so the leg that would normally hold the ground has no constituency to organise. Compare `asbestos-import-legal`, where IAVA exists because asbestos *victims* exist and are named.

**Institution — directed, dated, and overdue.** The NGT judgment of 30 October 2025 is the first Indian instrument that names this exposure route. Its operative directions on this leaf:
- **XVI — CPCB** to prepare, finalise and issue an SOP for proper disposal of cement-asbestos waste at authorised sites under the Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules 2016 "and **prevent re-entrainment into air matrix on degradation**." That clause is this leaf's remedy, ordered by name.
- **XIV — CPCB and the State Pollution Control Boards / Pollution Control Committees** to evolve a special mechanism for monitoring asbestos-waste disposal, the tribunal accepting that even proper disposal leaves gradual contamination requiring ongoing monitoring of affected areas.
- **XVII — MoEF&CC** to (i) review the entire scientific evidence and global best practice within six months and frame policy on use of AC roofing in schools, **residential houses and other buildings** with an action plan and timelines, per its own *Vision Statement on Environment and Human Health* ¶4.3.1 ("alternatives to asbestos may be used to the extent possible and the use of asbestos may be phased out"); and (ii) issue guidelines on manufacturing, installation, maintenance, **dismantling** and disposal, including AC water-supply pipelines.
- **XV — Ministry of Education** to advise schools to maintain existing sheets in good condition, coat them, follow IS 11769 Part 1 in handling, and dispose at authorised sites. **MoHUA** is respondent no. 2 and an ATR filer.
- ¶124 requires circulation to all Chief Secretaries and SPCB/PCC Member Secretaries for strict compliance; ¶125 sets the ATR; ¶126 puts the Registrar General on relisting duty. The authority does contain the source here — CPCB can write the SOP — which distinguishes this from the usual tier-1 pattern. The failure is not jurisdictional, it is non-delivery against a live order.
- **ICMR-NIOH** (2012) and **DGFASLI** (2019) hold the only Indian fieldwork capability; both have measured only occupationally. **ICMR-NICPR**'s representative declining to sign the expert committee's additional report is the single documented institutional dissent on the record.

**Enterprise (a) — serving the harm as framed: present, wrong segment, and now with more datapoints than the stub had.** The abatement and testing layer exists in India and is NABL-accredited: **EPSCO India** (Mumbai; founder Suhel Parker, US-certified inspector/planner/contractor) for survey, removal and disposal, and **Qualitek Labs**, **ITC Labs**, **ALS India** and **Elca Labs** for ACM surveys and airborne fibre analysis by PLM/PCM. Every one of them sells into hospitals, airports, pharma and food plants, data centres, power stations and infrastructure projects — customers who procure compliance voluntarily or under a project condition. **None sells ambient monitoring, because nobody is required to buy it.** This changes the stub's verdict in an important way: the constraint is not that India lacks the instrument or the laboratory. India has both, idle. The constraint is the absent procurement trigger — which is exactly direction XVI, ordered and undelivered. The residential and informal-demolition segment, holding the entire in-place mass, has no actor and no payer.
- **Over-served check.** Corporate-facility abatement is served by at least five commercial actors; the ~100 million people under AC roofs are served by none. The served population is not the harmed one — the tier-1 over-served pattern, in a market small enough to count on one hand.

**Enterprise (b) — attacking the binding constraint: the substitute exists, and the incumbents make it.** The binding constraint is price parity against AC sheet, not availability. **Everest Industries** sells Hi-Tech non-asbestos fibre-cement profile sheets reinforced with high-impact PVA/polypropylene fibre at roughly ₹18–23 per sq ft; **BirlaNu** (formerly HIL) and **Visaka** run comparable non-asbestos lines; **ARL Infratech** distributes. **2K Technologies** sells the plant machinery for *both* asbestos and non-asbestos corrugated sheet — the same line, a different input, which is the cleanest available evidence that the substitution is an input decision rather than a capital one. **SHERA India** is already in the registry as a fibre-cement alternative maker. Kerala's 2019 government order specifies the substitute by name (HIPP-reinforced 6 mm corrugated sheet), so a state has already written a procurement spec that works.
- The graveyard here is not of failed ventures; it is of demand. No venture needs inventing. What is missing is the mandate that would move a buyer from the ₹X asbestos sheet to the ₹X+δ non-asbestos one — the leg-2 → leg-3 handoff, where a mandate constitutes the market.

**Opposed — present and well-resourced.** The **Fibre Cement Products Manufacturers Association** (FCPMA, formerly ACPMA) was respondent no. 4, appeared through three counsel against a party-in-person, argued that fibres stay locked in the cement matrix (rejected), and after the judgment placed a press release carried by ANI and republished by *Business Standard* and *The Wire* under the headline "NGT Upholds Safety of Asbestos-Cement Roofing: No Evidence of Public Health Risk." BANI published the same judgment as a reiteration of the Supreme Court's directions paving the way for alternatives. **Both readings are defensible from a split outcome** — relief refused, seventeen remedial directions issued and a phase-out action plan ordered — and the divergence is itself the finding: the framing that reached the wires was placed by the losing respondent, and the judgment does not say what the headline says.

**Positive control — Kerala, on the instrument only.** The Kerala High Court banned asbestos roofs in schools in 2019 with a two-year replacement window, implemented by G.O. (Ms) No. 162/2019/GEDN of 9 October 2019, which specifies non-asbestos HIPP-reinforced sheet as the substitute for schools and anganwadis; the Kerala State Human Rights Commission had recommended a phase-out as early as 31 January 2009. The NGT expressly carved Kerala out of its holding that no direction was warranted. No measured before/after exists — which is the same problem as everywhere else on this leaf. It is a positive control on the rule, not on the exposure.

**Representation present?** Not at the unit of the harm. Activism is present at the central-organisation unit and carried substantially by one unpaid individual; institution is present, correctly scoped, and in default of a dated order; enterprise is present at the corporate-facility segment and absent at the residential one. No actor of any leg is organised at the unit of the person living under, or standing downwind of, a degrading asbestos roof — and no such actor can form until someone measures.

## E · Gap

The gap is **representation**, and the stub's provisional verdict was right about the value and wrong about the reason. The stub read the enterprise leg as structurally empty because the harmed cannot pay. It is not empty: five commercial actors run abatement and NABL-accredited fibre analysis in India today, and the substitute product is made at scale by the incumbents. They serve airports, data centres and pharma plants. The leg is present at the wrong unit, which is a representation gap and not a coverage one — `gap_missing_leg: [enterprise]`.

What has changed against the stub is that the missing thing is now precisely nameable and dated. It is not a study in the abstract; it is direction XVI of a judgment of 30 October 2025 — a CPCB standard operating procedure to prevent re-entrainment on degradation — plus the MoEF&CC policy under XVII(i), both due, both unfiled, with the Registrar General empowered to relist. Constructing a claimant on this leaf still resolves to *commissioning a measurement* rather than founding an organisation, but the commissioning body is now identified, under order, and in default.
