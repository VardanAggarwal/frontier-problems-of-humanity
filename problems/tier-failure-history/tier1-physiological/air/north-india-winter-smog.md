# North Indian winter smog

## A · Classification

- **Channel — structural.** The harm follows from how the airshed — the body of air a region shares, here the whole Indo-Gangetic Plain — has its systems arranged — vehicle fleet, freight routing, industrial clusters and brick kilns, thermal plants, crop-residue disposal, urban form, winter meteorology — not from one actor's act (`direct`) and not from a hazard with no system design (`ambient-accidental`). No single emitter is decisive; the arrangement is.
- **Satisfier relation — degraded-quality.** Breathable air is present but carries PM2.5 at ~20× the WHO annual guideline of 5 µg/m³ across Delhi and much of the IGP. The emergency playbook itself behaves as a pseudo-satisfier — felt relief that discharges pressure and defers the real fix — but that describes the remedy, analysed in §C, not the satisfier.
- **Onset — chronic.** Year-round elevated exposure with acute seasonal peaks; the mortality is already in the modelled burden (1.67M attributable deaths, India, 2019). Not `latent`.
- **Agent — industrial-pollution.** Ambient combustion-derived particulate across transport, industry and biomass, no discrete accident. `daily-life` was considered — cooking and driving are everyday acts — and rejected: the load-bearing fraction is fleet, freight and industrial combustion.

## B · Evidence

- **Magnitude, with denominators.** Delhi's annual mean PM2.5 has sat in a ~92–100 µg/m³ band across 2021–2025 and was **99.6 µg/m³ for 2025** in IQAir's *cities* ranking — ~20× the WHO annual guideline. Delhi has been the world's most polluted **capital in 7 of the 8** IQAir reports covering 2018–2025 (N'Djamena, Chad took the capital slot for calendar 2022 and 2024); it is consistently the most polluted among cities above 1M population.
- **The airshed, not the city, is the unit.** The IGP–Himalayan-Foothills airshed is continuous across Bangladesh, India, Nepal and Pakistan (World Bank, *Striving for Clean Air*, 2023); in densely populated IGP locations PM2.5 exceeds 20× the WHO guideline. At 2023 levels the average Delhi resident stands to lose **~8.2 years of life expectancy** and IGP residents ~7.6 years relative to the WHO guideline (AQLI 2025).
- **Mortality.** **1.67 million deaths in India were attributable to air pollution in 2019 — 17.8% of all deaths — economic losses $36.8 billion, 1.36% of GDP** (GBD 2019, *Lancet Planetary Health*, 2020). Modelled from exposure surfaces, not counted from death registration, which does not record air pollution as a cause. No later national assessment revises it materially.
- **Source apportionment (DSS — the Decision Support System, IITM/MoES; seasonal averages, swing year to year).** Vehicles are **~51–53% of Delhi's *local* PM2.5** in the Nov 10–20 window across 2023–2025 — the single largest local source. Local sources were **~35% of Delhi's PM2.5 in 2025** (up from ~27% in 2024); regional inflow **~26% in 2025** (down from ~36% in 2023). **Biomass/stubble burning ≈ 7% of the post-monsoon load and ≈ 0% in deep winter.** The seasonal spike is real; it is not the load-bearing fraction. This is Delhi's local split. The national, mortality-weighted split across residential biomass, coal power and industry, dust and agricultural burning is a different accounting, set out on the [air](/need/air) need page.
- **The structural money is aimed at the wrong fraction.** Under the National Clean Air Programme (NCAP), per CREA's *Tracing the Hazy Air 2026*, of **₹13,415 crore released** under NCAP and the 15th Finance Commission air-quality grants since 2019, **₹9,929 crore has been spent (74%)** — and **~68% of it went to road-dust management** (mechanical sweeping, sprinkling, anti-smog guns: a coarse-PM10 action), ~14% to transport, and **under 1% each to industry and domestic fuel**, the categories carrying the PM2.5 mortality. Only **90 of 130 NCAP cities have completed a source-apportionment study**, so ~40 cities allocate abatement budgets without an established account of what is polluting them. NCAP's headline target is a **PM10** reduction (revised 2022 to 40% against a 2017 baseline), scored on a **city-wide annual average** — the fraction dust spending can move, not the fraction that kills. Delhi had spent only **17% of its own NCAP allocation (₹14.1 cr of ₹81.36 cr) by December 2025**. CSE's 2024 reform assessment finds the same independently, calling interventions "largely confined to road dust control". The 15th FC's Million-Plus Cities air-quality grant (~₹12,000–13,600 cr across ~42 urban agglomerations, FY2020-21 to 2025-26) runs to the same municipal unit on the same "show a PM10 improvement" logic — so the misallocation is architectural, not an NCAP quirk.
- **The spend has not produced the result it is measured against.** Of 130 NCAP cities, **51 met the original 20–30% PM10 reduction target, 23 met the revised 40% target, 77 showed some PM10 reduction, and 23 recorded PM10 *increases*** over the programme period (CREA, *Tracing the Hazy Air 2026*; the two counts of 23 are coincidental, not the same cities). In 2025, **190 of 229 cities** with adequate PM10 data still exceeded India's own NAAQS, and **103 of 231** exceeded it on PM2.5. Utilisation is low and uneven — **82 NCAP-line cities spent under 66% of released funds** over five years (Newslaundry, Jan 2025: ₹1,092 cr of ₹1,615 cr released to those cities). CREA's 2025 report found only ~31% of 131 cities meeting even the PM10 target. Seven years and ₹9,929 cr in, the national PM2.5 signal is still within meteorological noise (above).
- **The 2025 dip.** Delhi's 2025 annual mean fell ~8% year on year — the first meaningful move in the series — but IQAir's lead scientist characterises the national decline as not statistically significant and likely weather-driven, and CSE's winter 2024-25 analysis found stubble fires down while Delhi's *local* pollution rose. Hold both: some real source-specific gains, but the annual signal is within meteorological noise.
- **Differential vulnerability.** (a) *Hit hardest:* outdoor workers (street vendors, traffic police, construction and gig/delivery labour, waste pickers), the very young and very old, residents of informal settlements beside arterial roads and industrial clusters — they breathe the ambient load all day and many also cook on biomass, compounding the tier-1 air burden. (b) *Why they cannot exit or defend:* no resources (cannot buy purifiers, air-conditioned indoor work, or a winter out of the city), no information at an actionable unit (AQI advisories assume you can stay indoors), and no standing (informal workers have no occupational-health regime, and the construction bans under GRAP — the Graded Response Action Plan, the escalating emergency curbs triggered when Delhi-NCR air crosses set thresholds — cut their wages, so the emergency remedy is experienced as a threat). This "no standing / income set against the remedy" profile is why activism on this leaf sits with resident-parent bodies, not the exposed workforce — and it drives the §E gap.
- **Measurement state.** PM2.5 *is* measured — Delhi runs one of the densest monitor networks of any megacity, with real-time source apportionment — but the IGP tail is not: only **~15% of India's population (~200M) lives within 10 km of a continuous monitor**, 28 NCAP cities still have no continuous station, and India is ~465 stations short of its own 1,500 manual-station target (*State of India's Environment 2026*). The episode counts and annual means are solid; the **attributable-mortality denominator is an estimate no agency owns**.

Data note — IQAir's 2025 report gives Delhi both **99.6 µg/m³** ("Delhi", *cities* ranking) and **82.2 µg/m³** ("New Delhi", *capital-cities* ranking); different administrative boundaries, the same disagreement recorded in-repo for the 2022 report. Cite one and say which — do not average.

Data note — India's PM2.5 National Ambient Air Quality Standard (NAAQS) is **40 µg/m³ — 8× the WHO guideline of 5** — so "compliant" IGP air is still hazardous.

Data note — DSS source shares are seasonal averages quoted with the window named, not a fixed split.

Data note — 1.67M is a **2019** figure, unrevised; no trend is asserted for the 2025 dip beyond "first meaningful move, likely meteorological".

Data note — failed hypothesis, "NCAP/CAQM is starting to bite": not supported by the 2025 dip. The mechanism the dip revealed is `primary-vs-derivative-burden` (§C).

## C · Diagnosis

- **Mechanism — `solution-at-hand-blocked` (primary).** Year-round source control is mapped and costed: the remaining transport levers (fleet electrification, freight bypass, public-transport supply), industrial fuel switching and brick-kiln conversion, thermal-plant FGD, diesel-genset displacement and mechanised residue management are all in the DSS inventory and the World Bank's *A Breath of Change* roadmap (target: IGP PM2.5 < 35 µg/m³ by 2035). It is politically stuck because every structural lever has a concentrated, organised loser, whereas GRAP bans and school closures have diffuse, transient ones. The emergency measure is chosen every winter because it is enforceable inside a news cycle and creates no permanent constituency against the enforcer — the tier file's "remedy with high visibility and no identifiable loser" selection pressure operating on enforcement design.
- **Mechanism — `authority-mismatched-to-harm` (primary).** The harm unit is the IGP airshed — ~40% of India's population across seven-plus states, ~26–36% of Delhi's winter PM2.5 blowing in from outside the city, the airshed continuous with Pakistan, Nepal and Bangladesh. CAQM covers only NCR + adjoining districts of four states; the Delhi government covers only Delhi; thermal-plant FGD sits with the power ministry and CPCB; farm fires sit with state agriculture departments CAQM cannot politically compel. **No Indian body's jurisdiction is the airshed, and none has an international mandate.** CAQM's reporting unit is the daily Delhi AQI and the count of GRAP-stage days — an *episode* counter, not an annual-exposure or attributable-mortality unit.
- **Mechanism — spend-mismatched-to-source.** NCAP money flows to municipal corporations, which can buy sweepers but have no authority over the combustion sources that carry the mortality — so ~68% of ₹9,929 cr went to road dust and under 1% each to industry and domestic fuel.
- **Mechanism — `instrument-keyed-to-wrong-object`.** The **PM10 city-average KPI** rewards dust suppression and is near-silent on whether combustion PM2.5 fell, so a city can pass NCAP while its residents breathe air the WHO calls hazardous. The corrective — fund source apportionment first, route abatement money to the apportioned combustion sources, score on PM2.5 — has been published by CREA and CSE in almost these words every year since ~2021 and not adopted (`solution-at-hand-blocked`). This is why "structural source control" has a budget line and still isn't happening.
- **Mechanism — `primary-vs-derivative-burden`.** The visible cause each November — stubble burning and firecrackers — is derivative (~7% of the post-monsoon load, ~0% deep winter per DSS; 2025 had stubble comparable to 2023 but higher local pollution). The load-bearing cause is the **year-round combustion baseline** — vehicles (>50% of local PM2.5), industry, waste burning — amplified by the winter inversion.
- **Blocker.** Each structural lever has an organised loser — the car-owning middle class and transport unions; industrial clusters and brick kilns; thermal-power operators with repeatedly-extended FGD deadlines; Punjab/Haryana farmers and their unions; the construction lobby. No political principal will spend capital against all of them at once for a diffuse public-health gain, so the camera-ready emergency measure wins every winter. CAQM has the legal power to compel (CAQM Act 2021, s.14(1), including jail terms) but not the political backing to use it against states — the SC itself found in October 2024 that CAQM "does not seem to be making any efforts to follow up on implementation of its own directions".

Data note — the NCAP/15th-FC spend material above was folded in from the retired `ncap-spend-misallocation` leaf on 2026-09-08. The downstream failure of combustion-abatement delivery into informal industry is carried by `small-industrial-town-air` §E.

## D · Who is working on this

**Activism** — present and affected-led at the household/parent unit, unit-mismatched to an airshed harm, with an over-represented professional cluster above it.
- **Warrior Moms** — network of 1,500+ mothers across 13 states, co-founded 2020 by five Delhi mothers including **Bhavreen Kandhari**. Volunteer, small philanthropic grants (Clean Air Fund ecosystem). `affected_led: yes` (parents of harmed children).
- **Care for Air** — Delhi non-profit, est. 2015, co-founded by **Jyoti Pande Lavakare** (financial journalist; author, *Breathing Here is Injurious to Your Health*, written after losing her mother to lung cancer she attributes to Delhi air). Awareness + advocacy; donation-funded. `affected_led: yes`.
- **Help Delhi Breathe** — citizen-mobilisation campaign, IKEA Foundation / Clean Air Fund backed (delivery via Purpose / Asar). `affected_led: partial` — the campaign infrastructure is professionalised.
- **Right to Breathe India** — citizen-petition movement; thin institutional form; `affected_led: yes`.
- These four sit at the **household / parent unit**. They won awareness, kept air pollution on the SC/NGT docket and in electoral discourse, and helped install the "right to breathe" framing in judicial language. They did **not** win execution — Delhi's annual mean has not moved in a decade — because their unit cannot deliver airshed source control.
- **Over-represented — this is the finding.** Delhi hosts an unusually dense professional clean-air ecosystem — **CSE, CREA, Climate Trends, CEEW, TERI, EPIC-India/AQLI, Vital Strategies, Clean Air Fund** — plus international press every November. This density is disproportionate to on-ground change and tracks the affluent, English-speaking, capital-resident population (the same people who can buy purifiers), not the outdoor workers who carry the exposure.
- **No actor of this leg for** the outdoor-worker / urban-poor cohort: no affected-led body is organised around ambient air as an *occupational and residential* harm for them. Structural: they are informal and migratory, and GRAP's construction bans cut their income, so the visible remedy is partly experienced as a threat.

**Institution** — the airshed body exists and has real power; it defaults to emergency triggers; the year-round programme is unbuilt; jurisdiction stops short of the airshed.
- **Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM)** — statutory; replaced the SC-appointed EPCA (~1998–2020); created by ordinance 2020, **CAQM Act 2021**; prosecutorial power (s.14(1), including jail terms); jurisdiction over NCR + adjoining areas of Punjab, Haryana, UP and Rajasthan; supersedes state governments on air. Unit: central-org. Not affected-led.
- **MoEFCC** — owns NCAP; notified GRAP in 2017; GRAP revised to forecast-based/pre-emptive, effective 1 Oct 2022, administered by CAQM.
- **CPCB** and the **Delhi Pollution Control Committee** — monitoring and GRAP enforcement on the ground; DPCC told the NGT the city would need ~40,000 smog towers.
- **IITM Pune (Ministry of Earth Sciences)** — runs the Decision Support System (DSS v1.0, *Geosci. Model Dev.* 2024) and the forecasts GRAP triggers off.
- **Supreme Court of India** (M.C. Mehta line; approved GRAP 2016; rebuked CAQM Oct 2024; called odd-even "optics" Nov 2023) and the **National Green Tribunal**.
- **World Bank South Asia** — authored the airshed framing and the *A Breath of Change* roadmap; convening + lending, no executive authority; estimates 4-country coordination brings cost per µg to ~$278M vs $780M–$2.6B acting alone.
- CAQM's authority contains NCR sources and, on paper, adjoining-state sources; it does **not** contain the IGP airshed, thermal-plant FGD, or the political capacity to compel Punjab on farm fires. EPCA + CAQM ≈ 25 years of a central Delhi-NCR air authority and Delhi's annual PM2.5 has stayed ~100 µg/m³. CAQM's own 2025 review found 84–99.6% construction-site inspection shortfalls and 47–81% unresolved GRAP complaints across NCR states. **The emergency layer was built; the year-round source-control layer was not.** No domestic body maps the IGP airshed; the transboundary dimension has only the World Bank's advisory convening.

**Enterprise** — present only where a non-exposed third party pays; absent for the exposed, structurally.
- **Ambient sensing:** Respirer Living Sciences (2,000+ low-cost monitors, ~700 across UP/Bihar), Ambee (air-quality-data-as-a-service), Airveda (~₹4.31 cr FY25 revenue, mostly B2B monitors). Buyers: governments, ESG/corporate, data platforms.
- **Consumer air purifiers:** category ~$507M (2024) → projected ~$1.8B (2033) — Atlanta Healthcare, Nirvana Being, Atovio, Praan (raised in the US after failing to raise in India), plus Dyson/Philips/Xiaomi. Buyer: affluent household / corporate office.
- **CEMS compliance vendors** — payer is the mandated emitter.
- The only functioning payers are governments/platforms buying data, affluent households buying indoor air, and mandated emitters buying compliance hardware. **None is the exposed outdoor worker or slum resident.** Praan could not raise in India for outdoor-scale filtration; the ₹25 cr Connaught Place smog tower cut PM2.5 by ≤17% within 100 m and DPCC told the NGT the city would need ~40,000 of them. Consumer purifiers are the densest sub-market and serve close to the least-exposed population — the silicosis leaf's finding, repeated. Outdoor ambient PM2.5 reduction as a product is structurally absent: the beneficiary is everyone in the airshed and cannot be charged; abatement is a public good delivered by regulation, not sale.

**Representation present?** At two units — a statutory airshed institution (CAQM) and affected-led household activism (Warrior Moms, Care for Air) — but neither at the IGP-airshed unit, and the outdoor-worker cohort carrying the exposure has no affected-led body of its own.

## E · Gap

`gap: representation` — `gap_missing_leg: [activism]`, `gap_as_of: 2026-09-08`.

Two residual mismatches make this a representation gap rather than `none`: no body is constituted at the airshed unit, and the outdoor-worker / urban-poor cohort taking the highest ambient dose has no affected-led body of its own — and is partly set *against* the only remedy on offer, because GRAP's construction bans cut its wages. Constructing that claimant, and something at the airshed unit, is the work; there is no single actor at a unit that can both perceive and act on this harm as it actually falls.

The professional clean-air cluster and CAQM together produce a great deal of measurement and litigation and no movement in the annual mean, which is consistent with the representation reading: the bodies that exist are not at the units where the harm and the political cost meet. Enterprise absence for the exposed is **structural and expected** (outdoor air is a public good; the fix is regulatory) and is not counted as the gap.
