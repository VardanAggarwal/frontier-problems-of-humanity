# Household air pollution from solid-fuel cooking

## A · Classification

- **Channel — structural.** The harm follows from how cooking-fuel provision and household cash economics are arranged: biomass is free at the point of collection, LPG is a recurring cash outlay of ~₹800–900 a cylinder, and the household that cannot commit that monthly reverts to wood and dung. Not `direct` (no single actor causes it), not `ambient-accidental` (the subsidy architecture is a deliberate arrangement that funds acquisition and not use).
- **Satisfier relation — degraded-quality.** Breathable air is present in the kitchen but carries PM2.5 at multiples of any guideline during cooking hours. The LPG *connection* is a stand-in for the thing actually needed: issuing it lets the problem be declared solved while the smoke continues. The need-level classification is nonetheless degraded-quality — the household has air, and what fails is its quality.
- **Onset — chronic.** Household air pollution (HAP) disease (COPD, ischaemic heart disease, child lower-respiratory infection, lung cancer, cataract) accrues over years of daily exposure and is squarely in mortality data — not `latent`.
- **Agent — daily-life.** A domestic inhalation exposure from an everyday household activity, not an industrial or occupational one.

## B · Evidence

- **The connection denominator.** The Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana (PMUY) has released about **103 million LPG connections** since May 2016 — 10.33 crore as of 1 March 2025 (Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas [MoPNG] / PIB, 2025). Overall LPG coverage rose from **62% of households in 2016 to near-universal on paper** — ~96 LPG-or-PNG connections per 100 households by 2024 (Council on Energy, Environment and Water, CEEW). India has ~326M active LPG consumers across ~25,000 distributors.
- **The use denominator, against it.** Survey data put **only ~60–61% of households using LPG/PNG as their *primary* cooking fuel in 2024, with ~33% still relying mainly on firewood** (CEDA/Ashoka, 2024). That ~33% is **on the order of 100 million households**; State of Global Air frames it as India's population exposed to household air pollution falling from **63% (2013) to 42% (2023)** — still ~590–600 million people. Kerosene as a cooking fuel is now marginal (public-distribution-system — PDS — kerosene largely withdrawn), so the residual is overwhelmingly wood and dung.
- **The kerosene rung — never a cooking fuel at scale.** The PDS ration was **~2.8 litres per card per month** (AAY/PHH), against the ~8–10 litres a five-person household needs to cook on kerosene; by NSSO's 68th round only **~0.1% of rural and ~0.5% of urban households** cooked primarily on it, and **~40–45% of the PDS allocation leaked** to the parallel market as a diesel adulterant (IISD/GSI, 2014). It was a lighting fuel pre-electrification.
- **What it did give the poor was divisibility.** Buyable in ₹10–20 increments at the fixed ~₹15/litre PDS price (against ~₹50 cost, a ~70% subsidy), it let a household with irregular daily income buy exactly the energy it could afford that day. The 14.2 kg LPG cylinder removes that — a single **~₹800–900 outlay** (~₹500–600 net for a PMUY beneficiary after the ₹300 subsidy) for ~20 litres-of-kerosene-equivalent of cooking energy. Per useful MJ the two are close once kerosene is bought at open-market price; the difference that binds is **lumpiness, not unit cost**.
- **The rung has now been removed.** The Union Budget 2021-22 zeroed the kerosene subsidy and states including Andhra Pradesh, Delhi, Haryana and Punjab are now "kerosene-free", so the intermediate step between free biomass and the ₹900 lump is gone: a household priced out of a refill steps directly back to wood and dung. Kerosene is not itself a clean fuel — WHO removed it from the clean-fuel list in 2014 — so this rung was less-bad, not a baseline.
- **The refill gap — the core magnitude.** Average annual refills per PMUY beneficiary: **~3.0 (FY2019-20) → ~3.95 (FY2023-24) → ~4.3–4.5 (FY2024-25)** (MoPNG data.gov.in series). Non-PMUY ("general") consumers average **~6.7 a year**; a small household cooking *exclusively* on LPG needs roughly **8–12**. The peer-reviewed statement of the miss: PMUY beneficiaries' refill rate is "less than half that of rural general consumers" (Kar, Pachauri, Bailis & Zerriffi, *Nature Energy*, 2019), and the capital-cost subsidy "promoted rapid adoption of LPG but not regular use" (Stockholm Environment Institute, SEI, 2024). Ratio check: ~4 refills / ~8+ needed ≈ **under 50% of cooking done on the clean fuel** — the "stayed below half" in the title.
- Data note — the "below half" figure is defensible only in this PMUY-beneficiary sustained-use sense. Nationally, 60–61% of *all* households use LPG as their primary fuel, i.e. above half. The two measure different things and must not be reconciled; §C spells out the distinction.
- **The audited early signal.** Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) Report No. 14 of 2019 (audit period May 2016–Dec 2018): of beneficiaries a year or more in, **~5.6 million (17.61%) never returned for a second refill** and **~10.5 million (33.02%) took only one to three refills a year**. The CAG's conclusion: connection count "cannot be the sole criterion" of success.
- **Differential vulnerability.** (a) *Hit hardest:* rural women, who do the cooking and absorb the highest cumulative dose, and children under five near the hearth — lower-respiratory infection in that group is a major share of the attributable deaths. **More than half of the poorest 20% of households still use firewood as their principal fuel** (CEEW/Data For India, 2024). (b) *Why they cannot exit:* **no resources** — the binding constraint is the recurring cash cost of refills, not access or awareness (SEI 2024; Kar et al. 2019); fuel stacking (free collected biomass + occasional LPG) is rational on cash flow; and **no standing** — the beneficiary is a dispersed individual with no organised voice on fuel-subsidy policy. This "no resources / no standing" profile routes representation to a body acting *for* the household, and none dedicated exists.
- **Measurement state.** Connections, refills per beneficiary and oil-marketing-company sales are counted well and published — one of the better-instrumented failures in the need. What is *not* routinely measured is **in-kitchen PM2.5 exposure** or the household-level clean/solid split over a year (fuel stacking is inferred from sales and periodic surveys). The **mortality attribution is contested**: WHO's older figure is "~1 million Indian deaths a year from household air pollution"; GBD-2019-derived estimates put HAP-attributable deaths lower (~0.6–0.8 million) and declining; State of Global Air reports the trend as a fall in exposed share (63%→42%, 2013–2023). Write the range, not a point estimate.


## C · Diagnosis

- **Mechanism — `instrument-keyed-to-wrong-object` (primary).** The subsidy is keyed to the **connection (a one-time capex event)**; the object that determines the health outcome is **sustained refill purchase (recurring opex)**. The instrument attaches adjacent to the harm and, by design, excludes the class it most needed to reach — households that can acquire but not afford to *run* LPG. The ₹300/cylinder targeted subsidy for up to 12 refills (introduced 2023, extended 2024) is a partial correction to the opex side; refill rates rose ~3.0→~4.3 over the same window, but not to the level of exclusive use.
- **Mechanism — `primary-vs-derivative-burden`.** The visible, reported, politically rewarded unit is **connections released**; the load-bearing unit is **cylinders actually burned**. Effort routes to the countable acquisition metric because it moves fast and photographs well.
- **Mechanism — `aggregation-masks-failure`.** "99.8% LPG coverage" / "~96 connections per 100 households" is a national aggregate over *connections*; the harm unit is *a household-year of cooking on clean fuel*, and at that unit ~40% of the country still fails.
- **Energy node membership — how the node produces this failure.** This leaf sits under `energy` (sector node). The node's asserted air chain reads: "the generation mix determines ambient combustion load; the cooking-fuel gap drives indoor smoke." This leaf **is** that cooking-fuel gap. The node's `second-half-never-built` tag applies directly: the first half of the fuel transition (the connection, the bottling plants, the distributor network) was built; the **second half — a durable mechanism to keep the poorest household on the fuel through recurring cost — was not.** The relevant sub-lever is listed on the node: *"LPG/PNG cooking-fuel subsidy and last-mile refill (MoPNG, Ujjwala)."*
- **Blocker.** The recurring fiscal cost: a deep opex subsidy is ~₹ per cylinder × ~100 million households × 8–12 cylinders a year — an open-ended annual liability, versus the connection subsidy's bounded one-time cost. The connection metric is politically sufficient, and there is no organised constituency of the harmed pressing for the opex side to be made permanent.

## D · Who is working on this

**Institution** — present, holds the lever, has moved it partially.
- **Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas (MoPNG)** and its executing vehicle **Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana**, delivered through the three state **Oil Marketing Companies** (IndianOil, BPCL, HPCL). Funding: Union budget (connection subsidy + the ₹300/cylinder targeted refill subsidy); PMUY outlay in the thousands of crore annually. Ujjwala 2.0 (2021) and a +75 lakh expansion (2023) took connections to 10.33 crore. MoPNG sets both the connection subsidy and the refill subsidy — it directly controls the opex lever that is the binding constraint — but its reporting unit is **connections and refills-per-beneficiary**, not household-years on clean fuel or in-kitchen exposure. Spend was front-loaded onto capex; the opex subsidy arrived seven years late (2023), is set annually, and at ₹300 for up to 12 cylinders still leaves a meaningful per-cylinder cash cost. Refill rates rose but plateaued near 4/year.
- **CEEW** — Delhi policy-research institute; produces the "State of the Clean Cooking Energy Sector" series and the fuel-supply/affordability analyses this leaf leans on. Proxy, not affected-led; philanthropic + multilateral research funding.
- **Household biogas — the tried-and-stalled state alternative.** Before LPG-for-the-poor, the same one-time-capex-subsidy design was run on biogas: the **National Project on Biogas Development (1981-82) → National Biogas and Manure Management Programme → today's NNBOMP / GOBARdhan** (MNRE). A capital subsidy (currently **₹9,800–70,400 per plant** by size, +₹1,600 for a toilet-linked plant with slurry filter) builds a household fixed-dome/floating-drum digester; the household then feeds it daily and cooks on the gas. Outcome: **~5 million household plants against MNRE's own 12-million potential estimate — ~40%, then flat.**
- **It excludes this leaf's population by design.** A 1 m³ plant needs ~25 kg of fresh dung a day (≈2–3 head of cattle) plus an equal volume of water and a plot for the digester and slurry pit, and **cattle ownership is itself a subsidy-eligibility criterion** — excluding the landless poorest third, the exact Ujjwala residual. The digester also runs on **wet dung, not the dry biomass a household already gathers**, so it is not a drop-in substitute for the existing fuel-collection chain.
- **And it repeats `second-half-never-built`.** The subsidy covered construction, not O&M, and field evaluations (Assam NBMMP study, Raha et al. 2014) report dome cracks, seasonal cattle migration and dried-up water leaving plants non-functional with no post-installation monitoring despite nominal four-year guarantees; official inspections put ~4.5% non-functional (independent field studies find worse). Current expert reading (CEEW, 2024) places biogas in cattle-dense **dairy clusters** (Gujarat, Punjab, Rajasthan) as one prong of a diversified mix — not a solution for the smoke-exposed landless household.

**Enterprise** — present, but keyed to a different object (improved biomass, not fuel switching). **The payer is the carbon-credit buyer, not the household.** Every attempt to sell *clean fuel* (LPG, electric, biogas) to this population as a business has failed on unit economics — the household cannot pay the recurring cost, so revenue requires a permanent third-party subsidy, which is a programme, not a company. The improved-biomass firms are the market's answer to "what can you actually sell here", and it is a partial-mitigation product: an efficient wood stove cuts smoke ~60–70% but does not remove it.

Earthfit is a newer instance of the exact Greenway model, with carbon monitoring built in digitally. It confirms the pattern; it does not change the gap.

- *Constraint-attack attempts, 2025–26 (all pre-scale, none with a bottom-of-pyramid payer yet):* **MyLPG AI** — Indian app predicting cylinder run-out and nudging refills, targeting the "gas khatam" affordability-planning problem rather than the fuel cost itself. **Modern Energy Cooking Services (MECS) "Sparking the Cooking Supply Chain" (SC2)** electric-induction pilots in Aurangabad and Gaya (biomass households, induction stove + utensil kit). Proposed induction-cooktop device subsidy on FAME lines. Pay-as-you-go LPG smart-metering exists (PayGo Energy) but only in East Africa, no Indian deployment. The binding constraint — recurring refill opex for the poorest third — still has no venture attached.

**Activism / affected-led** — absent at every unit.
- **SEWA (Self-Employed Women's Association)** runs a biogas/improved-cookstove programme for members that measurably cut firewood-collection time (reported 60→15 hours/month) and is women-centred and bottom-up in delivery — but it is one livelihoods programme inside a large organisation, **not a body whose members organised around indoor air as their claim** (adjacent unit; SEWA already has a record, link this leaf to it).
- **No dedicated organisation of household-air-pollution-affected women was found.** There is no HAP analogue of the silicosis victims' associations, no fenceline-community equivalent, no petitioner. The harm is diffuse, domestic, normalised, and its sufferers are among the least-organised demographic in the country. The subsidy that would fix it is therefore defended by no one when it competes for budget.

**Over-served comparison.** The densest commercial activity adjacent to "clean air indoors" is the **consumer air-purifier market (~$507M in 2024 → ~$1.8B projected 2033)**, serving affluent urban households — close to the least HAP-exposed population in India. Problem specification followed ability to pay, again.

**Representation present?** For the *institutional lever* — a body is present (MoPNG) and has moved the opex subsidy once, but the fiscal commitment at the scale and permanence sustained use needs is the empty slot. Enterprise builders exist for poor-cashflow delivery models (pay-as-you-go LPG metering, 5 kg cylinders, prepaid micro-refills, e-cooking) but stay pre-scale for want of a payer; the enterprise that *has* scaled is mis-keyed to improved-biomass mitigation. For the *harmed household* — no local-affected body. The load-bearing gap is the institutional funder for recurring sustained-use cost.

## E · Gap

`gap: coverage`, `gap_missing_leg: [institution]`, `gap_as_of: 2026-09-08`.

Harm unit = the rural woman cooking daily on wood or dung. The empty slot is not a delivery instrument — those exist (§D) — but the institutional actor that would underwrite recurring sustained-use cost permanently: a restructured MoPNG entitlement, a dedicated clean-cooking fund, a blended-finance vehicle. None has been built, and the lever-holder that exists will not commit at that scale absent a constituency pressing it to.

An institution is present, holds the lever, and has already moved it once (the 2023 refill subsidy) — so this reads as a coverage gap inside an active institutional relationship, not an absence of any body able to perceive the harm.
