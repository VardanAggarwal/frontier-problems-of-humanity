# Asbestos in the air — the legal import trade and the installed roofing stock

One carrier, one need, two routes into the air. **Route 1 — the trade:** chrysotile fibre is legally imported at a third to half a million tonnes a year and fabricated into asbestos-cement (AC) sheeting; the harmed are plant workers, the informal construction labour who cut and fit it, and the households who buy the roof. **Route 2 — the stock:** the ~7 million tonnes already installed since 1960 re-enter the air as they weather, are renovated and are demolished; the harmed are the occupant and the bystander, who never bought anything and never worked with the material. The routes have different evidence bases, different blockers and — decisively — different administrative owners, but they are the same failure: nothing in India measures asbestos in the air, and so neither route can be stopped.

The split had followed the exposure route; the corpus splits leaves by material and failure, not by route — [`silicosis-stone-industry`](/leaf/silicosis-stone-industry) covers quarrying, stone cutting, agate grinding, foundries and construction in one record.

## A · Classification

Chrysotile ("white") asbestos — a confirmed Group 1 human carcinogen causing asbestosis, lung cancer, mesothelioma, and laryngeal/ovarian cancer — is banned outright in roughly 68–70 countries. India banned only **mining** (no fresh leases from 1986; the last mines wound down by the mid-1990s) and the **import and use of crocidolite** (blue asbestos). Import, manufacture, sale and use of chrysotile remain fully legal. India is the world's largest importer and consumer, ~85%+ of it fabricated into AC roofing sheets and pipes explicitly marketed as an affordable building material for low-income households. The hazard is installed into poor housing stock as a development product, and the bill falls due decades later — first as occupational exposure, then, twenty years on, as ambient exposure when the roof is cut, repaired or pulled down.

- **Channel — structural.** No single actor acts; the exposure is a property of how the trade and the informal building economy are arranged — legal fibre imports → AC-sheet manufacture (a countable set of ~15–20 plants and their workers) → roofing sold through the informal housing market → in-situ weathering, cutting, drilling, re-roofing and demolition by householders and unregistered construction labour, none of it under an exposure-control regime. Controls exist on paper (Supreme Court, *CERC v. Union of India*, 1995: membrane-filter monitoring, 40-year worker health records, compulsory insurance, decadal ILO-aligned permissible-exposure-limit review; BIS IS 11769 on installation and handling) and are defeated by the arrangement of the work.
  - **The route-2 tension, named.** For the demolition route the *product* is designed but the *release path* is not: the Construction and Demolition Waste Management Rules 2016 contain no asbestos stream, no pre-demolition survey requirement and no wet-removal protocol, so nothing in the system is aimed at that pathway at all. Read alone, that route reads `ambient-accidental`. It is classified `structural` here because the absence is not accidental — it is the same instrument design that excluded the resident from every other asbestos control, which is carried in §C as `instrument-keyed-to-wrong-object` rather than as a separate channel.
- **Satisfier relation — violator.** Breathable air at the worksite and in the home carries respirable fibres (`degraded-quality` at the surface); the load-bearing structure is that a *shelter* satisfier — cheap AC roofing — is the air violator over 20–40 years, the within-tier loop named in the [tier-1 summary](/tier/1), and hardest at the roof's end of life.
- **Onset — latent.** Mesothelioma latency is 20–40 years; asbestosis 10–30. India's consumption is peaking now, so the population-scale mortality wave arrives in the **2040s–2060s**, after the political window for prevention has closed. Isolated hospital-diagnosed cases already exist; a counted national toll does not. **No local affected party exists by construction** — §D routes to central activism, to institution, or to nobody.
- **Agent — industrial-exposure.** A chronic industrial inhalation hazard: AC-plant workers, construction and demolition labour, plumbers and re-roofers by employment; occupants and bystanders by residence. The relation to the source is the material, not a discharge — which is why one value covers both routes.

## B · Evidence

### Route 1 — the import trade

- **Consumption magnitude.** India's raw chrysotile fibre imports by year (Indian customs data compiled by IBAS and trade databases): ~379,043 t (2014); ~436,119 t (2021); ~403,292 t (2022); **~485,182 t of white asbestos in 2023** (~500,000 t counting all asbestos lines), the peak year; **~348,000–349,000 t in 2024**, a ~28% fall by weight (IndexBox reports the drop as −18% *by value*, to US$243M). The [air](/need/air) record already carries this as a **precedent disagreement**: figures differ by whether they count **chrysotile fibre only** or **all asbestos lines**, and by weight vs customs value. The reliable claim is **sustained scale of the order of a third to a half million tonnes a year for over a decade** — not a growth rate, not a single authoritative number, and whether the 2024 fall is real demand contraction or a reporting artefact is unresolved.
- **Source countries.** Historically ~85% from Russia; after the 2022 sanctions Russian tonnage roughly halved and Brazil and Kazakhstan took up the slack — Russia + Brazil + Kazakhstan ≈ 89% of 2024 imports.
- **End use.** The overwhelming majority is AC roofing sheet and pipe. India has ~15–20 AC-product plants (members of the manufacturers' association, ACPMA, since renamed FCPMA); the product is sold as the cheapest durable roof, so the exposed household population is a large fraction of rural and peri-urban India plus the informal construction workforce that cuts and fits it. Exact denominators are not published.

### Route 2 — the installed stock

- **The stock.** India has incorporated on the order of **7 million tonnes of asbestos into its building stock since 1960**, almost all of it as AC corrugated roofing, with an advocacy estimate of **more than 100 million people — disproportionately Dalit — living under asbestos roofs**. That stock is now aging into its renovation-and-demolition window.
- **The release mechanism is conceded on the record.** In the NGT proceeding the **MoEF&CC admitted** that asbestos fibres "can enter the air, water and soil from the weathering, renovation or demolition of manufactured asbestos products and people are likely to be exposed … through inhalation of asbestos airborne fibres." The tribunal used that admission to reject the manufacturers' association's contrary claim that fibres stay locked in the cement matrix (judgment ¶121). This route is no longer speculative; a central ministry has conceded it in a judicial proceeding.
- **The one Indian TEM measurement is near-source, not urban.** Singh, Fitzgerald, Dada and Frank (IJERPH, February 2025; medRxiv preprint October 2024) analysed dust from a marble waste slurry dump-yard at Rajsamand, Rajasthan by **transmission electron microscopy** and found abundant **tremolite** asbestos — an equivalent quantity of the tested Kelwa marble dust containing on the order of **37,000 countable fibrous structures** of a regulated asbestos mineral. Contractors dispose of slurry on open land; it spreads in the rains and becomes airborne when dry. The site is open to the public and has become a tourist destination. This confirms the analytical route and a second, previously unmapped Indian source (asbestos as a contaminant of *non*-asbestos mining), but it is dump-yard dust — the urban ambient measurement still does not exist.

### The measurement state — one absence, two shapes, and it is the finding

- **Hundreds of thousands of tonnes, near-zero reported disease.** **India has never reported a single mesothelioma death to the WHO mortality database.** China, Russia and India are routinely *excluded* from global asbestos-mortality models for lack of any usable national data. The National Cancer Registry Programme covers ~**16% of the population** (ICMR-NCDIR). A hospital-based study (Public Health Action / The Union, 2024) found **2,213 mesothelioma cases from 83 hospitals from 2012 onward**; for 2012–2016 it recorded **1,126 hospital-reported cases against 54 in the National Cancer Registry** — a ~20× under-capture, and cancer is not nationally notifiable. There is no dedicated mesothelioma or asbestos-disease registry, no systematic occupational-disease surveillance, and few pathologists trained to diagnose asbestos-related disease.
- **Advocacy estimate.** Toxics Watch Alliance's June 2011 complaint to the National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) cited **~50,000 asbestos-related deaths a year** — a modelled/advocacy figure, contested and unverifiable against any surveillance system. Global reference points: WHO puts asbestos-related deaths above 200,000/year worldwide (~38,000 mesothelioma); Global Burden of Disease models ~30,000 mesothelioma/year. India's true figure sits in a range whose lower bound (official) is **zero** and whose upper bound (advocacy) is **~50,000** — and the width of that range is the finding, not a number to be picked. The UK still records ~2,500 asbestos-related deaths a year — the delayed bill for use that *peaked in the 1960s–70s and was banned in 1999*; India's exposure curve is 40+ years behind and still rising.
- **No ambient measurement exists, and in 2025 that absence decided a case.** Asked to assess *non-occupational* asbestos hazard, the expert committee constituted by CPCB on 7 November 2024 — 12 experts, two meetings, **no study of its own** — had three measurement sources available. Two were Indian and both occupational: ICMR-NIOH's 2012 study (427 observations in or near asbestos industry; 18–19% above the 0.1 f/cc work-environment limit) and DGFASLI's 2019 study of 50 AC plants (35 within 0.1 f/ml, 15 in the range 0.2–0.4 f/ml). The third, and the only household-ambient one, was **Sri Lankan** — the National Building Research Organisation's 2021 air-quality study, reporting household concentrations of 0.00016–0.00034 f/cc average and 0.00071–0.00077 f/cc maximum. There is no Indian equivalent to substitute for it.
- **The threshold was keyed to the wrong population.** The committee benchmarked non-occupational exposure against **0.1 fibres/cc, the industrial occupational limit**, while the Sri Lankan study it relied on states a **non-occupational standard of 0.0009 fibres/cc** — a factor of roughly 111 — and itself recommends including asbestos in air-quality standards and monitoring programmes. The applicant also placed on record that the Indian soil standard is 10,000 mg/kg where several countries use 100 mg/kg, and that the ICMR-NICPR representative did not sign the committee's additional report of 21 February 2025.
- **The consequence.** The tribunal held that "in the absence of any positive specific scientific evidence/material" an immediate discontinuance direction was not warranted, declining to apply the precautionary principle and citing *Kalyaneshwari v. Union of India* (2011) 3 SCC 287. The missing denominator produced the ruling. This is the strongest instance in tier 1 of absence of measurement being load-bearing rather than merely a gap in the research.
- **Feasible and routine elsewhere.** US EPA ran more than 10,000 TEM samples after 9/11; Denmark monitors its four largest cities. India has no ambient programme for a hazard sitting on ~100 million roofs, no exposure registry keyed to the person, no register of asbestos-laden buildings, and no functioning mandatory occupational-disease notification. The state imports and consumes asbestos at world-leading scale and operates **no instrument that would let the resulting disease be counted**. Absence of data is being read as absence of harm.

### Differential vulnerability

- **(a) Hit hardest.** AC-plant production workers; unregistered construction, demolition and re-roofing labour; plumbers and electricians drilling AC sheet; and — distinctively for India — **household members** living under weathering AC roofs and **women and children** during in-home cutting/repair. The population under aging roofs is poor and disproportionately Dalit; the roof is the cheapest durable option, and its most dangerous phase is its last one, which arrives without an owner having done anything. Informal demolition and C&D-waste handlers work the debris stream with no separation, no wetting and no PPE. Non-smokers are affected (asbestos causes lung cancer independent of smoking) — a partial explanation for India's non-smoker lung-cancer load.
- **(b) Why they cannot exit or defend.** **No information** (chrysotile is widely believed safe; latency means no felt symptom for decades and no attributable event at exposure), **no resources** (AC sheet is bought precisely because it is the cheapest roof, and replacing one costs more than keeping it), and **no standing** (informal workers, no employment record, no diagnosis, no notifiable-disease pathway; and for the resident, no measurement, so no claim to make and nothing to be a claimant *of*). This routes representation to a **central organisation** performing attribution as a specialist function, exactly as the [tier-1 summary](/tier/1) assigns it — and on route 2 the first thing such a body must do is commission a measurement, not file a claim.

## C · Diagnosis

- **Mechanism — `instrument-keyed-to-wrong-object` (primary, and it explains both routes with one sentence).** Every asbestos instrument India owns is keyed to the wrong object. The *ban* is keyed to **mining** — domestic extraction that had already all but ceased — and to **crocidolite**, the fibre India barely imports, so the actual exposure pathway (chrysotile import → manufacture → use → demolition) is excluded from the prohibition **by design** and the government can claim it has "regulated asbestos". The *exposure controls* are keyed to the worker: the 0.1 f/cc permissible exposure limit is an occupational limit, both Indian studies are occupational, the notification regimes sit under the Factories and Mines Acts, and NIOH and DGFASLI are occupational-health bodies. The exposed resident is adjacent to all of it and covered by none — which is why, when a tribunal asked the non-occupational question in 2025, the only available instrument answered a different one.
- **Mechanism — `solution-at-hand-blocked`.** On route 1 the fix is not to use it: chrysotile is banned in ~68–70 countries, and non-asbestos fibre-cement (cellulose/PVA), micro-concrete roofing tiles and galvanised/coated steel are established substitutes at comparable cost; the controls India has not adopted are settled ILO/WHO practice. On route 2 a TEM ambient survey is settled technique, routine in the US and Denmark, and India has at least four NABL-accredited laboratories already running asbestos air monitoring. Nothing technical is missing on either route. The [tier-1 summary](/tier/1) names this "the cleanest diagnosis in the tier" and routes it **activism → institution; capital cannot move it**.
- **Mechanism — `authority-mismatched-to-harm`.** Import sits with DGFT / Ministry of Commerce; manufacture and clearance with MoEF&CC and state Pollution Control Boards; workplace exposure with the Ministry of Labour / DGFASLI; disease surveillance with MoHFW / ICMR-NCDIR. Asbestos is not among India's 12 NAAQS parameters, so CPCB's ambient-air mandate does not contain the pollutant; the C&D Waste Management Rules 2016 give the urban local body a demolition-waste duty with no asbestos stream inside it; hazardous-waste rules cover disposal but not re-entrainment. No body's jurisdiction contains the exposed person, and no instrument makes that person a unit — which is why the Supreme Court (1995) and the NGT (2025) have each had to *assemble* a cross-ministry directive rather than point to an owner.
- **Mechanism — `compensation-substitutes-for-counting` — in its degenerate form.** For silicosis the state built a functioning payout layer (Rajasthan, ₹550 cr+) and never built the detection layer. For asbestos **neither half was built**: no state asbestos-disease compensation scheme, no registry, no detection. The mechanism applies only as its "no counting" half — which shades into pure measurement-absence — but the political logic is identical: not counting the harm keeps liability invisible and protects the trade. Tagged for cross-leaf comparability with silicosis and lead under the node; the payout half is absent.
- **Mechanism — `within-tier-loop` (secondary).** Shelter provision degrades air within the same tier: the cheapest way to satisfy the shelter need is the thing that fails the air need twenty years later. The loop is why the harmed population cannot simply be told to stop — the alternative sheet is the same object at a higher price.
- **Primary vs derivative burden.** Two mismatches, one per route. On route 1 the visible, debated object is **occupational exposure in AC plants** (the industry's "controlled use" framing), while the load-bearing burden is the **stock already installed** plus the tonnage still being added each year. On route 2 the visible claim-fight is about **schools**, because a school is a legible unit with a named authority and a sympathetic occupant — the tribunal itself noticed the mismatch, observing that the prayer for schools "ignores/overlooks the adverse impact of asbestos cement roofing sheets in residential houses or other buildings likely to be frequented by the children/students, where their stay may also be for equal or longer periods." The load-bearing exposure is residential; the litigated unit is institutional.
- **Nodes.** Downstream of [`toxic-exposure-class`](/node/toxic-exposure-class) — asbestos is one of the node's three carriers (with lead and silica): long-latency toxic exposure with no person-keyed registry, banned or controlled elsewhere, reaching the poor as a construction/development input. AC roofing is the exact analogue of leaded paint and unmonitored quarry silica, and the node's single prescribed intervention — a statutory exposure registry keyed to the person, plus a dated phase-out schedule aligned to banning jurisdictions — is precisely this leaf's two missing layers. Also downstream of [`construction`](/node/construction) — the sector node, where the specific sub-lever is the C&D Waste Management Rules' missing asbestos stream and pre-demolition survey.
- **Blocker — three, and they are sequential rather than alternative.**
  1. **The domestic asbestos-cement manufacturing lobby** — the manufacturers' association (ACPMA, since renamed FCPMA) and member firms (Everest Industries, Visaka Industries, HIL/BirlaNu, Ramco, UAL, Sarvottam), a ~₹5,000+ crore roofing-sheet industry that funds "controlled use" science and lobbies against any import restriction.
  2. **India's standing veto at the Rotterdam Convention** — chrysotile has been recommended for Annex III (Prior Informed Consent trade-notification, *not* a ban) by the Chemical Review Committee since 2006 and blocked at every Conference of the Parties through April–May 2025, because listing requires consensus and a small producer/user bloc (Russia, Kazakhstan, India, Zimbabwe, Pakistan) refuses; India's formal position since February 2005 is that chrysotile is *not* a hazardous chemical.
  3. **On route 2, an evidence standard applied to evidence nobody is obliged to generate** — the precautionary principle was declined for want of "positive specific scientific evidence", and the directions that would break that loop were issued and the deadline passed. MoEF&CC had six months from 30 October 2025 to review the evidence and frame policy; MoEF&CC, MoHUA, MoE and CPCB were to file Action Taken Reports within one month after that. **The ATR was due around 31 May 2026; as of this leaf's date, 9 September 2026, no compliance is on the public record.** Paragraph 126 makes the tribunal's Registrar General responsible for relisting the matter if the reports do not come.

  Real recent movement on the first two: the **NGT's 30 October 2025 order** (85 pages) endorsed MoEF&CC's stated "vision" to phase out **all** forms of asbestos and adopt alternatives, directed state Chief Secretaries and PCBs, reiterated the 1995 SC directions, and exposed the NHRC's 2016 closure as resting on a conflicted study — a directive on a stated intent, not yet a statutory phase-out.

Union of India*, O.A. 298/2023, argued **as party-in-person** against three ministries, CPCB and the manufacturers' association's three counsel; and the **first author** of the only Indian TEM asbestos-dust measurement of the last decade (IJERPH 2025). The same person is both litigating the absence of evidence and personally generating the evidence. That combination does not appear anywhere else in this tier.
- **Sean Fitzgerald** (Scientific Analytical Institute, US) — the TEM analytical capability behind the Rajasthan finding. **Arthur L. Frank** (Drexel) — asbestos epidemiologist with a long India engagement. **Rima Dada** (AIIMS New Delhi) — the Indian institutional co-author, and the plausible in-country host for a survey. All three are evidence-base authors: `registry` under the ground test, not monitored.
- **Won rulings, lost the object.** The 1995 SC directions were never operationalised; a 2011 MoEF phase-out statement of intent went nowhere; the 2011 TWA/NHRC complaint was **closed in August 2016** on the strength of an NIOH "no significant health impact" study **co-funded by the asbestos industry (~₹16 lakh)** — a conflict the NGT called out nine years later. Kerala restricted government use (2009); Maharashtra issued a "no asbestos" line in regional plans (2018). Import remained legal throughout, and the activists have **never moved the import trade or India's Rotterdam position** — the campaign's defining stalemate. Density of activist effort is *low* relative to the exposure; a handful of individuals carry most of it — under-resourced, not over-represented.
- **Nobody is affected-led on route 2, and the reason is structural.** An affected-led body needs an affected population that knows it is affected. With no measurement, a 20–40 year latency and no diagnosis, the exposed resident cannot identify themselves — so the leg that would normally hold the ground has no constituency to organise. IAVA exists on route 1 only because asbestos *victims* exist and are named; there is no resident equivalent.

**Institution** — cross-ministry directives with no owner; a surveillance body that does not surveil; and, since October 2025, a dated order in default.
- **Supreme Court of India** — *CERC v. Union of India* (1995): the foundational directions, substantially unimplemented. **National Human Rights Commission** — held the 2011–2016 TWA complaint, closed it in 2016 on the conflicted NIOH study; criticised by the NGT in 2025. **DGFT (Directorate General of Foreign Trade) / Ministry of Commerce** — controls the import licence; the single administrative edit that would end fresh supply is moving chrysotile to the prohibited import list. **ICMR-NCDIR** (National Cancer Registry Programme, ~16% coverage) — the body whose reporting unit would have to become the mesothelioma / asbestos-disease case, and currently does not. **ICMR-NIOH** — did the industry-co-funded study the NHRC relied on; the institution that would otherwise be the natural home of an exposure registry. **ICMR-NICPR**'s representative declining to sign the expert committee's additional report is the single documented institutional dissent on the record. **ICMR-NIOH** (2012) and **DGFASLI** (2019) hold the only Indian fieldwork capability; both have measured only occupationally.
- **The NGT judgment of 30 October 2025** is the first Indian instrument that names the installed-stock exposure route. Its operative directions:
  - **XVI — CPCB** to prepare, finalise and issue an SOP for proper disposal of cement-asbestos waste at authorised sites under the Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules 2016 "and **prevent re-entrainment into air matrix on degradation**." That clause is route 2's remedy, ordered by name.
  - **XIV — CPCB and the State Pollution Control Boards / Pollution Control Committees** to evolve a special mechanism for monitoring asbestos-waste disposal, the tribunal accepting that even proper disposal leaves gradual contamination requiring ongoing monitoring of affected areas.
  - **XVII — MoEF&CC** to (i) review the entire scientific evidence and global best practice within six months and frame policy on use of AC roofing in schools, **residential houses and other buildings** with an action plan and timelines, per its own *Vision Statement on Environment and Human Health* ¶4.3.1; and (ii) issue guidelines on manufacturing, installation, maintenance, **dismantling** and disposal, including AC water-supply pipelines.
  - **XV — Ministry of Education** to advise schools to maintain existing sheets in good condition, coat them, follow IS 11769 Part 1 in handling, and dispose at authorised sites. **MoHUA** is respondent no. 2 and an ATR filer.
  - ¶124 requires circulation to all Chief Secretaries and SPCB/PCC Member Secretaries; ¶125 sets the ATR; ¶126 puts the Registrar General on relisting duty.
- **The asymmetry between the routes is the institutional finding.** On route 1 no ministry's jurisdiction spans import + manufacture + use + disease, and the courts have had to assemble authority twice (1995, 2025) precisely because no standing owner exists — there is *less* institutional machinery on asbestos than on silicosis (which at least has state pneumoconiosis boards) or lead: no board, no scheme, no registry. On route 2 the authority *does* contain the source — CPCB can write the SOP — which distinguishes it from the usual tier-1 pattern. There the failure is not jurisdictional; it is non-delivery against a live order.

**Enterprise (a) — serving the harm as framed: present on route 2, at the wrong segment; absent on route 1 and organised against it.**
- The abatement and testing layer exists in India and is NABL-accredited: **EPSCO India** (Mumbai; founder **Suhel Parker**, US-certified inspector/planner/contractor) for survey, removal and disposal, and **Qualitek Labs**, **ITC Labs**, **ALS India** and **Elca Labs** for ACM surveys and airborne fibre analysis by PLM/PCM. Every one of them sells into hospitals, airports, pharma and food plants, data centres, power stations and infrastructure projects — customers who procure compliance voluntarily or under a project condition. **None sells ambient monitoring, because nobody is required to buy it.** The constraint is not that India lacks the instrument or the laboratory. India has both, idle. The constraint is the absent procurement trigger — which is exactly direction XVI, ordered and undelivered. The residential and informal-demolition segment, holding the entire in-place mass, has no actor and no payer.
- **Over-served check.** Corporate-facility abatement is served by at least five commercial actors; the ~100 million people under AC roofs are served by none. The served population is not the harmed one — the tier-1 over-served pattern, in a market small enough to count on one hand.

**Enterprise (b) — attacking the binding constraint: the substitute exists, and the incumbents make it.** The binding constraint is price parity against AC sheet, not availability. **Everest Industries** sells Hi-Tech non-asbestos fibre-cement profile sheets reinforced with high-impact PVA/polypropylene fibre at roughly ₹18–23 per sq ft; **BirlaNu** (formerly HIL) and **Visaka** run comparable non-asbestos lines; **ARL Infratech** distributes. **2K Technologies** sells the plant machinery for *both* asbestos and non-asbestos corrugated sheet — the same line, a different input, which is the cleanest available evidence that the substitution is an input decision rather than a capital one. Kerala's 2019 government order specifies the substitute by name (HIPP-reinforced 6 mm corrugated sheet), so a state has already written a procurement spec that works.
- **But the substitute makers are the incumbents, and they are hedged.** All four largest fibre-cement makers run a non-asbestos line alongside a chrysotile line they will not exit:

- All four fund the association's "controlled use" / anti-Rotterdam line; none markets its substitute on worker-health grounds or holds a phase-out commitment, so none is tracked as a remedy actor. The one near-pure-play named — **SHERA India** (`shera-india`; Mahaphant / Shera Thailand; no asbestos product anywhere in the line) — is `stance: neutral`, `depth: registry`: a premium wood-substitute board *imported* (no Indian plant), small and restructuring, with green-building/aesthetic framing, zero advocacy, and no distribution to the households who buy AC roofing. A pure substitute maker *can* exist — SHERA proves it — but it does not reach the exposed cohort and is not an ally.
- **The graveyard here is not of failed ventures; it is of demand.** No venture needs inventing. What is missing is the mandate that would move a buyer from the ₹X asbestos sheet to the ₹X+δ non-asbestos one — the leg-2 → leg-3 handoff, where a mandate constitutes the market. On the import route the fix is a prohibition plus a registry, neither of which is a market, as the [tier-1 summary](/tier/1) says explicitly ("capital cannot move it").

**Opposed — present and well-resourced.** The **Fibre Cement Products Manufacturers Association** (FCPMA, formerly ACPMA) — the domestic AC roofing-sheet and pipe industry, `stance: organised-against-remedy`. It was respondent no. 4 in the NGT case, appeared through three counsel against a party-in-person, argued that fibres stay locked in the cement matrix (rejected), and after the judgment placed a press release carried by ANI and republished by *Business Standard* and *The Wire* under the headline "NGT Upholds Safety of Asbestos-Cement Roofing: No Evidence of Public Health Risk." BANI published the same judgment as a reiteration of the Supreme Court's directions paving the way for alternatives. **Both readings are defensible from a split outcome** — relief refused, seventeen remedial directions issued and a phase-out action plan ordered — and the divergence is itself the finding: the framing that reached the wires was placed by the losing respondent, and the judgment does not say what the headline says. The **International Chrysotile Association (ICA)** is the global producer lobby (Russian/Kazakh mining interests, Brazil), which produces the "controlled/safe use" science and the COP-by-COP Rotterdam lobbying material. The paying customer for AC roofing is rural and peri-urban households buying the cheapest durable roof, and government housing schemes that historically specified it — the demand is real and price-driven, the industry's strongest card.

**Positive control — Kerala, on the instrument only.** The Kerala High Court banned asbestos roofs in schools in 2019 with a two-year replacement window, implemented by G.O. (Ms) No. 162/2019/GEDN of 9 October 2019, which specifies non-asbestos HIPP-reinforced sheet as the substitute for schools and anganwadis; the Kerala State Human Rights Commission had recommended a phase-out as early as 31 January 2009. The NGT expressly carved Kerala out of its holding that no direction was warranted. No measured before/after exists — the same problem as everywhere else on this leaf. It is a positive control on the rule, not on the exposure.

**Representation present?** Split by route, and that split is why the leaf-level verdict is `representation`. On the import route, yes and at the right unit: BANI / IAVA / OEHNI / Gopal Krishna / T. K. Joshi constitute a durable central-org representation present since ~2000, which has repeatedly won rulings (1995, 2025) and repeatedly failed to move the trade — representation is *eliminated* as the explanation there, and the blocker isolates cleanly to the manufacturers' lobby and the Commerce-ministry import licence. On the installed-stock route, no: activism is carried substantially by one unpaid individual, institution is correctly scoped and in default of a dated order, and enterprise is present at the corporate-facility segment and absent at the residential one. No actor of any leg is organised at the unit of the person living under, or standing downwind of, a degrading asbestos roof — and no such actor can form until someone measures.

## E · Gap

The gap is **representation**, and it resolves differently on each route — which is the argument for holding them in one record rather than two, because the catalyst move on one is the missing half of the other.

**On the import route, representation is eliminated as the explanation — which is not the same as the problem being handled.** Campaigners have been present and correctly located since 2000 (central, because a latency harm gives no sufferer anything to attribute), have forced two apex rulings, and the trade is unchanged. With representation, technology and precedent all present, the 25-year non-ban attributes to a **named blocker**: the asbestos-cement lobby's domestic weight, plus the Commerce/Rotterdam veto that keeps fresh chrysotile flowing and keeps even a labelling obligation off the table. The enterprise leg is empty here and structurally so — the remedy is a ban plus a person-keyed registry, and neither is a market, so no company is missing; one would be misplaced.

**On the installed-stock route the enterprise leg is present at the wrong unit** — `gap_missing_leg: [enterprise]`. Five commercial actors run abatement and NABL-accredited fibre analysis in India today, and the substitute product is made at scale by the incumbents. They serve airports, data centres and pharma plants. What is missing is now precisely nameable and dated: not a study in the abstract, but direction XVI of the judgment of 30 October 2025 — a CPCB standard operating procedure to prevent re-entrainment on degradation — plus the MoEF&CC policy under XVII(i), both due, both unfiled, with the Registrar General empowered to relist. Constructing a claimant here still resolves to *commissioning a measurement* rather than founding an organisation, but the commissioning body is identified, under order, and in default.

**The substitute technology is not the lever it looks like on either route.** The four largest fibre-cement makers (Ramco, Everest, Visaka, HIL/BirlaNu) each run a non-asbestos line *alongside* a chrysotile line they will not exit, and fund the lobby's blocking — hedged dual-producers who would net-lose on a phase-out. The one near-pure-play, `shera-india`, is a premium importer that never reaches the AC-roofing customer. So the catalyst move is not to construct a claimant, and not to ally the substitute industry. It is two connections: the existing campaigners to whoever can move the import licence at DGFT / Ministry of Commerce, and the same campaigners — who have the standing and the 25-year record — to the overdue CPCB and MoEF&CC filings that Raja Singh is currently pursuing alone.
