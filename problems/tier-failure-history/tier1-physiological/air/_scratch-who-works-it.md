# Who is commercially working on this, and who pays them

- **Consumer air purifiers are the densest market and serve close to the least-exposed population.** The category runs **$507M (2024) toward a projected $1.8B (2033)** — Airveda (₹4.31 cr FY25 revenue, largely B2B monitors for corporates and construction sites), Atlanta Healthcare, Nirvana Being, Atovio (bootstrapped, ~18,000 units at ₹3,500), Praan (which failed to raise in India and raised in the US instead). The buyer is an affluent urban household or a corporate office. Against that: **52 million silica-exposed workers with under 1% detection**, and outdoor workers, informal-industry labourers and slum residents who buy nothing. Problem specification followed ability to pay, not exposure.
- **Silicosis and lead have no commercial actor of scale, and the missing element is a payer rather than a technology.** ICMR-NIOH developed a **point-of-care CC16 finger-prick silicosis screening test** — a public research output, not a product line anyone sells. Wet drilling, ventilation and respirators are settled controls with no company selling them into informal quarrying at scale. Blood-lead testing is likewise absent as a market despite **275 million children with elevated blood lead**. Quarry workers cannot pay, unorganised employers will not, and no insurer carries the risk.
- **Rajasthan's silicosis scheme shows why no detection market formed.** **23,436 certifications (incl. 6,876 deaths) and ₹550 cr+ disbursed** (revised figures; migrated to `air/silicosis-stone-industry.md` §D) — a compensation layer that pays *after* disease is certified rather than funding the finding of it. That caps liability at the number of people who manage to get certified and leaves the denominator unknown, which is `00-summary.md` mechanism 7 (*compensation substitutes for counting*) reappearing as a reason no diagnostics industry exists. A payout ceiling is not a market signal.
- **Ambient sensing sells to governments and platforms, not to the exposed.** **Respirer Living Sciences** has deployed 2,000+ monitors including ~700 across UP and Bihar; **Ambee** sells hyperlocal air-quality data as a service. Revenue comes from government contracts, corporate ESG and platform partnerships. The detection layer this tier keeps finding absent is, in air specifically, commercially viable — but only because a non-exposed third party buys the data.
- **CEMS is a functioning compliance market.** CPCB mandated continuous emissions monitoring across **17 industry categories (2014)**, with CSIR-NPL certifying instruments since 2019. The payer is the emitter under mandate. Together with ZLD in `02-water.md`, this is the second instance in the tier of regulation constituting an industry — and both attach to a countable set of permit-holding facilities.
- **Stubble burning is being solved by a mandate written for a different reason.** Punjab had **16 operational biomass pelletisation plants with 21 more expected by late 2024**, against a **₹50 cr CPCB subsidy (₹12.37 cr disbursed)** — but the demand actually comes from the **7% biomass co-firing obligation on thermal plants within 300 km of Delhi (FY25–26)**. Happy Seeder and the PUSA bio-decomposer remain subsidised equipment rather than businesses, because at farm-gate prices the farmer's cost-benefit does not clear. The pellet market works because a power utility must buy; the seeder does not because only the farmer would.
- **Clean cooking is the tier's best-documented capital graveyard, and the reason is instrument design.** Ujjwala delivered **70M+ LPG connections**; refill rates run at **less than half those of general rural consumers**, **25% of beneficiaries took zero or one refill in 2022–23 (CAG)**, and no household cohort sustained LPG above 55% of cooking time. The subsidy covered **capex (the connection) and not opex (the refills)** — cost, not access or awareness, is the binding constraint (SEI 2024; *Nature Energy* 2019). Commercial actors selling *improved biomass* stoves — Greenway (~550K units), Envirofit, Prakti — survive precisely by not attempting fuel switching. This is *instrument keyed to the wrong object* with an unusually clean measurement of the miss.
- **Construction dust is the most commercially served failure by rupee volume and among the least well-matched to harm.** CREA's *Tracing the Hazy Air* (2026) finds **68% of ₹9,929 cr in NCAP spending** went to mechanical sweepers, sprinklers and anti-smog guns. A procurement market exists, the only payer is a municipal tender, and the spend is not aimed at the sources carrying the mortality burden — the same *spend mismatched to source* finding, now visible from the vendor side.

# Who represents the affected, and at what unit

- **Delhi-NCR general air pollution — present at the local unit, unit mismatch against an airshed-scale harm.** Warrior Moms (1,500+ members, 13 states, founded by five Delhi mothers) is the clearest case, already classified in `00-summary.md`. Three further citizen-founded (not think-tank) groups sit in the same slot: **Care for Air** (est. 2015, NCR volunteer base), **Help Delhi Breathe** (citizen mobilisation backed by IKEA Foundation/Clean Air Fund), and **Right to Breathe India** (citizen petition movement). All four are genuinely affected-led — urban, educated parents and residents — rather than professional NGO staff, and all four carry the same structural mismatch: the harm unit is an airshed, the representation unit is a household.
- **Stubble burning — present, and organised against the remedy.** Punjab farmer unions (Bharti Kisan Union – Ekta Ugrahan; the broader Kisan Mazdoor Morcha coalition) actively mobilise against the *penalty regime* — contesting 1,000+ FIRs, demanding case withdrawal — rather than around residue-management alternatives. This is the tier's clearest instance of the affected party also being the source, which is the same shape as farm power and ethanol feedstock elsewhere in the taxonomy: representation exists, and its object is resisting the fix rather than seeking it.
- **Loni and Byrnihat — largely absent, with one data point of spontaneous local action.** No standing resident association was found for either town. Byrnihat produced one instance of direct affected-led action — Lower Balian residents physically shutting down an illegal ferro-alloy unit (2026) — which is a blocker moved once, but ad hoc rather than institutionalised; there is no organisation to build on. Loni has documented resident anger at hazardous AQI but no named body. Given that both towns' industry is informal-sector and their workers among the least organised populations in this file, this is close to the gate's failing verdict — absent at every unit — softened only by Byrnihat's one-off.
- **Indoor cookfire smoke — present, but at the wrong unit (diffuse, not claim-specific).** SEWA runs a biogas/cookstove programme that measurably cuts smoke exposure for members (firewood-collection time down from 60 to 15 hours/month), and it is women-centred and bottom-up in delivery. But it is one livelihoods programme among many inside a broader organisation, not a body whose members organised specifically around indoor air as their claim — closer to an adjacent representation unit than a dedicated one.
- **CEMS / industrial-emitter compliance — absent at every unit.** No worker union or fenceline-community organisation was found pushing on emissions data or emitters directly; all coverage of CEMS is regulatory/technical (CPCB and SPCB data-quality gaps). The compliance market above has no organised counterpart on the harm side — confirming that the industry exists because a mandate manufactured a customer, not because anyone harmed can act against the emitter.
- **Asbestos — present at the right unit, unmoved for decades.** BANI (Ban Asbestos Network India) and IAVA (India Asbestos Victims Association) have campaigned centrally since ~2000 — the correct unit for a 20–40 year latency harm no individual sufferer can attribute at the point of exposure. Representation is eliminated as the explanation for inaction; the blocker isolates cleanly to the import trade itself.
