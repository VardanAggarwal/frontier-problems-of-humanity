# Small industrial towns as the worst-air places

## A · Classification

- **Channel — structural.** The harm follows from how these towns are arranged: dense clusters of informal, sub-threshold manufacturing units; captive diesel/coal combustion against an unreliable grid; a consent-to-operate and continuous-emissions regime keyed to large registered "red-category" facilities; and a monitoring network that reaches named cities and not the towns around them. Byrnihat has identifiable point emitters, which pulls toward `direct`, but the recurring pattern across Loni, Ghaziabad, Bhiwadi, Bahadurgarh, Hapur and peers is systemic, and there is deliberate system design (thresholds, city lists) rather than an accidental source — so not `ambient-accidental`.
- **Satisfier relation — degraded-quality.** Breathable air is present but annual PM2.5 sits at 20–22× the WHO guideline of 5 µg/m³ (Loni 112.5 µg/m³, 2025). Against India's own NAAQS of 40 µg/m³ — itself 8× WHO — these towns are still 2.5–3× non-compliant.
- **Onset — chronic.** Year-round elevated PM2.5, not an episode. It is in mortality data — the GBD 2019 figure of ~1.67 million annual air-pollution deaths in India is not disaggregated to these towns but is not disputed downward. `chronic`, not `latent`.
- **Agent — industrial-pollution.** Ambient community exposure produced by industry and its captive power, distinct from the occupational inhalation of the silicosis leaf (`industrial-exposure`).

## B · Evidence

- **The worst-air places are small industrial towns, not the metros — and the ranking is drawn from the measured minority.** IQAir's World Air Quality Report for 2025 (8th edition, published March 2026) puts **Loni (Ghaziabad district, UP) first in the world at 112.5 µg/m³ annual PM2.5**, up ~23% year on year while India's national average edged down from 50.6 to 48.9 µg/m³. Five of the world's ten most polluted cities are Indian — Loni, Byrnihat, Delhi, Ghaziabad and Ula — and Delhi ranks only third among them. In the prior report (for 2024, 7th edition), **Byrnihat was the single most polluted place on Earth at 128.2 µg/m³**; in the 2025 report it fell to 2nd or 3rd. 66 of the world's 100 most polluted cities are in India.
- **Loni's load is locally produced by informal industry and captive power.** Loni hosts on the order of 1,000+ small manufacturing units (garments, leather, plastics, e-waste, auto parts, metal) and sits on a Delhi–western-UP truck transit corridor; most units run diesel gensets against grid gaps (CNN, 2026). Ghaziabad district as a whole has ~27,000 industrial units, many on polluting DG sets, and the NGT has repeatedly directed the UP Pollution Control Board to act on generator emissions (Down To Earth, 2023). The pollution is generated inside the enforcement gap, not blown in.
- **Byrnihat is a cross-border airshed with a split regulator.** Byrnihat straddles the Assam–Meghalaya line. Meghalaya's own Chief Minister and its State Pollution Control Board publicly attribute the pollution to red-category industry (coke, ferro-alloys, cement) **on the Assam side — roughly 20 units, against 2–5 on the Meghalaya side** (Assam Tribune / Sentinel Assam, 2025). The regulator with jurisdiction over the exposed residents (MSPCB) has little authority over the emitters; the regulator over the emitters (PCB Assam) is not accountable to Byrnihat's residents. The Byrnihat Industrial Area (Assam) is a CPCB-designated Critically Polluted Area and a NCAP non-attainment town with a CPCB action plan on paper.
- **Measurement state — the visibility, not the pollution, is what is new; and the absence of measurement is the finding.** Per CSE's *State of India's Environment 2026*, India runs **562 real-time monitors across 294 cities and 966 manual stations across 419 cities and towns**, against roughly **7,900 census and statutory towns nationally** (Census 2011). **Only ~15% of the population (~200 million) lives within 10 km of a continuous monitor**; 64% of districts have none. Byrnihat got its first widely-reported real-time government monitor only in **December 2025** — *after* it had been ranked the most polluted town on Earth for 2024 on non-governmental sensor data. Small towns entering the rankings therefore reflect newly acquired *visibility* at least as much as newly acquired pollution, and the unmonitored tail — over a billion people — is unknown by construction.
- **Abatement money does not reach these towns' pollution.** Of ₹9,929 crore spent under NCAP and the 15th Finance Commission air-quality grants, **~68% went to road-dust management and under 1% to industry** (CREA, *Tracing the Hazy Air 2026*). The disbursing unit is a municipal corporation — it can buy sweepers, sprinklers and anti-smog guns but has no reach over informal-cluster combustion or captive diesel gensets, the sources actually loading Loni's and Byrnihat's air. **~40 of 130 NCAP cities have no completed source-apportionment study**, so their action plans and budgets are set without an account of what pollutes them; small industrial towns, late to get any monitor at all, are the sharp end of that gap. (Macro spend detail: [north India's winter smog](/leaf/north-india-winter-smog). National source split: the [air](/need/air) record.)
- **The same resolution failure operates inside cities.** Mumbai's 2023 winter briefly ran worse than Delhi, but the trend did not hold — CSE's winter 2024-25 analysis found Mumbai's peak daily PM2.5 at 81 µg/m³ (28 Dec 2024), a **44% fall** from the mean of the previous three winter peaks. What persists is ward-level (Deonar ~80 µg/m³ across the winter; Malad W spiking to 194 µg/m³), concealed by the city average — a city mean masks its wards the same way a national narrative masks Loni's 23% rise.
- **Magnitude / denominator.** Directly exposed residents of the named towns are order 10^6 (Loni ~0.5M, Ghaziabad ~1.7M, Byrnihat tens of thousands) and, with the belt of peer IGP industrial towns (Bhiwadi, Bahadurgarh, Bulandshahr, Hapur, Muzaffarnagar, Greater Noida, Faridabad, and unmeasured others), order 10^7 — hence `scale: 7`. The denominator that matters is the coverage ratio: **~419 towns instrumented (manual) / ~7,900 urban settlements ≈ 5%**, and **~15% of population within range of a continuous monitor**. The exposed count in the *unmonitored* tail cannot be given, and that is the result.
- **Differential vulnerability.** (a) *Hit hardest:* informal-sector workers and low-income residents co-located with the units — often the same households, since the workforce lives in the cluster; internal migrants; outdoor workers. (b) *Why they cannot exit or defend:* **no information** (no local monitor until 2025 in Byrnihat; no ward data in most towns), **no resources** (renters and informal workers cannot relocate out of the cluster and buy nothing to defend indoors), and **no standing** (no resident association; the affected are not a registered constituency, and in Byrnihat the emitters answer to a different state). This "no standing / no information" profile routes representation to a body that spans the airshed and is accountable to residents, and no such body exists.

Data note — Byrnihat's place in the 2025 IQAir report is given as 2nd by some sources and 3rd by others; write the range, not one rank.

Data note — the Meghalaya-side red-category unit count differs by source, which is why it is written as a range (2–5) and not a point figure; the Assam-side ~20 is consistent across sources.

Data note — a case exists for `scale: 8` if the unmonitored tail is read as the exposed population; kept at 7 on the narrower reading — residents of identifiable industrial towns — since the leaf is precisely about the tail being uncountable.

## C · Diagnosis

- **Mechanism — `authority-mismatched-to-harm` (primary).** Byrnihat is the textbook case: the harm unit is one airshed; the regulatory unit is two state pollution control boards, neither spanning it, with no statutory inter-state airshed mechanism. MSPCB issued closure notices to six Byrnihat (Meghalaya-side) factories in 2025-26 and Maithan Alloys announced shutdown of its Byrnihat unit — a blocker moved once, but only against the 2–5 units on MSPCB's own side; the ~20 Assam-side emitters are structurally out of its reach. Meghalaya has *proposed* a joint committee with Assam; it is not constituted.
- **Mechanism — `aggregation-masks-failure`.** The reporting unit (national average PM2.5, "India's air improved"; or a city-wide mean) is larger than the harm unit (the town, the ward). Loni rose 23% in the same year the national average fell; Mumbai's ward extremes vanish into its city figure. The improvement narrative is an artefact of averaging over a network that does not resolve the places doing the dying.
- **Mechanism — `instrument-keyed-to-wrong-object` + `spend-mismatched-to-source`.** Emission regulation attaches to objects that exclude this failure by design: consent-to-operate and the CEMS mandate cover 17 large "red-category" industry classes and permit-holding facilities; NCAP funds a list of named non-attainment cities; the monitoring network is built city-by-city. Informal sub-threshold units, captive gensets, and the ~95% of towns with no station fall outside every instrument. And the abatement money that does flow is keyed to the wrong fraction — ~68% of NCAP/15th-FC spend goes to visible coarse road dust (sweepers, sprinklers, smog guns), under 1% to industry, while the load on these towns is combustion PM2.5 from informal clusters and captive gensets. The PM10 city-average KPI rewards the dust action and is near-silent on combustion PM2.5. (Macro spend numbers and the `spend-mismatched-to-source` diagnosis: `north-india-winter-smog` §B/§C; both leaves inherit this from the retired `ncap-spend-misallocation` leaf, 2026-09-08.)
- **Primary vs derivative burden.** The visible, fundable activity is city-level dust suppression and emergency response; the load-bearing gap is (a) an airshed regulator that spans state lines and (b) enforcement capacity against a dispersed informal employer base. Neither is a procurement line item, so neither gets built.
- **Node — `energy` (sector node), via informal-industry fuel.** Two of the node's sub-levers produce this failure directly: *rural/industrial electrification and reliability-of-supply* (unreliable DISCOM supply is why 1,000+ Loni units and 27,000 Ghaziabad units run diesel gensets — captive combustion is a grid-quality failure) and *generation/fuel mix* (Byrnihat's coke, ferro-alloy and cement units are energy-intensive industry sited for cheap power and burning coal/coke/furnace oil). The node's PNG-conversion and grid-reliability levers (cf. Ghaziabad's PNG-genset transition order) would remove a measurable slice of a small town's PM2.5 without touching the informal-enforcement problem — which is why the node is a distinct lens.
- **Blocker.** No statutory instrument creates an airshed as a regulatory unit that can cross a state boundary; the Byrnihat joint committee is voluntary and unformed. Enforcement against informal clusters has no inspection capacity attached. And the monitoring build-out that would make the tail visible is itself underfunded (India ~465 stations short of its own 1,500 manual-station target; 28 NCAP cities still with no continuous station).

## D · Who is working on this

**Activism** — largely absent at the town unit, structurally so.
- No standing resident association was found for Loni or Byrnihat. Byrnihat produced one instance of spontaneous affected-led action — **Lower Balian residents locked an illegal ferro-alloy unit demanding it show valid clearances, February 2026** (Devdiscourse). Loni has documented resident anger at hazardous AQI but no named body. Delhi-NCR groups — **Warrior Moms**, Care for Air, Help Delhi Breathe, Right to Breathe India — are genuinely citizen/affected-led but NCR-airshed-focused; none runs a Loni or Byrnihat programme, and Byrnihat is outside their geography entirely.
- The one Byrnihat action was affected-led but ad hoc — no organisation, no continuity; it forced a compliance check on one unit and did not institutionalise. Activism density is concentrated in Delhi-NCR (multiple citizen groups, national media) and near-zero in the towns that top the ranking — density inverse to measured harm; the served population (educated NCR parents) is not the worst-exposed one. **No affected-led body for Loni or Byrnihat.** Structural: the exposed are informal workers and renters co-located with the units — among the least-organised populations in this tier — and in Byrnihat the emitters are in another state, so a resident body would have no regulator to petition.

**Institution** — present, but split and keyed to formal industry.
- **Central Pollution Control Board** (designated Byrnihat Industrial Area a Critically Polluted Area; runs NCAP; Byrnihat non-attainment action plan). **Meghalaya State Pollution Control Board** (jurisdiction over Byrnihat residents; issued closure notices to 6 Meghalaya-side factories, 2025-26). **Pollution Control Board Assam** (jurisdiction over the ~20 red-category emitters). **UP Pollution Control Board** (Loni/Ghaziabad; DG-set regulation under NGT direction). **National Green Tribunal** (directed UPPCB on diesel-generator emissions; oversees CPA compliance). **Conrad Sangma / Meghalaya government** (proposed an Assam–Meghalaya joint action committee; simultaneously disputed the IQAir ranking as "satisfactory").
- No body's authority contains the source *and* has the resident as its reporting unit: MSPCB's authority does not contain the Assam-side source; PCB Assam's reporting unit is not the Byrnihat resident; CPCB's NCAP unit is a named-city list; the monitoring unit is a station-equipped city. MSPCB acted post-ranking against its own side only (2–5 units) — a partial, one-sided enforcement that leaves ~80% of the emitting capacity untouched. Delhi-NCR has GRAP, CAQM, odd-even, an emergency playbook, a ₹25 cr smog tower; Loni — in the same NCR — and Byrnihat have an action plan PDF and, since Dec 2025, one monitor. Institutional density tracks political visibility, not the ranking. **No body constitutes the Byrnihat airshed as a regulable unit across the state line** — the CAQM's statutory airshed remit stops at the NCR boundary, and creating a cross-state airshed unit requires Parliament or an inter-state compact.

**Enterprise** — present adjacent and, on genset emissions, present via a mandate; absent at the harm unit.
- *Sensing and purifiers, adjacent:* **Respirer Living Sciences** (2,000+ low-cost monitors, ~700 across UP and Bihar — sells sensing to governments, platforms, researchers). **Ambee** (hyperlocal air-quality data as a service). Consumer air-purifier vendors (Airveda, Atlanta Healthcare, Nirvana Being, Atovio, Praan). **IQAir** — the Swiss firm whose report made these towns globally visible; sells monitors and purifiers, runs the public ranking. For sensing the paying customer is a government, corporate ESG buyer or platform partner; for purifiers, affluent urban households and corporate offices — not the Loni worker or the Byrnihat renter. Praan failed to raise in India and raised in the US; purifier penetration outside metro affluent segments is negligible.
- *Genset emission control — a mandate-made market, keyed to the formal segment.* **CPCB IV+ (July 2023)** requires certified retrofit emission control devices (RECDs) on in-use diesel gensets up to 800 kW, and a supplier industry formed against that mandate: **Chakr Innovation** (`chakr-innovation`; first CPCB type-approval, Chakr Shield, ~5,000 installs, FY25 revenue ₹189 cr, Series C $23m Sep 2025), **Sudhir Power**, **EO Energy**, **Saranya Swachh Vayu**. This is the CEMS pattern a second time — an emitter-funded compliance market for a countable, licensable set of facilities. But it reaches *permit-holding* genset owners (malls, hospitals, telecom, formal industry); the ~1,000 informal Loni units and ~27,000 Ghaziabad units run cheap, uncertified, uninspected sets outside the mandate's reach. So the enterprise leg on the genset sub-source is a **segment mismatch, not a void** — capable suppliers exist, their market stops at the formal boundary.
- **No enterprise whose customer is the small-town resident or the informal employer** — structural on that side: residents cannot pay, informal employers will not, and the RECD mandate that manufactured a customer did so only for inspectable facilities. Informal-cluster combustion (the larger PM source than gensets) has no compliance market at all.

**Representation present?** No, for the harm unit. Institutions exist but split across a state line or keyed to a city list; activism exists but in the wrong airshed (NCR) or as a single un-institutionalised event (Byrnihat). No actor spans the Byrnihat airshed and answers to its residents; none organises Loni's exposed population. The measurement actor (IQAir) is the only party that has reliably *perceived* the harm, and it cannot act on the source.

## E · Gap

`gap: representation`, `gap_missing_leg: [activism, enterprise]`, `gap_as_of: 2026-09-07`.

No affected-led body was found in either town. Byrnihat's February 2026 factory lock-in shows a latent claimant but no organisation, so there is nobody to submit and filling the slot is fieldwork, not a registry gap. **Activism is absent at the town unit and structurally so:** the exposed population has no organised voice. Constructing the claimant — a Byrnihat cross-state residents' body, and something equivalent in the Loni informal-cluster belt — is step one on the activism leg.

**Enterprise is a segment mismatch on the genset sub-source and absent on the rest:** the CPCB IV+ / RECD mandate (July 2023) did manufacture a genset-emission-control market — Chakr Innovation, Sudhir Power, EO Energy, Saranya — but keyed to permit-holding facilities, so the informal Loni/Ghaziabad genset base is outside it, and informal-cluster combustion (the larger PM source) has no compliance market at all. `enterprise` stays on `gap_missing_leg` for that reason — present at the wrong unit, like the institution leg — not because no capable supplier exists. The leg is filled for the informal segment only by extending the mandate's reach or subsidising retrofit into it. This is the general shape of the combustion-abatement gap folded in from the retired `ncap-spend-misallocation` leaf: **no enterprise sells combustion-PM2.5 abatement into the informal industrial sector at programme scale** — kiln retrofits, cluster fuel switching, small-industry emission control — because the payer would be a reluctant SPCB or an informal unit that will not pay, not a municipal corporation with a sweeper budget, and NCAP's design routes the money to the latter. The dust-suppression-equipment market that does exist (anti-smog guns, truck sprinklers, road sweepers — Cloudtec, Kapmix and peers, 600+ tenders in 2025) is viable *because* the spend is misallocated toward what it sells; it is a symptom, not coverage.

The institution leg is *present but split* — CPCB, MSPCB, PCB Assam, UPPCB and NGT all act on parts of this, but no body's jurisdiction spans the cross-border airshed or the informal cluster — so institution is a unit mismatch, not an absence, and is not listed as a missing leg.
