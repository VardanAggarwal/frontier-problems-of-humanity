# Post-monsoon paddy-straw burning in the north-west

## A · Classification

- **Channel — structural.** The harm follows from how the north-west cropping system is arranged — a near-monoculture paddy–wheat rotation locked in by assured MSP procurement and free/flat farm power, a transplanting date pushed later by the Punjab Preservation of Subsoil Water Act 2009, combine-harvested paddy that leaves tall anchored stubble, and a compressed turnaround to wheat — not from any single actor's act (`direct`) and not from a source with no system design (`ambient-accidental`; there is a designed cropping calendar behind it). No one farmer's burn is individually material; the arrangement is.
- **Satisfier relation — degraded-quality.** Breathable air is present across the airshed but is seasonally loaded above any defensible threshold — India's PM2.5 NAAQS is 40 µg/m³ (8× the WHO guideline) and Delhi-NCR runs multi-day "severe" episodes above 400–450 AQI through the burning weeks.
- **Onset — chronic.** A recurring annual multi-week exposure already in modelled mortality data (tens of thousands of PM2.5-attributable deaths per year), not a latent risk and not a one-off acute event, though each October–November episode is acute in intensity.
- **Agent — daily-life.** The emitting act is an agrarian practice, so `daily-life` is the honest proximate tag. It understates the cause — §C argues the practice is sustained by state policy (MSP procurement, free farm power, the 2009 transplanting-date law) and the compressed harvest-to-sowing calendar that law tightened — but that upstream structure is the `primary-vs-derivative-burden` mechanism, not an agent value.

## B · Evidence

- **Measurement state — the trend and the detection layer are entangled, and that is the finding.** The entire public series is **satellite-derived**. Polar-orbiting sensors (VIIRS, MODIS) pass over north India only in a **~10:30 a.m.–1:30 p.m. window**. Since 2022, **>90% of large Punjab farm fires are lit after 3 p.m.** (NASA Earth Observatory 2025; Outlook Business 2025), up from ~3% in 2021 — so the headline "90% reduction" cannot be cleanly separated from a shift in *when* farmers burn. Straw-tonnage and health figures are modelled, not measured. This is the third instance in the air need of a measured decline that is partly a detection artefact — cf. Mumbai's apparent 2023 worsening and small-industrial-town rankings; see `small-industrial-town-air` and `north-india-winter-smog`.
  - *Monitoring chain and the fix.* The operational series is run by **ICAR-IARI's CREAMS lab** (ingesting ~7 polar-orbiting sensors at IARI's ground station); state burn *alerts* — the trigger for the SDM → nodal-officer → challan pipeline — are issued by the **Punjab Remote Sensing Centre** and **Haryana Space Applications Centre**, and diverge often from ground verification (e.g. 6 of 23 confirmed in Malerkotla). The fix to the overpass gap is geostationary detection — **INSAT-3D/3DR** (ISRO/NRSC) and South Korea's **GEO-KOMPSAT-2A** can see the post-3 p.m. fires — under slow evaluation by ISRO and CAQM, not yet the basis of the published count.
- **Source population.** ~12.8 lakh farmers registered to sell paddy in Punjab and ~4–5 lakh in Haryana in KMS 2024-25 (Central Foodgrain Procurement Portal; PIB 2024) — **~1.7 million paddy-growing households** across the two states, ~68% smallholders (<2 ha); in Punjab only ~39% cultivate solely their own land. Paddy is grown on ~3.1M ha in Punjab (≈87% of the kharif area) and ~1.5M ha in Haryana. The one-line's "~2 million" is the right order of magnitude; ~1.7M is the tighter figure.
- **Straw burnt.** Punjab generates ~19–20 Mt of paddy straw a year, Haryana ~7 Mt. Older inventories (c. 2017–19) put the *burnt* fraction at ~21.3 Mt (Punjab) + ~9.2 Mt (Haryana) ≈ 30 Mt, ~140 Gg PM2.5; the burnt fraction has fallen since.
- **Fire-count series (satellite, IARI-CREAMS / NASA VIIRS-MODIS).** Punjab: **36,663 (2023) → 10,909 (2024) → 5,114 (2025 season, 15 Sep–30 Nov)** per PIB/CAQM; Haryana runs roughly a fifth to a third of Punjab's and fell in parallel. The government told Parliament (1 Dec 2025) that Punjab + Haryana together recorded a **~90% reduction "in 2025"**.
- **Downwind exposed population (`scale: 8`).** The plume covers the lower Indo-Gangetic Plain — Delhi-NCR, Haryana, west UP, north Rajasthan — several hundred million people. During the peak burning fortnight, upwind crop-residue burning has been estimated to contribute **~30–45% of Delhi's daily PM2.5 on the worst days** (Jethva et al. 2019; up to ~70% on single days across studies), against a much smaller share of Delhi's *annual* PM2.5 (~8–15%).
- **Health.** The most-cited modelled estimate is **~66,000 PM2.5-attributable deaths in India in a single year from crop-residue burning** (Chakrabarti et al. 2019 / GBD-MAPS synthesis); a wider band of **~44,000–98,000 premature deaths/year** appears across the literature. Sub-national attribution: rural crop-residue burning ≈ 32% of post-monsoon PM2.5 mortality in Delhi and ≈53% in Kanpur.
- **Differential vulnerability.** (a) *Hit harder:* downwind outdoor workers, elderly, children, people with respiratory or cardiac disease across the IGP; the urban and rural poor who cannot afford filtration or to stay indoors; rural populations near the fields, who take the highest acute doses. At source: the farm operator and family, who inhale the smoke of their own fields. This compounds on `north-india-winter-smog` — stubble burning is the seasonal spike on top of a chronically loaded airshed. (b) *Why they cannot exit or defend:* downwind, **no standing** — the emission source is 150–300 km away in another state, and until 2021 no authority had jurisdiction over both emitter and affected (what CAQM was created to fix); poorer households additionally have **no resources**. At source, the farmer **benefits from the cause in the immediate sense** — burning is free and takes hours, whereas a Happy Seeder pass costs ₹1,500–3,000/acre he does not recover, and the 10–20-day paddy-to-wheat window leaves no slack. Same "affected party is also the source" profile as the farm-power-tariff and ethanol-feedstock cases.

Data note — the "~90% reduction" headline is measured against a **2022** baseline, not 2024; against **2024** the Punjab drop is **~53%**.

Data note — straw tonnage is modelled (crop area × residue-to-grain ratio × burnt fraction), not weighed; the c. 2017–19 burnt-fraction inventories do not pair with the 2025 fire counts.

Data note — the peak-fortnight share of Delhi PM2.5 and the annual share are different quantities; do not quote one for the other.

Data note — the mortality figures are AOD-plus-exposure-response model outputs, not counted deaths.

Data note — Punjab imposed ~₹2.4 crore of environmental compensation on farmers in a recent season and recovered only a small fraction (Tribune); the recovered amount is reported ambiguously.

## C · Diagnosis

- **Mechanism — `solution-at-hand-blocked` (primary).** Both families of alternative exist and are technically settled: *in-situ* (Happy Seeder / Super Seeder direct-drilling, mulchers, the PUSA microbial bio-decomposer) and *ex-situ* (baling + offtake to biomass pellet plants, compressed biogas, 2G ethanol, co-firing). Punjab already holds enough CRM machinery — ~43,450 Super Seeders + ~13,560 Happy Seeders (CEEW 2024) — to cover 100% of its paddy area at capacity. It is not deployed at capacity because at farm-gate prices the farmer's cost–benefit does not clear (operating cost ₹1,500–3,000/acre; claimed input savings ~₹2,500/acre are contested and back-loaded; Happy Seeder needs a 50+ HP tractor many smallholders rent), and because the compressed window rewards the fastest clearance. Cheap, settled, not done — the block is economics + calendar rather than politics.
- **Mechanism — `primary-vs-derivative-burden` (primary).** The visible cause is the burn; the load-bearing causes are upstream: (1) the paddy acreage itself, sustained by assured MSP procurement + free/flat farm power that make paddy the only low-risk crop; (2) the **10–20-day window** between paddy harvest and wheat sowing, narrowed by the Punjab Preservation of Subsoil Water Act 2009 (which delayed transplanting to save groundwater and, as a side effect, pushed the harvest into late October); (3) combine harvesting, which leaves tall anchored stubble. Penalising the burn (FIRs, environmental compensation, red-entries) attacks the derivative. Crop diversification and a wider calendar attack the primary — and are not funded at a scale that changes the farmer's decision.
- **A `visible-win-strands-residual` dynamic, partial instance.** The sharp 2023→2025 fire-count fall (real reduction plus detection artefact) and the co-firing mandate's visible ex-situ offtake have already drained some of the political pressure that would fund the harder primary fix — the calendar and the acreage.
- **Node membership — `paddy-procurement-complex`.** This leaf is the first member of that sector node. The node's object — assured FCI procurement of rice in Punjab/Haryana plus the 2009 subsoil-water laws — is what fixes the acreage and the late harvest date; the compressed burn window is a direct output of the transplanting-date sub-lever.
- **Blocker.** Two, stacked. (1) **Farm-gate economics:** no instrument pays the farmer enough to make in-situ or bale-and-remove cheaper than a match; the ex-situ chains that work do so only because a *mandated third-party buyer* (thermal plant, OMC) exists, and their combined offtake is a fraction of ~30 Mt. (2) **The calendar:** the paddy-to-wheat turnaround is too short for incorporation at scale, and widening it means touching the transplanting-date law and the MSP basket — political objects no air authority controls.

## D · Who is working on this

**Activism** — present, affected-led, and organised against the penalty rather than the burn.
- *Source side:* Punjab/Haryana farmer unions — **BKU Ekta Ugrahan** (largest Punjab union; president Joginder Singh Ugrahan), BKU Kadian, and the broader **Kisan Mazdoor Morcha** / SKM-adjacent coalitions. They mobilise against the **penalty regime** — protesting FIRs, demanding withdrawal of "red-entry" revenue markings, asking for a **₹200/quintal bonus or free machinery** in exchange for not burning. `affected_led: yes`, at the **local-affected** unit — but their organised object is *resisting the fix's cost being placed on the farmer*, not ending the burn.
- *Downwind side:* **Warrior Moms**, **Care for Air**, **Help Delhi Breathe**, **Right to Breathe India** — affected-led (urban educated parents/residents) but at a **household/city unit** against an airshed + cross-state agricultural harm.
- Farmer unions **won the framing** (NGT/SC observations that farmers should not bear sole responsibility without support) and translated it into pressure that keeps penalty *recovery* near zero and periodically forces FIR withdrawals. They have **not** organised around adoption of alternatives, because the economics make that a demand for someone else's money. Downwind groups won litigation attention and GRAP tightening, not source control.
- **No activism actor works *for* the switch as the farmer's interest** — structurally, because the harmed-at-source benefit from the cause in the immediate term. Nobody of any leg represents the west-UP / north-Rajasthan rural downwind population, which takes high doses and has no organised voice.

**Institution** — the airshed authority exists; the detection-and-penalty half is built and does not collect; the offtake half is the one lever that moved real straw.

Three bodies hold the levers. **CAQM** (Commission for Air Quality Management, statutory, 2021) is the first body whose jurisdiction spans both the emitter states and the affected NCR. The **Ministry of Power**'s biomass co-firing policy (Nov 2025) is the instrument that moved real straw. **MoEFCC** (Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change) doubled environmental-compensation rates on 6 Nov 2024 — ₹5,000 / ₹10,000 / ₹30,000 by landholding band. The **in-situ economics** half — the CRM machine subsidy — is funded (~₹3,623 cr, 2.95 lakh machines) and **aimed at capex not opex**, so a documented share of machines sits idle. Crop diversification / calendar reform (the primary burden) is barely funded. No institution owns the **farm-gate residue economics**: CACP owns the MSP basket, the Punjab legislature owns the transplanting date, CAQM owns the air, and none owns the join.

The ex-situ routes divide by who manufactured the customer: biofuel/CBG/pellet offtake exists because a mandate created a buyer, while biochar is capped on the buyer side at only ~3,000 t/yr of demand across both states. The in-situ column has no buyer at all — the payer is CSR or a capex subsidy.

- *Whole-plant mechanical removal — machinery exists, no deploying actor.* The **reaper-binder** (tractor-mounted; cuts paddy at ground level and binds sheaves in one pass, near-total straw recovery, ~1 acre/hr) is the one machine that removes the entire plant rather than leaving anchored stubble for a cutter + baler to chase. Made in the Mansa cluster by **Sardar Industries** and **Thind Machinery Store** (plus BCS India, Mahindra) — but sold for seed/fodder crops and small holdings, not deployed at scale against paddy burning, because combines dominate the belt on speed and labour and the reaper-binder handles lodged/wet paddy and large fields poorly. This route has OEMs but no operator, no CHC network and no subsidy line.
- The paying customer for ex-situ is a **mandated buyer** — a thermal plant under the co-firing rule, an OMC under CBG/ethanol targets — never the farmer; VERBIO and PRESPL can pay a small farm-gate price *because* they have contracted downstream offtake. For in-situ the "customer" is the **subsidy** (80% for CHCs, 50% for individuals); absent it there is no sale, and the bio-decomposer has no revenue model at all. Punjab's earlier biomass power plants (10–15 IPPs) had a "chequered journey" — straw supply-chain cost, moisture, seasonality and storage fires made unit economics marginal; several ran below capacity or closed. **No farm-gate market for in-situ residue management** — structural: the farmer cannot/will not pay, and he has a free substitute. Ex-situ offtake exists but its combined capacity is a fraction of ~30 Mt/yr and is geographically clustered around a handful of plants.

**Representation present?** Yes, on all three legs, but with a defect on each. The airshed authority exists (CAQM). A working institutional lever exists (co-firing mandate) with enterprise actors behind it (VERBIO, PRESPL, IOC, Farm2Energy, A2P). The source population is organised (farmer unions) and the downwind population is organised (Warrior Moms et al.). What is missing is not a leg — it is the **join**: an actor or budget that closes the farm-gate economics of not-burning so the union's demand and the aggregator's offtake meet. Three routes are thin rather than defective: *biochar* has one enterprise (Takachar) plus a PepsiCo/PAU CSR project, all against ~3,000 t/yr demand; the *fungal/mycelium* route has one funded enterprise (Dharaksha) plus an edible-mushroom SHG demonstration; *whole-plant mechanical removal* has machinery (reaper-binder OEMs) but no operator deploying it against the burn.

## E · Gap

`gap: coverage`

**The empty slot is an enterprise, not an authority:** an operator that makes *not* burning cheaper than burning for a smallholder inside the 10–20-day window — an FPO-run straw-procurement operation, an ag-fintech underwriting the operating cost, a carbon-finance aggregator — is nameable but not yet catalogued.

**What stays broken regardless of the label:**
1. **Farm-gate economics** — no instrument makes not-burning the cheaper choice for a small farmer in the window.
2. **Scale** — co-firing + CBG + 2G ethanol together absorb only a fraction of the ~20 Mt of straw, and the plants are clustered.
3. **The upstream driver, untouched** — paddy acreage and the compressed calendar sit with CACP and the Punjab legislature (the `paddy-procurement-complex` node), outside any air authority's reach.
4. **Three disposal routes thin** — biochar (Takachar + a PepsiCo/PAU CSR project, against ~3,000 t/yr demand), fungal/mycelium (Dharaksha as a funded enterprise; an edible-mushroom SHG demo alongside), whole-plant mechanical removal (machinery exists; no operator deploys it against the burn).
