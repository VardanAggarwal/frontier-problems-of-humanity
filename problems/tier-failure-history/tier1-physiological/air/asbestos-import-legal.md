# Chrysotile asbestos still legally imported and used

## A · Classification

Chrysotile ("white") asbestos — a confirmed Group 1 human carcinogen causing asbestosis, lung cancer, mesothelioma, and laryngeal/ovarian cancer — is banned outright in roughly 68–70 countries. India banned only **mining** (no fresh leases from 1986; the last mines wound down by the mid-1990s) and the **import and use of crocidolite** (blue asbestos). Import, manufacture, sale and use of chrysotile remain fully legal. India is the world's largest importer and consumer, taking in on the order of **350,000–485,000 tonnes a year** across recent reporting years, ~85%+ of it fabricated into asbestos-cement (AC) roofing sheets and pipes explicitly marketed as an affordable building material for low-income households. The hazard is installed into poor housing stock as a development product, and the bill falls due decades later.

- **Channel — structural.** No single actor acts; the exposure is a property of how the trade and the informal building economy are arranged — legal fibre imports → AC-sheet manufacture (a countable set of ~15–20 plants and their workers) → roofing sold through the informal housing market → in-situ weathering, cutting, drilling, re-roofing and demolition by householders and unregistered construction labour, none of it under an exposure-control regime. The controls exist on paper (Supreme Court, *CERC v. Union of India*, 1995: membrane-filter monitoring, 40-year worker health records, compulsory insurance, decadal ILO-aligned permissible-exposure-limit review) and are defeated by the arrangement of the work.
- **Satisfier relation — violator.** Breathable air at the worksite and in the home carries respirable fibres (`degraded-quality` at the surface); the load-bearing structure is that a *shelter* satisfier — cheap AC roofing — is the air violator over 20–40 years, the within-tier loop named in the [tier-1 summary](/tier/1).
- **Onset — latent.** Mesothelioma latency is 20–40 years; asbestosis 10–30. India's consumption is peaking now, so the population-scale mortality wave arrives in the **2040s–2060s**, after the political window for prevention has closed. Isolated hospital-diagnosed cases already exist; a counted national toll does not. **No local affected party exists by construction** — §D routes to central activism, to institution, or to nobody.
- **Agent — industrial-exposure.** A chronic occupational inhalation hazard (AC-plant workers, construction and demolition labour, plumbers, re-roofers), not a discrete accident.

## B · Evidence

- **Consumption magnitude.** India's raw chrysotile fibre imports by year (Indian customs data compiled by IBAS and trade databases): ~379,043 t (2014); ~436,119 t (2021); ~403,292 t (2022); **~485,182 t of white asbestos in 2023** (~500,000 t counting all asbestos lines), the peak year; **~348,000–349,000 t in 2024**, a ~28% fall by weight (IndexBox reports the drop as −18% *by value*, to US$243M). The [air](/need/air) record already carries this as a **precedent disagreement**: figures differ by whether they count **chrysotile fibre only** or **all asbestos lines**, and by weight vs customs value. The reliable claim is **sustained scale of the order of a third to a half million tonnes a year for over a decade** — not a growth rate, not a single authoritative number, and whether the 2024 fall is real demand contraction or a reporting artefact is unresolved.
- **Source countries.** Historically ~85% from Russia; after the 2022 sanctions Russian tonnage roughly halved and Brazil and Kazakhstan took up the slack — Russia + Brazil + Kazakhstan ≈ 89% of 2024 imports.
- **End use.** The overwhelming majority is AC roofing sheet and pipe. India has ~15–20 AC-product plants (members of the Asbestos Cement Products Manufacturers Association, ACPMA); the product is sold as the cheapest durable roof, so the exposed household population is a large fraction of rural and peri-urban India plus the informal construction workforce that cuts and fits it. Exact denominators are not published — the exposed population is "millions, order of magnitude 7", not a surveyed count.
- **The finding: hundreds of thousands of tonnes, near-zero reported disease.** **India has never reported a single mesothelioma death to the WHO mortality database.** China, Russia and India are routinely *excluded* from global asbestos-mortality models for lack of any usable national data. The National Cancer Registry Programme covers ~**16% of the population** (ICMR-NCDIR). A hospital-based study (Public Health Action / The Union, 2024) found **2,213 mesothelioma cases from 83 hospitals from 2012 onward**; for 2012–2016 it recorded **1,126 hospital-reported cases against 54 in the National Cancer Registry** — a ~20× under-capture, and cancer is not nationally notifiable. There is no dedicated mesothelioma or asbestos-disease registry, no systematic occupational-disease surveillance, and few pathologists trained to diagnose asbestos-related disease.
- **Advocacy estimate:** Toxics Watch Alliance's June 2011 complaint to the National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) cited **~50,000 asbestos-related deaths a year** — a modelled/advocacy figure, contested and unverifiable against any surveillance system. Global reference points: WHO puts asbestos-related deaths above 200,000/year worldwide (~38,000 mesothelioma); Global Burden of Disease models ~30,000 mesothelioma/year. India's true figure sits in a range whose lower bound (official) is **zero** and whose upper bound (advocacy) is **~50,000** — and the width of that range is the finding, not a number to be picked. The UK still records ~2,500 asbestos-related deaths a year — the delayed bill for use that *peaked in the 1960s–70s and was banned in 1999*; India's exposure curve is 40+ years behind and still rising.
- **Differential vulnerability.** (a) *Hit hardest:* AC-plant production workers; unregistered construction, demolition and re-roofing labour; plumbers and electricians drilling AC sheet; and — distinctively for India — **household members** living under weathering AC roofs and **women and children** during in-home cutting/repair. Non-smokers are affected (asbestos causes lung cancer independent of smoking) — a partial explanation for India's non-smoker lung-cancer load. (b) *Why they cannot exit or defend:* **no information** (chrysotile is widely believed safe; latency means no felt symptom for decades and no attributable event at exposure), **no resources** (AC sheet is bought precisely because it is the cheapest roof), and **no standing** (informal workers, no employment record, no diagnosis, no notifiable-disease pathway). This "no information / no standing / harm imperceptible" profile routes representation to a **central organisation** performing attribution as a specialist function, exactly as the [tier-1 summary](/tier/1) assigns it.
- **Measurement state — the absence is the finding.** There is no exposure registry keyed to the person, no register of asbestos-laden buildings, no ambient fibre monitoring in Indian cities, and no functioning mandatory occupational-disease notification. The state imports and consumes asbestos at world-leading scale and operates **no instrument that would let the resulting disease be counted**. Absence of data is being read as absence of harm.

## C · Diagnosis

- **Mechanism — `solution-at-hand-blocked` (primary).** The fix is not to use it. Chrysotile is banned in ~68–70 countries; non-asbestos fibre-cement (cellulose/PVA), micro-concrete roofing tiles and galvanised/coated steel are established substitutes at comparable cost. The controls India has not adopted are settled ILO/WHO practice. As the [tier-1 summary](/tier/1) names this "the cleanest diagnosis in the tier" and routes it **activism → institution; capital cannot move it**.
- **Mechanism — `compensation-substitutes-for-counting` — in its degenerate form.** For silicosis the state built a functioning payout layer (Rajasthan, ₹550 cr+) and never built the detection layer. For asbestos **neither half was built**: no state asbestos-disease compensation scheme, no registry, no detection. The mechanism applies only as its "no counting" half — which shades into pure measurement-absence — but the political logic is identical: not counting the harm keeps liability invisible and protects the trade. Tagged for cross-leaf comparability with silicosis and lead under the node; §C flags that the payout half is absent.
- **Mechanism — `instrument-keyed-to-wrong-object`.** India's ban is keyed to **mining** — domestic extraction that had already all but ceased — and to **crocidolite**, the fibre India barely imports. The actual exposure pathway (chrysotile import → manufacture → use → demolition) is excluded from the prohibition **by design**, so the government can and does claim it has "regulated asbestos".
- **Authority fragmentation (noted, carried by the node's `authority-mismatched-to-harm` tag).** Import sits with DGFT / Ministry of Commerce; manufacture and clearance with MoEF&CC and state Pollution Control Boards (PCBs); workplace exposure with the Ministry of Labour / DGFASLI (Directorate General Factory Advice Service and Labour Institutes); disease surveillance with MoHFW / ICMR-NCDIR. No body's jurisdiction contains the exposed person, which is why the Supreme Court (1995) and the National Green Tribunal, NGT (2025) have each had to *assemble* a cross-ministry directive rather than point to an owner.
- **Primary vs derivative burden.** The visible, debated object is **occupational exposure in AC plants** (the industry's "controlled use" framing). The load-bearing burden is the **stock already installed** — millions of tonnes of chrysotile in roofs that will be cut, weathered and demolished over the next 40 years by people with no protection and no record — plus the tonnage still being added each year.
- **Node membership — `toxic-exposure-class`.** Asbestos is one of the node's three carriers (with lead and silica): long-latency toxic exposure with no person-keyed registry, banned or controlled elsewhere, reaching the poor as a construction/development input. AC roofing is the exact analogue of leaded paint and unmonitored quarry silica. The node's single prescribed intervention — a statutory exposure registry keyed to the person, plus a dated phase-out schedule aligned to banning jurisdictions — is precisely this leaf's two missing layers.
- **Blocker.** Two interlocking, both named actors: **(1) the domestic asbestos-cement manufacturing lobby** — the Asbestos Cement Products Manufacturers Association and member firms (Everest Industries, Visaka Industries, HIL / CK Birla, Ramco, UAL, Sarvottam), a ~₹5,000+ crore roofing-sheet industry that funds "controlled use" science and lobbies against any import restriction; **(2) India's standing veto at the Rotterdam Convention** — chrysotile has been recommended for Annex III (Prior Informed Consent trade-notification, *not* a ban) by the Chemical Review Committee since 2006 and blocked at every Conference of the Parties (COP) through April–May 2025 because listing requires consensus and a small producer/user bloc (Russia, Kazakhstan, India, Zimbabwe, Pakistan) refuses; India's formal position since February 2005 is that chrysotile is *not* a hazardous chemical. Real recent movement: the **NGT's 30 October 2025 order** (85 pages) endorsed MoEF&CC's stated "vision" to phase out **all** forms of asbestos and adopt alternatives, directed state Chief Secretaries and PCBs, reiterated the 1995 SC directions, and exposed the NHRC's 2016 closure as resting on a conflicted study — a directive on a stated intent, not yet a statutory phase-out.

## D · Who is working on this

**Activism** — present, central, at the correct unit, and unmoved on the core object for ~25 years.
- **Ban Asbestos Network of India (BANI)** — a people's alliance for an asbestos-free India, active since ~2000; co-founder and public face **Dr Gopal Krishna** (edits asbestosfreeindia.org, still posting November 2025). Convenes victims' groups, doctors, researcher-activists, trade unions. Not separately incorporated; volunteer / small-grant support. `affected_led: partial` — includes victims (via the victims’ association below) but researcher/advocate-convened.
- **India Asbestos Victims Association (IAVA)** — the victims' body, paired as "BANI-IAVA"; `affected_led: yes` but no verified independent channel, operates through BANI.
- **Toxics Watch Alliance (TWA)** — Gopal Krishna's research-and-litigation vehicle; **filed the June 2011 NHRC complaint** (61 recorded actions 2011–2016) that seeded the 2025 NGT proceeding.
- **Occupational & Environmental Health Network of India (OEHNI)** — a network of victims' groups, unions and OHS bodies (formed 2006); co-organised the December 2017 "India Beyond Asbestos" strategy meeting with Building and Wood Workers' International. Overlaps the silicosis actor set (PTRC / Jagdish Patel) — a bridge across the two `toxic-exposure-class` leaves.
- **Dr T. K. Joshi** — occupational-health physician (Centre for Occupational & Environmental Health, MAMC / Lok Nayak, Delhi), Collegium Ramazzini; long-standing scientific voice for a ban.
- **Building and Wood Workers' International (BWI)** and **Union Aid Abroad–APHEDA** — global construction-union federation and Australian union humanitarian body; back the Indian ban campaign, co-ran the 2017 meet.
- The representation is at the **central** unit, which is the *correct* unit for a 20–40 year latency harm no individual sufferer can attribute at exposure. **Won rulings, lost the object:** the 1995 SC directions were never operationalised; a 2011 MoEF phase-out statement of intent went nowhere; the 2011 TWA/NHRC complaint was **closed in August 2016** on the strength of a National Institute of Occupational Health (NIOH) "no significant health impact" study **co-funded by the asbestos industry (~₹16 lakh)** — a conflict the NGT called out nine years later. Kerala restricted government use (2009); Maharashtra issued a "no asbestos" line in regional plans (2018). Import remained legal throughout, and the activists have **never moved the import trade or India's Rotterdam position** — the campaign's defining stalemate. Density of activist effort is *low* relative to the exposure; a handful of individuals carry most of it — under-resourced, not over-represented.

**Institution** — cross-ministry directives with no owner; a surveillance body that does not surveil.
- **Supreme Court of India** — *CERC v. Union of India* (1995): the foundational directions, substantially unimplemented. **National Green Tribunal** — 30 October 2025 order endorsing MoEF&CC's phase-out vision, directing states/PCBs, reiterating the 1995 directions. **National Human Rights Commission** — held the 2011–2016 TWA complaint, closed it in 2016 on the conflicted NIOH study; criticised by the NGT in 2025. **MoEF&CC** — holds the stated (per NGT) intent to phase out all asbestos; has not legislated it; nominates the member to the Rotterdam Chemical Review Committee. **ICMR-NCDIR** (National Cancer Registry Programme, ~16% coverage) — the body whose reporting unit would have to become the mesothelioma / asbestos-disease case, and currently does not. **ICMR-NIOH** — did the industry-co-funded study the NHRC relied on; the institution that would otherwise be the natural home of an exposure registry. **DGFT (Directorate General of Foreign Trade) / Ministry of Commerce** — controls the import licence; the single administrative edit that would end fresh supply is moving chrysotile to the prohibited import list.
- No ministry's jurisdiction spans import + manufacture + use + disease; NCDIR's reporting unit covers 16% of people and does not code occupational cause; NIOH covers registered factories only. The courts have had to assemble authority twice (1995, 2025) precisely because no standing owner exists. There is *less* institutional machinery on asbestos than on silicosis (which at least has state pneumoconiosis boards) or lead — no board, no scheme, no registry.

**Enterprise** — absent on the remedy; present and organised *against* it.
- **Asbestos Cement Products Manufacturers Association (ACPMA)** and member firms — the domestic AC roofing-sheet and pipe industry. `stance: organised-against-remedy`. **International Chrysotile Association (ICA)** — the global producer lobby (Russian/Kazakh mining interests, Brazil), which produces the "controlled/safe use" science and the COP-by-COP Rotterdam lobbying material.
- The paying customer for AC roofing is rural and peri-urban households buying the cheapest durable roof, and government housing schemes that historically specified it — the demand is real and price-driven, the industry's strongest card. There is **no remedy-side enterprise** to have a capital graveyard, and the reason is sharper than "no one built one": **the substitute technology already sits inside the AC-sheet incumbents, who decline to position on it.** All four run a substitute alongside a chrysotile line they will not exit.

## E · Gap

**`none` here means representation is eliminated as the explanation — not that the problem is handled.** Campaigners have been present and correctly located since 2000 (central, because a latency harm gives no sufferer anything to attribute), have forced two apex rulings, and the trade is unchanged. The institution leg exists in degraded form — periodic court directives with no owner — which is a quality failure, not an absence; the NGT's October 2025 order is a live attempt to fix it. The enterprise leg is empty and **structurally so**: the remedy is a ban plus a person-keyed registry, and neither is a market, so no company is missing here — one would be misplaced.

With representation, technology and precedent all present, the 25-year non-ban attributes to a **named blocker**: the asbestos-cement lobby's domestic weight, plus the Commerce/Rotterdam veto that keeps fresh chrysotile flowing and keeps even a labelling obligation off the table.

The substitute technology is not the lever it looks like. The four largest fibre-cement makers (Ramco, Everest, Visaka, HIL/BirlaNu) each run a non-asbestos line *alongside* a chrysotile line they will not exit, and fund the lobby's blocking — hedged dual-producers who would net-lose on a phase-out. The one near-pure-play, `shera-india`, is a premium importer that never reaches the AC-roofing customer. So the catalyst move is not to construct a claimant, and not to ally the substitute industry; it is to connect the existing campaigners to whoever can move the import licence at DGFT / Ministry of Commerce.
