# Silica dust exposure and silicosis at national scale

## A · Classification

- **Channel — structural.** The harm follows from how informal quarrying, stone-cutting and agate work are arranged — piece-rate, dry cutting and drilling, no dust suppression, no enforcement — not from a single actor's act (that would be `direct`) and not from a source with no system design (`ambient-accidental`). The controls are designed and written into the Mines Act 1952 and Factories Act 1948; the arrangement of the work is what defeats them.
- **Satisfier relation — degraded-quality.** Breathable air is present at the worksite but carries respirable crystalline silica above any defensible threshold; India's own permissible exposure limit of 0.15 mg/m³ over 8 hours is three times the current US limit, so "compliant" air is still disease-causing.
- **Onset — chronic.** Chronic silicosis develops over 10–20 years of exposure; accelerated and acute forms run faster. It is squarely in mortality data — Rajasthan alone has certified 6,876 silicosis deaths — so this is `chronic`, not `latent`.
- **Agent — industrial-exposure.** A workplace inhalation hazard, chronic rather than a discrete accident.

## B · Evidence

- **Magnitude.** A Periodic Labour Force Survey analysis (The India Forum, 2024) identifying 79 National Industrial Classification sub-classes and five occupational groups with silica-exposure potential puts the exposed workforce at **more than 55.9 million in 2024 (3.99% of the population), up from ~29 million in 2018 (2.2%)** — most in construction, then mining, stone quarrying, agate polishing and slate-pencil work.
- Data note — the widely cited figure of "~52 million workers exposed to silica dust by 2025" is attributed to a 2009 *India Labour Report*, but the estimate **cannot be found in that report** (The India Forum, 2024). Treat its provenance as broken; the order of magnitude survives on the survey estimate above, the canonical citation does not.
- **Prevalence where surveyed.** Independent studies report silicosis prevalence of **30–55% across exposed industries**, against official reporting rates under 1%. Specific surveys: up to **79% in Rajasthan sandstone blocks**; **~69% in Gujarat's agate sector**; of ~8,000 stone-carving workers screened in Sirohi district, close to half certified silicotic. Ratio check: Rajasthan has **3 million+ mining workers** and has certified **~23,436 pneumoconiosis cases** (of which 6,876 deaths) since its portal went online — **under 1% detection**.
- **Differential vulnerability.** Hit hardest: informal-sector quarry and stone workers, agate grinders (including home-based women, exposing children), and internal migrants — populations that also carry India's highest tuberculosis burden, and silica-TB co-infection accelerates both. Widows of silicosis-dead miners re-enter the same mines because the household has no other income (The Wire Science).
- **Why they cannot exit or defend.** **No information** (early silicosis is symptomless; diagnosis conventionally waits for chest radiography at an advanced stage), **no resources** (piece-rate income, no savings to leave), and **no standing** (unregistered workers, contractor chains, employers outside any inspection regime). This is a "no standing / no information" profile — it routes representation to an organised body acting *for* the worker, not to the worker as claimant.
- **Measurement state — the harm is not counted, and the absence is the finding.** There is no national silicosis registry and no enforced mandatory notification of occupational disease, so the national denominator does not exist. State compensation portals (Rajasthan, Haryana, Gujarat, West Bengal, Madhya Pradesh) count only the cases that reach certification; the Directorate General of Mines Safety covers registered mines only; the Directorate General Factory Advice Service and Labour Institutes covers registered factories; informal quarries and downstream stone-cutting fall between them and into state labour departments that do not survey. District Mineral Foundation funds are channelled to certified patients but those foundations "do not engage in data collection or active monitoring" (IJME, 2025).

## C · Diagnosis

- **Mechanism — `compensation-substitutes-for-counting` (primary).** Rajasthan built the payout layer — 2019 Pneumoconiosis Policy, screening camps, district medical boards, an online grant-disbursement portal, **₹550 crore+ disbursed** — without a detection mandate obliging anyone to find more cases. The scheme pays reliably for the cases that present and has no mechanism to enlarge the search, so liability stays bounded by the size of the detection effort and the denominator stays unknown. It is the pattern this tier meets repeatedly — a payout layer built without the detection layer beneath it — and it has a commercial corollary: a compensation ceiling is not a market signal, which is *why* no detection industry formed (see *Who is working on this*, Enterprise).
- **Mechanism — `solution-at-hand-blocked`.** Silicosis is essentially 100% preventable with wet drilling/cutting, local exhaust ventilation and respirators — settled controls, cheap, already in the Mines and Factories Acts. Unlike asbestos it does not even require a ban; it requires enforcement of rules that already exist. Technically settled, economically cheap, not done.
- **Mechanism — `authority-mismatched-to-harm`.** The exposed workforce is spread across registered mines (the Directorate General of Mines Safety), registered factories (the Directorate General Factory Advice Service and Labour Institutes), informal quarries and stone yards (state labour departments, effectively no one), and construction (a fourth regime). No single body's jurisdiction contains the exposed population, so no body's reporting unit is the exposed person, and the Supreme Court had to invent a coordinating layer in 2024 because none existed.
- **Primary vs derivative burden.** The visible, funded activity is compensation-after-certification; the load-bearing gap is detection-before-disease. Effort routes to the certified minority because that is the countable, disbursable unit.
- **Node.** Downstream of `toxic-exposure-class` — silica is one of the three carriers (with asbestos and lead) of the same mechanism: long-latency toxic exposure with no person-keyed registry, controlled or banned elsewhere, reaching the poor as a work or construction input. The node's one intervention — a statutory exposure registry keyed to the person — is exactly this leaf's missing layer.
- **Blocker.** Enforcement against a dispersed, largely informal employer base with no inspection capacity attached, plus the absence of any obligation to screen the asymptomatic. The compensation schemes remove political pressure to build the detection layer by visibly "doing something".

## D · Who is working on this

**Activism** — present, affected-adjacent, local, and it has repeatedly won the rule and lost the execution.
- **PRASAR (Peoples Rights and Social Research Centre)**, Delhi — the writ petitioner in *PRASAR v. Union of India*, WP(C) 110/2006, decided **6 August 2024** (2024 INSC 582). Proxy representation, not affected-led: a research centre carried an 18-year petition on behalf of a workforce that could not litigate for itself, and won a national, structural remedy — a constitutional duty plus a multi-body accountability model. A petitioner has no delivery capacity, so execution passed to the bodies the judgment named.
- **MLPC (Mine Labour Protection Campaign Trust)**, Jodhpur — grew from an Oxfam India-funded network, registered under the Rajasthan Trust Act in 2007; founder Rana Sengupta (Ashoka Fellow). Supports **7,000+ silicosis-affected miners and widows** with oxygen banks, ambulances, telemedicine and green-livelihood substitution (goat farming, solar mills, mushroom cultivation), and organises quarry workers into ownership cooperatives. Helped shape and then police the Rajasthan 2019 Pneumoconiosis Policy. Worker-based but NGO-convened.
- **PTRC (People's Training & Research Centre)**, Vadodara — founded 1992 by **Jagdish Patel** (director; the reachable warm surface, active on LinkedIn; lead petitioner in the Gujarat High Court case) and **Vijay Kanhere** (Mumbai occupational-health specialist). Three decades on agate-sector silicosis in Khambhat/Shakarpur and now sandstone in Morbi/Surendranagar; runs a weekly silicosis clinic and a day-care centre for agate women's children; **located 94 new cases in the past year, 377 total (224 alive, 153 dead)**; backs the Khambhat **Silicosis Victims Association** in advocacy. Lead petitioner in *People's Training and Research Centre v State of Gujarat*, Gujarat High Court, **August 2025** — which imposed a ₹3 lakh minimum for living unorganised-sector patients, struck the 90-day claim deadline as "practically not feasible", and recognised widows and migrants. Worker-based but NGO-convened.
- **Khambhat Silicosis Victims Association** — the agate workers' own body, led by affected workers themselves, working through PTRC.
- Pattern: four rule-wins behind these two orgs (the Rajasthan 2019 policy, itself pushed by the state human rights commission acting on its own motion; the 2016 pleas to the National Human Rights Commission; the **August 2024 Supreme Court** Article 21 ruling; the August 2025 Gujarat HC order) and, on the ground, under-1% detection and — in Gujarat — 106 of 152 claims approved since 2017-18 with ₹1.6 crore disbursed and 17 rejected as time-barred. The pattern is won the law, lost the execution, in its cleanest form in this tier.

**Institution** — a compensation layer that works and a detection layer that does not exist; a central coordinating layer newly ordered and unproven.
- **Rajasthan State Human Rights Commission** — took suo motu cognizance of quarry-worker deaths and issued directions in the 2010s that are the documented push behind the 2019 Pneumoconiosis Policy; its **August 2022 Special Report on Silicosis** to the state assembly then records the non-implementation (screening still radiograph-gated, dust control unenforced). Recommendatory power only — moved the policy once, cannot compel its execution. `authority-mismatched-to-harm` in a second form: the body closest to the harm unit has the least enforcement power.
- **State compensation schemes.** Rajasthan (**Rajasthan Pneumoconiosis Board**) is the model — pension irrespective of income, a corpus funded from District Mineral Foundation money, portal disbursal, ₹550 crore+ paid, ~23,436 certified. Haryana, West Bengal, Madhya Pradesh and (court-forced) Gujarat run narrower versions. Authority contains the *payout* but not the *source*: none screens the asymptomatic, none enforces dust controls.
- **Supreme Court of India, *PRASAR v. Union of India* (2024 INSC 582, 6 August 2024)** — held failure to prevent silicosis a violation of Article 21 read with Articles 39(e) and 42, and built a layered accountability model: the **National Green Tribunal** to monitor silica-prone industries and environmental compliance; the **National Human Rights Commission** to oversee compensation delivery; the **Employees' State Insurance Corporation** to oversee timely compensation and rehabilitation; the **Central Pollution Control Board** on industrial inspection (carried from the 2017 orders); a **national coordination committee** for detection and management; and Chief-Secretary-level state oversight. First central institutional hook — but as of this leaf's date the committee is unconstituted and none of the named bodies has reported action, so the model exists on paper. Each layer also inherits the core mismatch: the tribunal and the pollution board reach permitted industry, the insurance corporation covers organised-sector rolls, and the exposure is in the informal economy.
- **ICMR-NIOH (National Institute of Occupational Health)**, Ahmedabad, **with ICMR-NIV** (Pune) — developed a **point-of-care finger-prick screening kit** reading the blood marker CC16 (lateral-flow, 100% sensitivity / 95% specificity, *Scientific Reports* 2021) for early detection before radiographic disease. A public research output — **no product line, no procurement, not deployed at scale**.
- The **mines-safety** and **factory-advice** directorates named above, plus state Factory Inspectorates — the two occupational-safety regimes; neither reaches informal quarrying, home-based agate grinding or stone-cutting yards, the factory-advice directorate is advisory rather than enforcing, and mandatory occupational-disease notification under both the Mines Act and the Factories Act is unenforced. This is the jurisdictional fragmentation behind `authority-mismatched-to-harm`.

**Enterprise** — absent, and structurally so.
- No company sells wet drilling, local exhaust ventilation or respirators into informal quarrying at scale. No diagnostics firm has commercialised that kit or any silicosis screen — there is no payer: quarry workers cannot pay, unorganised employers will not, and no insurer carries the occupational risk. The compensation ceiling caps liability at the certified count, which removes the demand signal a detection market would need.
- **Over-served comparison.** The densest adjacent market — consumer air purifiers, ~$507M (2024) heading to ~$1.8B (2033) — serves affluent urban households and corporate offices, i.e. close to the least silica-exposed population in the country. Problem specification followed ability to pay.

**Representation present?** Yes for the harmed *worker* — MLPC and PTRC are durable, decades-old, affected-adjacent bodies at the right (local) unit, and they have moved blockers repeatedly. What has no actor of any kind is the **detection layer**: the function that finds disease (or exposure) before certification. That is an enterprise/institution function, and it is empty.

## E · Gap

The gap is **representation** — but narrowly, and not on the activism leg. The worker is represented: MLPC and PTRC sit at the correct local unit, are affected-adjacent, and have won the rule four times over two decades. The missing unit is detection, and it is absent at every level — no enterprise actor at all, and only a paper institutional hook. This is not a *coverage* gap: the actors that would work it do not exist, so constructing the payer, or the mandate, is step one.
