# Residual childhood lead exposure (non-petrol sources)

## A · Classification

- **Channel — structural.** The exposure follows from how India's informal recycling economy and its unenforced consumer-product standards are arranged: an informal used-lead-acid-battery (ULAB) sector that outbids formal smelters for feedstock and melts batteries in homes; a paint rule (90 ppm, 2016) left to manufacturer self-certification; a spice supply chain in which lead-chromate adulteration is a routine colour/weight practice. The leaf genuinely spans channels — the turmeric-adulteration sub-route is deliberate at the adulterator's bench (`direct`), and cookware and paint come closest to `ambient-accidental` — but `structural` is the population-weighted centre of gravity and is consistent with §C's finding that no instrument is keyed to it.
- **Satisfier relation — degraded-quality.** The domestic environment (food, house paint, cooking vessels, neighbourhood soil) is present but carries lead above any defensible threshold. There is no safe blood-lead level; the 5 µg/dL (micrograms of lead per decilitre of blood) figure is an action trigger, not a floor.
- **Onset — chronic, not latent.** Paediatric lead neurotoxicity — IQ loss, attention and behavioural deficits — is contemporaneous with exposure and is *already* carried by the present cohort of children; the adult cardiovascular mortality is a delayed tail but is likewise already inside the modelled burden. Not asbestos, where the harm is incurred and not yet in mortality data. Ongoing exposure, ongoing accrual, already in the estimates → `chronic`.
- **Agent — daily-life.** The dominant routes are ordinary domestic consumption: turmeric in essentially every Indian kitchen, cooking vessels, house paint, cosmetics. `industrial-pollution` is the co-route (soil and dust around informal smelters and 700+ legacy contaminated sites Pure Earth has identified) but does not reach the majority of the 275M.

## B · Evidence

- **Magnitude, with its denominator.** **~275 million Indian children carry blood lead at or above 5 µg/dL** — the largest such population of any country — per *The Toxic Truth* (UNICEF & Pure Earth, July 2020). The estimate is **modelled by IHME from GBD 2019 data**, not measured. India's child population is on the order of 400 million, so the claim is that **well over half of Indian children** are above the action threshold.

Data note — the widely repeated "1 in 3 children" ratio is the **global** figure (up to 800 million of ~2.4 billion children). UNICEF published no India-specific ratio, and India's is materially higher than one-in-three; do not quote one-in-three for India.

- **CSIR / NITI Aayog validation (2022).** Pooled **89 datasets from 36 studies conducted 1970–2014** and concluded the *Toxic Truth* figures hold; **23 states exceed 5 µg/dL** mean childhood blood lead, and Bihar, UP, MP, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh and Andhra Pradesh — ~40% of the population — average **above 7 µg/dL**. The newest underlying data point is **2014**.
- **Sub-national measurement, where it exists, is worse than the model.** Pure Earth's 2023 blood-lead survey across 8 Bihar districts found **>90% of urban and >80% of rural children above 5 µg/dL**. The 2020 Patna source-apportionment study (Forsyth et al., *PLOS Global Public Health* 2022) identified **spices as the primary exposure source** in that cohort.
- **The sources left after petrol.**

- **Differential vulnerability.** (a) *Hit hardest:* children under 5 (developing brain, far higher gut lead absorption); children in Bihar/UP/eastern India where both blood-lead means and turmeric adulteration peak; children of informal ULAB workers, who carry lead home on skin, clothing and house dust; lead also compounds undernutrition (iron and calcium deficiency raise uptake). (b) *Why they cannot exit or defend:* **no information** — no routine blood-lead test, the harm is invisible at the individual level, and no parent can attribute a child's lower IQ to lead; **no resources** to substitute certified-safe spices, paint or cookware even where they exist; **no standing** — the exposed are a diffuse consumer public, not a workforce or a fenceline community with a body to join. This routes representation to a central actor constructing the claim, and it is why §D finds no affected-led organisation — one run by the people harmed — at all.
- **Measurement state — this is the finding.** India has **no national blood-lead surveillance programme**. The National Family Health Survey carries no blood-lead module. The most recent national synthesis (CSIR/NITI Aayog 2022) rests on data collected up to 2014. The 275-million figure has been modelled twice and **never measured at national scale**. Sub-national data exists only where a research group or Pure Earth went and looked. The denominator a programme would need — which children, in which districts, from which source — does not exist. Absence of measurement is not a gap in this research; it is the failure.

Data note — economic cost: CSIR/NITI Aayog (2022) put lead poisoning at ~5% of GDP (~$236 bn/yr); Pure Earth's 2023 fact sheet, citing Larsen & Sánchez-Triana (*Lancet Planetary Health* 2023), states India could save "over 10 lakh lives and 9% of GDP". Different base years — the 2023 paper adds an adult-cardiovascular-mortality channel the earlier modelling omitted. Write both.

Data note — death toll: ~232,000/yr (CSIR/NITI Aayog) vs ~1 million+/yr implied for India by the 2023 reanalysis once adult IHD deaths are attributed. Range, not a point.

Data note — the petrol "win" already contained the residual: mean childhood blood lead across five cities fell 18.1 → 12.1 µg/dL across the phase-out, and 12.1 is still more than double the 5 µg/dL action level. The US CDC lowered its reference value to 3.5 µg/dL in 2021, so the 5 µg/dL count is already conservative.

## C · Diagnosis

- **Mechanism — `authority-mismatched-to-harm` (primary).** No Indian body's jurisdiction contains the exposed population, and none has the child as its reporting unit: **FSSAI** (the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India) owns lead in food (turmeric limit 10 µg/g) with the *food sample* as its unit and episodic enforcement (the 2024 MDH/Everest export bans by Hong Kong and Singapore, not domestic surveillance, triggered the last visible round of testing); **CPCB / MoEFCC** own battery waste and contaminated-site remediation with the *facility* / *tonne of battery* as the unit and the informal smelting stream outside the permit system; **MoEFCC** owns the 2016 paint rule with the *product* as the unit and self-declared compliance; **AYUSH**, **BIS** and **MoHFW** (which would run surveillance but does not) each hold a fragment. "Childhood lead exposure" is nobody's administrative object.
- **Mechanism — `solution-at-hand-blocked`.** Every carrier has a settled, cheap fix that is not executed: FSSAI enforcement and pigment-supply-chain control on turmeric (**Bangladesh did exactly this and cut market adulteration from 47% to undetectable and population blood lead by a median 30% in 16 months** — Forsyth et al. 2019/2023); formalising ULAB collection and smelting under enforced EPR; third-party testing instead of self-certification for paint; a leaded-cookware standard. None requires a ban or new science.
- **Mechanism — `visible-win-strands-residual` (the pattern this leaf names).** The 2000 leaded-petrol phase-out was real and load-bearing at the time and was removed cleanly, across three environmental media. Removing the single largest source produced a large decline and then the effort stopped — the diffuse residual (spice adulteration, informal battery smelting, paint, cookware) was never taken up: "A win against the most visible source became a reason to stop looking."
- **`compensation-substitutes-for-counting` deliberately NOT tagged.** For asbestos and silica the state built a payout layer without a detection layer. For lead it built neither.
- **Node membership — `toxic-exposure-class`.** Lead is one of the class's three carriers (with asbestos and silica): long-latency toxic exposure, no person-keyed exposure registry in India, controlled or phased out in most other jurisdictions, reaching the poor as a development/consumer input. The node's proposed one intervention — a statutory exposure registry keyed to the person — is exactly this leaf's missing detection layer. Lead is the most fragmented of the three carriers because it is spread across food, waste, paint and cosmetics law.
- **Blocker.** (1) No constituency: the harm is invisible, delayed, individually unattributable, spread across the whole consuming public, so there is no group whose mobilisation would create political cost. (2) The petrol success is cited as the lead story being over. (3) Building surveillance creates a large measured liability (a number for "how many poisoned children") with no compensation mechanism behind it, so there is an active disincentive to measure. (4) The fixes sit with regulators (FSSAI, CPCB) whose performance is judged on facilities and samples, not on children's blood lead.

## D · Who is working on this

**Activism** — no affected-led organisation exists; the closest actors are proxy / technical NGOs.
- **Pure Earth** (US-registered, in India since 2006; country director **Diksha Radhakrishnan**) — the only body running sub-national blood-lead surveys (Bihar 2023; ongoing Tamil Nadu and Maharashtra), source-apportionment studies, contaminated-site remediation (Patna 2018, Vellore 2020), rapid market screening of consumer products, and it convened the industry consultation that fed the Battery Waste Management Rules 2022. Funded by USAID, Takeda, Trafigura Foundation, GiveWell.
- **Lead Exposure Elimination Project (LEEP)** — Charity-Entrepreneurship-incubated, GiveWell/Founders Pledge funded; works government-facing on paint-lead regulation across ~40 countries. An India programme is **not confirmed** (its 2024 review cites an unnamed ">200 million residents" programme country).
- **Toxics Link** (Delhi, est. 1996; associate director **Satish Sinha**) — the recurring domestic source of paint-lead testing (2018–2023).
- **Stanford lead-turmeric research group** — Jenna Forsyth, Stephen Luby, Dinsha Mistree — produced the evidence base linking turmeric adulteration to India's blood-lead burden; runs supply-chain intervention research, not organising.
- All proxy. None is led by, or accountable to, parents of lead-poisoned children — because no such organised group exists. The 2016 paint rule was won and its execution lost to self-certification; the turmeric evidence is a decade old and has produced episodic FSSAI testing but no standing programme; the Battery Waste Management Rules 2022 were won on paper and the informal stream is untouched. "Won the rule, lost the delivery" — without even the affected-led body that, in the silicosis case, kept policing it. Nothing is over-served here; the adjacent over-served market is again consumer air and water purifiers.

**Institution** — one-report-and-done.
- **FSSAI** (lead-in-food standards, sporadic spice testing); **CPCB / MoEFCC** (Battery Waste Management Rules 2022; 2016 paint rule; contaminated-site oversight); **NITI Aayog + CSIR** (the 2022 assessment); **MoHFW** (named as a Pure Earth partner on state surveys, runs no national surveillance); **Indian Academy of Pediatrics** (professional body, episodic advisories). None's authority contains the source and none reports in children-with-elevated-blood-lead. The CSIR/NITI Aayog 2022 report **recommended state and national lead action plans and a surveillance system** and was not followed by a programme or a budget line — "the second half never built" in its clearest form: the government's own think tank published the diagnosis and the response layer was never constructed. The detection layer — routine blood-lead screening of children, district-level source apportionment — has no institutional owner.

**Enterprise** — present on the wrong side of the exposure.
- **Formal lead recyclers** — Gravita India (listed), and captive plants of battery makers (Exide, Amara Raja) — are viable businesses; **certified-safe spice / food-testing labs** serve exporters. No company sells blood-lead testing to Indian families at scale. For formal recyclers the paying customer is the lead value chain; for lead-free turmeric it is the **export regulator** (Hong Kong, Singapore, US FDA import alerts moved MDH and Everest in 2024, not domestic demand). In both cases the payer is not the exposed child or their household. Formal recyclers are **feedstock-starved** because informal collectors outbid them, and no deposit-refund or enforced-EPR scheme has closed that gap. A domestic blood-lead diagnostics market has not formed at all: the exposed cannot pay, no insurer carries the risk, and there is no compensation scheme whose payout would create demand for a test.
- **The George Foundation** ran Project Lead-Free and the National Referral Centre for Lead Poisoning in India (~1999–2002) — the blood-lead measurement effort that helped drive the petrol phase-out — and has been effectively **dormant on lead** since. Its withdrawal from the issue *is* "the effort stopped" made concrete.

**Representation present?** No. Activism is proxy-only and paint-skewed; the one actor doing anything that *perceives* the harm at population scale (Pure Earth, via sub-national blood-lead surveys) is a foreign-funded NGO with no enforcement power and no mandate. Institutions hold fragments of the source and none holds the child. The detection layer is absent at every leg.

## E · Gap

The harmed population — Indian children with elevated blood lead — has **no actor at a unit that can both perceive and act on the harm**. Activism exists only as foreign-funded proxy NGOs (Pure Earth, LEEP, Toxics Link) and an academic group, none affected-led, weighted toward paint rather than the larger spice and ULAB routes. Enterprise is absent domestically for want of a payer, exactly as in the silicosis leaf. Institutions hold carrier-fragments (FSSAI / CPCB / MoEFCC) but none reports in the harm unit, and the one body that diagnosed the problem at national scale — CSIR / NITI Aayog, 2022 — recommended a surveillance-and-action programme that was never built.

Institution is listed as fragmented-present rather than missing: the legal hooks exist per carrier and a national programme has been formally recommended, so the institutional task is *assembly*. An affected-led activism body and a domestic detection market do not exist at all and would have to be created — hence both legs on `gap_missing_leg`. This is not `coverage` (the actors working adjacent to this are real and known, simply proxy and thin) and not `none` (no one has moved the blocker on the residual sources in 25 years, and the one clean win is precisely what removed the pressure). It maps to "absent at every unit → representation; constructing a claimant is step one": the first move is a blood-lead surveillance programme whose district numbers would manufacture the missing constituency.
