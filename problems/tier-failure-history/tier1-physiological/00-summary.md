# Tier 1 — Summary

Six needs researched independently (`01`–`06`). This file is not a digest of those files; it is what survived them. Read this to decide, read the six to verify.

---

## 1. The state of the tier, in one line

**Tier 1 in India is not failing for lack of a known solution. It is failing at the point where a known solution meets an identifiable interest that benefits from the failure continuing.**

Every one of the six needs produced that same finding independently. That consistency is the single most important result of the tier, and it is what the rest of this file is organised around.

---

## 2. The shape of the failure

Three claims that hold across all six files, and none of which are obvious from the death tolls.

**The toll is chronic, not catastrophic.** Famine, cholera epidemic, exposure death — the acute forms that built the institutions — have largely been beaten. What remains is a chronic band: malnutrition rather than famine, ambient PM2.5 rather than a smog event, sleep loss rather than exposure death. This band is larger than the acute one ever was, and it kills slowly enough that no single death is attributable to it. Chronic failure produces no event, and institutions respond to events.

**The failure sorts by class, caste and identity. Not one of the six is randomly distributed.** Not a single failure mode found in tier 1 lands on the population at large. Air pollution loads onto informal industrial workers; heat onto tin-roof dwellings with 8% AC penetration; sanitation onto one caste by assignment; sleep onto gig and night-shift labour; displacement onto Adivasis (40%) and Dalits (20%). The consequence is structural, not rhetorical: **tier-1 failure in India is not a resource-availability problem, it is an allocation problem** — which means the causes sit in tiers 3–4 (caste, class, power) and a fix confined to tier 1 will keep getting overwritten from above.

**Absence of measurement is read as absence of harm.** Recurred in four files independently — no mesothelioma registry, no aquifer storage accounting, houseless count frozen at Census 2011, no national sleep survey. Reinforced by 15% of the population living within 10 km of an air monitor, and 714 of 766 districts *self-declaring* freedom from manual scavenging. This is the tier's most consistent finding and its cheapest intervention point, because a harm with no number cannot accumulate a constituency, and producing the number is work a small team can actually do.

## 3. Seven mechanisms that produced every failure

Not themes — mechanisms, each found in three or more files. This is the transferable content of the tier.

| Mechanism | What it does | Clearest instance |
|---|---|---|
| **Aggregation masks failure** | The unit of reporting is larger than the unit of harm, so the average is compliant while the parts are not | National groundwater 60.6% "safe" vs Punjab >100% extraction; Mumbai city AQI mean vs Malad W at 194 |
| **Spend mismatched to source** | The response is funded and executed, against the wrong cause | 68% of ₹9,929 cr clean-air spend on road dust; mortality is combustion PM2.5 |
| **Instrument keyed to the wrong object** | The remedy attaches to a thing adjacent to the harm, so a class of victims is excluded by design, not by failure | Displacement compensation keyed to *land title*; the harm is to *shelter*, so the landless get nothing |
| **Authority mismatched to harm** | The body with power does not contain the source, or *is* the source | Byrnihat's airshed spans a state line; government agencies hold >30% of encroached water-body area |
| **Primary vs derivative burden** | The visible cause is not the load-bearing one, so effort routes to the minority of the problem | 55% of acute food insecurity is conflict-driven, but chronic diet failure is larger and independent |
| **Solution at hand, blocked** | Technically settled, economically cheap, politically stuck | Asbestos: the fix is not using it; ~68 countries have full bans, India bans only mining (1986) |
| **Compensation substitutes for counting** | The state builds the payout layer without the detection layer, so liability stays bounded by the size of the search | Rajasthan silicosis: 22,000+ certified and ₹250 cr paid, against 3M+ mining workers — under 1% detection |

The seventh is the sharpened form of the measurement finding in §2 and the more useful one, because it names an *action* the state took rather than an absence. It recurs three times: silicosis (pays reliably, searches barely), manual scavenging (Supreme Court compensation mandated since 1993, 714 of 766 districts self-declaring themselves free), asbestos (no registry at all). Paying for a harm is politically cheaper than measuring it, and it caps the bill by leaving the denominator unknown.

Two secondary patterns worth carrying: **within-tier loops** (providing shelter degrades thermoregulation via tin/asbestos roofs, and construction degrades air and climate at 37% of global emissions), and **the second half never built** (collection exists, treatment does not — 61% official vs ~28% independent sewage treatment, so pathogens are relocated rather than removed).

### The toxic-exposure class

Asbestos, lead and silica are written across four files as three unrelated entries. They are one mechanism with three carriers, and the repetition is the finding:

| | Latency | Registry | Status elsewhere | How it reaches the poor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Asbestos** | 20–40 yrs | None | Banned in ~68 countries | Cement roofing sold as affordable housing |
| **Lead** | Lifetime, cognitive | None | Petrol/paint controlled globally | Spice adulteration, backyard battery smelting, old paint |
| **Silica** | 10–20 yrs | Detection <1% | Controlled by enforced workplace limits | Quarrying, stone cutting, construction |

Each is already solved technically, banned or controlled elsewhere, invisible in official statistics because nobody counts, and **installed into poor households as a development product**. Three independent instances of one mechanism is a stronger frontier signal than any single row of the shortlist below, because it says the failure is structural to how India handles slow toxicology rather than specific to one material.

### When a mandate constitutes a market

Added after the commercial-landscape pass across all six files. This is **not an eighth mechanism** and does not belong in the table above, because the seven describe how a *failure* is produced and this describes how a *remedy industry* is produced. It is kept separate for that reason, and it is the most predictive thing the tier contains.

Six instances, each a compliance obligation that called an industry into existence where none had reason to exist:

| Obligation | Industry it constituted |
|---|---|
| CPCB Red-category consent / **ZLD** | Ion Exchange, Thermax, VA Tech Wabag, L&T, Gradiant, Veolia |
| **CEMS**, 17 industry categories (2014), CSIR-NPL certifying from 2019 | certified continuous-monitoring instrument sector |
| **EPR, plastics** | Banyan Nation (~₹100 cr revenue, **80% EPR-driven**), Lucro; compliance market **$1.5B (2025) → $5B (2030)** |
| **Biomedical Waste Rules** | 200+ CBWTFs; **$2.32B (2024) → $3.53B (2030)** |
| **7% biomass co-firing** within 300 km of Delhi (FY25–26) | Punjab pelletisation, 16 plants operating with 21 more expected |
| **₹66.17/litre guaranteed ethanol procurement** | Praj Industries built ~65% of India's ethanol plants |

And one clean negative control in the same tier: **CGWA groundwater-extraction permits exist and manufactured nothing** — no metering-as-a-service, no compliance-audit sector, nothing.

**The discriminator appears to be countability.** ZLD, CEMS and biomedical waste all attach to a finite set of licensable, inspectable, permit-dependent facilities; extraction is diffuse across millions of tubewells, so there is no inspectable object for a compliance industry to serve. The corollary for the shortlist below is direct: where a harm has a countable set of responsible facilities and no obligation attached, **writing the obligation is the whole intervention** — the industry follows on a base rate this table gives you rather than on hope.

**Two existing mechanisms extend once the commercial layer is visible.**

- **Mechanism 7 gains a commercial corollary.** Rajasthan's silicosis scheme does not only cap liability — the cap is *why no detection market formed*. A compensation ceiling is not a market signal, so compensation substituting for counting also suppresses the industry that would do the counting. The absence of a blood-lead testing market against 275 million exposed children is the same shape.
- **Mechanism 3 is not a government phenomenon.** *Instrument keyed to the wrong object* now has as many instances in consumer markets as in state schemes: mattresses sold to households whose sleep interference is already low, air conditioning at ~8% penetration, purifiers to the least-exposed, OTC painkillers for a disability nobody asks about. The mechanism describes any remedy that attaches adjacent to the harm — the state has no monopoly on aiming badly.

## 4. The shortlist

Filtered on the criterion that emerged during the tier: **solution known, blocker identifiable and movable, harm under-measured.** Ordered by that combination, not by toll.

| | Solution known | Blocker | Irreversible | Measured | Toll |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lead** | Yes — enforcement, not invention | Informal battery recycling; spice trade | **Yes, cognitive** | **No surveillance** | 275M children, ~4 IQ points |
| **Asbestos** | Yes — don't use it | Import trade; ~68 countries precedent | Yes, per-person | **No registry** | Latent, arrives 2040s–60s |
| **Silicosis** | Yes — wet drilling, ventilation | Enforcement; detection under 1% | Yes, per-person | Compensated, not counted | ~52M exposed |
| **Agricultural night power** | Yes — feeder separation, done in 2003 | Discom finance only | No | Barely | Sub-lethal, very wide |
| **Heat at work** | Yes — work-rest rules, enforced in Gulf | No enforcement in informal sector | No | Modelled, not measured | 247bn labour-hrs (2024) |
| **Manual scavenging** | Yes — mechanise + caste exit | Caste; self-declared compliance | Per-person | Contested 3× over | Acute, lethal |
| **Cool roofs / heat** | Yes — 6–13°C demonstrated | **Landlord–tenant split incentive** (corrected — see below) | No | Partial | Rising with climate |
| **Air source-targeting** | Yes — target combustion, not dust | Closing a cluster creates a named loser | No (decade) | Sparse | ~1.67M/yr |
| **Ethanol feedstock** | Partly — feedstock rules, water conditions on the target | Sugar lobby; farmer incomes; energy-security framing | No | **Boundary-disputed, not unmeasured** | Provisioned forward to E27 |
| **Slope-failure zoning** | Yes — bind the hazard maps that already exist to construction approval | Quarrying and construction interests; Western Ghats ESA redrafted 6× and never notified | **Yes, per-person** | Hazard mapped well, toll unreconciled | Wayanad: 373 dead, 352 houses |
| **Data-centre water** | Yes — siting rules, closed-loop cooling, disclosure | IT/ITeS classification bypasses EIA; investment politics | No, if acted on now | **No disclosure requirement** | Latent, 6× by 2030 |
| **Groundwater** | Partly — cropping shift | Free farm power; MSP; 89% of draw is agriculture | **Yes** | Aggregated away | Slow, compounding |
| **Sewage treatment** | Yes, completely | Capex + O&M, no ribbon to cut | No | Disputed 2× | Chronic, national |
| **Chronic food quality** | Yes — Sen 1981 settled the theory | Distribution politics | No | Good (NFHS) | Largest in tier |
| **AMR** | **No** | — | **Yes** | Improving | Rising |
| **Faecal sludge management** | Yes, completely | **No obligation exists** — plastic got EPR, septage got nothing | No | ULB-level, patchy | Upstream of the tier's largest disease burden |
| **Fleet fatigue-risk management** | Yes — flight-time-style duty limits, standard in aviation and mining | No priced liability for a tired driver, so no operator buys it | No | Telematics collected, fatigue not interpreted | Gig + logistics, tens of millions |
| **Rental discrimination** | Partly — the remedy is a prohibition, not a technology | **No legal instrument at all**, therefore no compliance market | No | Documented, not enumerated | Caste, religion, gender; urban migrants |

**Three reads.**

- The top rows share one shape: cheap known fix, single identifiable interest holding the blocker, harm unmeasured. That combination is the tier's best target class, and the first three rows are one problem (see the toxic-exposure class above).
- **AMR is the only genuine knowledge frontier in tier 1** — and the only entry requiring a lab.
- The irreversible column — lead, asbestos, silicosis, groundwater, displacement, slope failure, AMR — is where leverage concentrates and attention does not.
- **Slope-failure zoning is the row where the measurement finding inverts.** Every other entry is blocked partly by not knowing: no registry, no surveillance, no disclosure, aggregated away. Here the detection layer is built, published and good — GSI's 4.3 lakh km² susceptibility map, ISRO's 80,000-slide atlas — and the harm continues because nothing binds a construction approval to it. It is the tier's proof that measurement is necessary and not sufficient, and a caution against reading "absence of measurement is a finding" as "measurement is the fix."

## 4b. The same shortlist, scored for capital rather than policy

The table above ranks on political movability. That answers a question this project is not asking: **the working purpose is deciding where to deploy money and effort, not what to advocate.** Scored on payer identity instead, the ordering changes substantially — and the mechanisms invert, because *solution at hand, blocked* means the blocker is political by construction and therefore not movable by capital, while *instrument keyed to the wrong object* means an unserved object exists and can be served directly.

| | Who owns the object | Is there a payer | Existing commercial density |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Data-centre water** | Hyperscale operators — countable, licensable | **Yes, well-capitalised** | Sensing exists (FluxGen), sold to industry already |
| **Faecal sludge management** | ULBs and informal desludgers | **No obligation, so no payer** | Near-zero — one demonstration FSTP, one policy alliance |
| **Fleet fatigue management** | Logistics operators and platforms | **Yes, if liability were priced** | Telematics dense (FleetX, Intangles); fatigue framing absent |
| **Cool roofs** | Landlord, not occupant | Split — payer and beneficiary differ | Vendors exist, sell to warehouses not households |
| **Silicosis / lead / asbestos** | Informal employers; importers | **No** — and compensation ceiling suppresses the detection market | Zero at scale; screening kit is a research output |
| **Sewage treatment** | Municipality on a central grant | State only | Real (VA Tech Wabag), entirely tender-fed |
| **Groundwater** | Millions of tubewell owners | No — diffuse, uncountable | Drip vendors, subsidy-dependent (Jain distressed) |
| **AMR** | Prescribers, pharmacies, manufacturers | **Worse than none** — every party earns more from more | Zero; stewardship is grant-funded |
| **Rental discrimination** | Landlords | No instrument, so no payer | Zero |

**What the rescoring says.**

- **The top three rows of §4 fall to the bottom here.** Lead, asbestos and silicosis are the tier's best policy targets and its worst capital targets: no payer, and in silicosis's case an active compensation ceiling that removes the reason a detection industry would form. This is not a contradiction — it is the two questions giving different answers, which is the point of running both.
- **Data-centre water rises to the top.** Countable operators, capital on hand, an existing sensing layer, and a single missing instrument (disclosure of aquifer draw). By the base rate in §3, an obligation here constitutes a market quickly. It is also the only latent entry where the harm has not yet been incurred.
- **AMR is worse than "the harmed cannot pay."** No actor was found in India whose revenue *falls* when antibiotic use falls. Manufacturers, pharmacies and diagnostic vendors all earn more from more prescribing. Every party with capital is aligned against the remedy — a distinct and harder category than an absent payer, and it belongs on the record as such.
- **Cool roofs was mis-specified in §4 and is corrected above.** The blocker is not capex on poor households; it is that **the landlord buys the roof and the tenant gets the cooling**, with no subsidy or mandate bridging the two. That reclassifies it from an affordability problem to a business-model problem — the third path described in `../../../CLAUDE.md`, where the work is making a non-paying beneficiary payable rather than inventing or legislating anything.
- **Fleet fatigue management is the tier's clearest greenfield.** The data a fatigue product needs is already being collected under AIS-140 telematics and sold on a harsh-braking and asset-protection framing. Nobody sells the interpretation, because no Indian operator carries priced liability for a tired driver. It is the only entry in tier 1 where **the party imposing the harm would be the buyer**.

### Where writing an obligation is the whole intervention

The most directly actionable output of the commercial pass. Each of these has a countable set of responsible parties, a technically settled remedy, and no obligation attached — the exact configuration that produced six industries in §3:

- **Aquifer-draw disclosure for data centres.** Countable operators, existing sensing vendors.
- **Faecal sludge management.** The plastic/septage comparison is decisive: EPR created a $1.5B compliance market for a smaller harm; septage has a larger one and no instrument.
- **Blood-lead screening**, against 275 million exposed children, and **silicosis detection**, against ~52 million exposed workers with under 1% found. Both require unbundling detection from the compensation scheme that currently caps it.
- **A cool-roof provision in building or rental code**, which converts a split incentive into an obligation on the party that owns the roof.

The caution attached to all four: an obligation only constitutes a market where the responsible parties are countable and inspectable. CGWA extraction permits are the standing proof that writing one against a diffuse population produces nothing.

## 4c. Cross-need nodes

Three policy objects each produce failures in more than one tier-1 need. This matters more for choosing work than the need-by-need list, because a single intervention at a node propagates across needs while a need-level fix does not.

**Farm power tariffs and scheduling → water + sleep.** Free and subsidised tube-well electricity drives groundwater over-extraction (`02`); the same supply being scheduled *at night*, because non-agricultural users hold daytime priority, forces night irrigation and destroys farmers' sleep (`06`). One instrument, two needs — and one fix. Gujarat's Jyotirgram Yojana separated agricultural from domestic feeders in 2003–06 for ~US$290 million, cut groundwater withdrawal ~37%, halved farm power subsidies, and gave tubewell owners eight daytime hours on a pre-announced schedule. **The circadian benefit was an uncosted byproduct nobody claimed.** Solar agricultural feeders extend the same design. This is the highest-multiplier lever identified in the tier.

**Construction → air + water + shelter + sleep + food.** Sand mining degrades rivers and aquifer recharge; dust and silica load the air; asbestos-cement roofing and lead paint enter the housing stock; displacement destroys shelter; heat islands and night construction noise degrade sleep; land conversion takes cropland and lakes. Construction is 37% of global emissions and India is the second-largest cement producer. No single regulator holds this object, which is why it appears in five files as five problems.

**Ethanol blending targets → water + food.** The third node, and the only one found after this summary was first written. The E20 target was met in 2025 and E27 is scheduled for 2030. On water (`02`) it commissions a demand class into Maharashtra, UP and Karnataka, where sugarcane already takes ~70% of state irrigation demand off ~6% of cultivable area, and adds 8–15 litres of spent wash per litre produced. On food (`01`) it routes ~12.7 Mt of FCI rice and ~7 Mt of maize a year into fuel, turning India into a net maize importer in 2024 and moving maize from ~₹15,000 to ~₹25,000/tonne against a feed sector that takes 60–70% of the crop. One instrument set — the EBP target schedule, the FCI allocation SOP, and MSP procurement — held by MoPNG, DFPD and FCI.

Three properties make it the most instructive node in the tier. **It was created on purpose**, on a published schedule, which is the opposite of every other entry here: this is not a failure that accumulated while nobody watched but one being provisioned forward, so the counterfactual is unusually clean. **It runs against another arm of the same state** — ethanol gives paddy and maize a second guaranteed buyer while the millet and pulse push tries to move land the other way. And **its central quantity is contested by system boundary rather than by measurement**: the water cost of a litre of ethanol is quoted at 3–5 litres, ~2,469, or ~10,790 depending on whether you count the distillery, the crop, or the rainfall, with each figure advanced by a party it favours and no agency publishing the blue-water number that would settle it. That is a distinct failure mode from the tier's usual measurement absence, and worth naming as its own: **not unmeasured, but measured to three different boundaries by interested parties.** Expect it wherever a harm is quantifiable in principle and the accounting frame is unregulated.

### Climate change: cross-cutting, but not a node

Climate appears in five of the six files and was not carried into this summary on the first pass. That was an omission, and correcting it requires saying why it happened, because the reason is structural rather than an oversight of attention.

A **node** as defined above is a *policy object* — an instrument some identifiable body controls, so that one intervention on it propagates across needs. Farm power and construction qualify. Climate does not: it is a **threat multiplier** operating on the substrate itself, and no body inside India holds it. It therefore needs its own category rather than a third row in the table above.

The routes, one per file:

| Need | Climate route | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Air** | Heat accelerates photochemical ozone formation; fire seasons lengthen, so 20th-century regulatory gains are partly given back | `03:76` |
| **Water** | HKH glaciers projected to lose up to 80% of volume by 2100; flood-drought whiplash against infrastructure sized for a stabler rainfall regime; GLOFs | `02:92,135,140` |
| **Food** | March 2022 heat cut wheat yields 10–15% (forecast 110 → 99 MMT); elevated CO2 reduces grain zinc ~9–10%, iron ~5–8%, protein ~6–8% (Myers et al., 2014) | `01:122,126` |
| **Shelter** | Heat load rising against 8% AC penetration and roofs that run 4–6°C hotter indoors | `04:112` |
| **Sleep** | Above 25°C outdoor night temperature the share sleeping under seven hours rises ~3.5%; +1°C ≈ three extra insufficient nights per 100 people per month (Minor et al., 2022) | `06:200` |

Four things follow that no single file states.

- **Climate is the only tier-1 threat that is simultaneously a cause and a consequence of another tier-1 failure.** Combustion PM2.5 kills directly and warms; construction is 37% of global emissions and degrades four other needs; irrigation pumping to compensate for erratic rainfall draws the aquifer that erratic rainfall no longer recharges. These are loops, not chains, and every other entry in this tier is a chain.
- **It inverts the tier's central finding.** §1 says tier 1 fails where a known solution meets an interest that benefits from the failure — an *implementation* frontier with an identifiable, movable blocker. Climate has the known solution and the identified blocker, but the blocker is neither national nor movable by anyone operating at the scale this project can operate at. It is the one entry where "solution at hand, blocker identifiable and movable" splits: the first two hold, the third does not.
- **The remedy class it attracts is the visible one.** Tree planting and smog towers are to climate and air what road dust is to PM2.5 — see the vegetation section in `03-air.md` under *Where this fails today*. Global land sink absorption fell to near zero in 2023 and 2024 while restoration was being promoted hardest, and the whole global tree-restoration potential is worth roughly ten years of emissions, once. Adaptation entries in the shortlist (cool roofs, work-rest rules, feeder separation) are the ones that survive this test; mitigation-by-vegetation is not.
- **Which is why it is absent from §4 rather than at the top of it.** The shortlist filters on *actionable by a small team in India*. Climate fails that filter while being the largest force in the tier — and the honest form of that statement is that the shortlist is a list of things one can work on, not a ranking of what matters. The adaptation rows in §4 are the tier's tractable share of this problem.

### Overcrowding: cross-need, and owned by nobody

A third category, distinct from both of the above. Household density is not a policy object anyone controls (so not a node) and not an external force acting on the substrate (so not a multiplier). It is a **shared condition produced by failures in three needs at once and owned by none of them.**

The route runs through TB, where India carries 25% of the global burden. Decomposition of NFHS-4 and NFHS-5 attributes **~10% and 8.2% of the wealth-based disparity in TB prevalence to overcrowding**, with house type a further 8.9% and 9.7%; among the poorest quintile the population-attributable fractions are **34.2% for low BMI and 28.5% for indoor air pollution**. Those three inputs sit in three different files — undernutrition in `01`, household solid fuel in `03`, the room itself in `04:116` — and TB control is run as a fourth programme, on drugs and diagnostics, with no housing determinant entering any target.

Two consequences worth carrying:

- **The component that is falling is the one being measured.** TB incidence fell 21% from 2015 to 2024, faster than the global rate, on detection and treatment. The environmental determinants were not part of that effort and there is no evidence they moved. A programme can post a real, verifiable win while the substrate producing the disease is untouched — the leaded-petrol pattern in §6, arriving early rather than after the win.
- **Density has no denominator.** No slum enumeration since Census 2011 (65.5 million), no published persons-per-room distribution for urban India, and NSS's ~46 m² mean dwelling area averages a one-room slum unit together with a bungalow. Every other measurement absence in this tier is of a *harm*; this is an absence of the *exposure*, which is worse, because it means no intervention on it could be evaluated even if one were attempted.

Expect this category — cross-need, no owner, no denominator — to be more common in tier 2 than in tier 1, since safety failures are less physically localised than physiological ones.

**Method note:** these nodes were only visible after all six files existed. Neither is discoverable from within a single need. Expect the same to be true across tiers — a node that appears here in two needs may appear in tier 2 or 3 as well, and the register of nodes should be maintained at the tier-set level rather than per tier.

## 5. The latent set

Three bills already incurred, none yet visible in mortality data. They score highest on irreversibility and lowest on political salience, which is the worst available combination and the one where early work is worth most.

- **Asbestos** — import of chrysotile remains legal; mesothelioma latency is 20–40 years; no surveillance, so the arrival will be invisible until it isn't.
- **Lead** — the harm is already in the children. 275 million with elevated blood lead, ~4 IQ points lost on average, cognitive damage that does not reverse. Unlike the others this bill is not arriving later; it has been paid and the country has not noticed, because the loss is distributed as slightly lower attainment across a generation rather than as identifiable cases.
- **Silicosis** — ~52 million exposed, 10–20 year latency, detection under 1% of the exposed workforce.
- **Seismic exposure** — ~60% of the landmass, unreinforced masonry stock, IS 4326/13828 unenforced. The shelter analogue of asbestos latency.
- **Aquifer depletion** — the only tier-1 failure with a genuinely depletable stock rather than a flow.
- **Data-centre water demand** — the one latent entry where the harm has *not* yet been incurred. 1.5 GW of capacity now, 8–10 GW by 2030, ~75% of it in water-stressed regions, ~80% of cooling water evaporated, and no agency requiring disclosure of aquifer draw. Every other item in this section is a bill already run up and awaiting presentation; this one is still being run up, which makes it the only place in tier 1 where prevention rather than mitigation is still available. That is a stronger reason to work on it than its current size.

## 6. What worked

An earlier draft of this file claimed Odisha was the tier's only positive control. That was wrong, and wrong in a way that changed the conclusion — it made the tier read as near-uniformly bleak when it isn't. Every file now carries a `### Where it worked` section. The India-specific set:

| Win | Measured change | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| **Odisha cyclone shelters** (1999–2018) | Fatalities per million affected **779.3 → 3.55** | Tier-1 mortality is a state-capacity variable, not an income variable |
| **Leaded petrol phase-out** (completed 2000) | Child blood lead **18.1 → 12.1 µg/dL**; urban air Pb 1.6 → 0.2 µg/m³ | A single substitution, measurable across air, water and blood |
| **Bihar PDS reform** | Leakage **68.7% → 19.2%** | Sen's distributional failure responds to plumbing |
| **Jyotirgram Yojana** (2003–06) | Groundwater withdrawal **−37%**; subsidies halved; daytime farm power | One instrument can fix two needs at once |
| **Polio-free certification** (2014) | ~50% of world cases (2009) → zero | Indian state capacity executes at national scale on an unambiguous target |
| **TB incidence** (2015–2024) | **−21%**, faster than global | Partial, and worth stating precisely — burden is still the world's largest |
| **Salt iodisation** | >90% household coverage | Universal carriers beat behaviour change |

**Three non-state wins, found only once the commercial pass was run.** Every row above is a government programme, which is the same institutional-source bias that shaped the rest of this tier. The organisations that actually reach the bottom quintile are these:

| Win | Reach | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| **Amul / GCMMF** | ~80–100M farmer families; **~80% of the consumer rupee returned to producers** against 35–40% in Western dairy | Producer-owned cooperative |
| **Sulabh** | **~15M users/day at ₹1–2 per use**, ~₹550M annual revenue, cross-subsidised | Nonprofit / social enterprise, municipal land |
| **SEWA parametric heat cover** (Ahmedabad, 2023) | 21,000 women across seven informal trades; payout at two consecutive days ≥43.72°C | Philanthropically backed insurance pilot |

**The pattern is ownership structure, not technology.** Two of the three are cooperative or nonprofit and the third is philanthropically underwritten. None is venture-financed, and none of the dense venture-financed categories found in the six files — snacking D2C, purifiers, co-living, mattresses, water purification — reaches this population at all. That is worth holding against the assumption that "the harmed cannot pay": Amul demonstrates that the binding constraint is often **who captures the margin**, not whether one exists. SEWA demonstrates that an exposed population can be made the beneficiary of a priced instrument. Both are small relative to the need — 21,000 against 400–500 million informal workers — and both are existence proofs rather than solutions.

Two things follow. First, the shortlist in §4 should be read as **neglected, not intractable** — the mechanism of change in every row above is administrative rather than technological. Second, the most instructive win is the leaded-petrol one, because of how it failed afterwards: removing the largest single source produced a large measured decline, and then the effort stopped. The residual sources were never touched, and 275 million children still carry elevated lead. **A win against the visible source became the reason to stop looking.** That pattern is worth watching for anywhere a headline number has improved.

## 7. What this tier does not contain

Stated explicitly, because the absences are findings.

- **No knowledge frontier except AMR.** Seventeen of the eighteen shortlist entries are implementation problems. The question left open in an earlier draft — whether that is true of the world or an artifact of the method — is now answered, and the answer is **mostly artifact, partly real**. Artifact, because four method choices each pushed the same way: the institutional-source preference selects state-generated evidence, so a harm no agency measures is invisible and a harm an agency measures arrives pre-framed as that agency's failure; the seven mechanisms are all mechanisms of *institutional* failure, with none for "nobody built it" or "the unit economics don't close"; the unit of analysis is a need at national scale, whose only plausible owner is the state; and the criterion itself selects for political blockers, since technical blockers are rare at population scale. Real, because tier 1 is the tier where provision genuinely is state-shaped — pipes, sewers, buffer stocks, feeder separation. **If that second reading holds, the conclusion for a capital allocator is that tier 1 is a poor hunting ground and tiers 2–5 are likely better** — a result, not a failure. Hold both.
- **No entry where the payer and the beneficiary are the same person outside the top quintile**, with three exceptions, all in §6 and none venture-financed. Across six files, every dense commercial category serves either an urban discretionary consumer, a farmer buying inputs on subsidy, a facility under a compliance mandate, or a government tender. Not one serves the exposed population as a paying customer. This is the tier's most consistent commercial finding and the reason the §4b ranking differs so sharply from §4.
- **No problem solvable from inside tier 1.** The causes route upward into caste, class and power. Any intervention that stays within the tier is treating a symptom of a tier-3/4 structure.
- **No propagation need — scoped out deliberately, not overlooked.** `../../tier-taxonomy.md` derives the hierarchy from *persist/propagate*, and all six tier-1 needs are persist. Obstetric survival (MMR 88/100k, ~22,500 maternal deaths a year, led by haemorrhage and compounded by the rising anaemia in `01-food.md`) is physiological and tier-1 in character, but the failure mode is **absence of care at the moment of risk** rather than absence of a substrate — closer to `tier2-safety/05-health-safety-net.md` than to food, water or air. Filed there. The distinction to preserve: birth is not the threat; the inability to survive a known and time-bounded medical event is.
- **No entry whose blocker is a security guarantee.** Manipur is in `04:104` — 58,821 displaced on government data, 281 relief camps still open in 2026, a ₹523 crore rehabilitation package, ~3,000 prefabricated houses built, and people who cannot go back because their villages are unsafe and their houses are held by armed groups. It is absent from §4 for the same reason climate is: solution known, blocker identified, blocker not movable by the instrument at hand or by a small team. It is worth naming as a *category* rather than a row, because the state's response to it — routing conflict rehabilitation through PMAY-G, a rural ownership-housing scheme — is the tier's most literal instance of building the thing you know how to build when the missing input is something else entirely.
- **No problem the researcher personally faces.** Tier 1 is met for anyone in a position to read this. The food loop closed because it could be run on oneself; none of these ten can be. Whatever gets picked here needs a substitute mechanism for short distance to the source of truth — the property flagged in `../../../notebook.md:22` as mattering more than the problem itself.

- **Distinguish nodes from multipliers in every later tier.** Tier 1 conflated them by having only nodes in view. A node is a policy object someone controls (farm power, construction); a multiplier is a force acting on the substrate that no one in the jurisdiction controls (climate). Both cross needs; only the first is a place to intervene. A third category appeared late — the **shared condition with no owner** (overcrowding), produced by several needs jointly and controlled by none, which is neither a node nor a multiplier and is the hardest of the three to act on because it has no denominator. Expect tier 2 to have at least one multiplier of its own — the candidate to watch is demographic and employment structure — and to have more shared conditions than tier 1 did.
