# Tier 1 — Summary

Six needs researched independently (`01`–`06`). This file is not a digest of those files; it is what survived them. Read this to decide, read the six to verify.

**Rewritten 2026-08-24** under the three-instrument frame (activism / political institution-building / social enterprise) replacing the single capital-allocation screen the first version was built on. The change was not cosmetic: the earlier file carried two rival shortlists — one ranked on political movability, one on payer identity — and declared the second to be the real question. Under three instruments neither is the question on its own, and a third gate that neither table contained turns out to reorder both. What survived the rewrite unchanged is marked where it matters.

---

## 1. The state of the tier, in one line

**Tier 1 in India is not failing for lack of a known solution. It is failing at the point where a known solution meets an identifiable interest that benefits from the failure continuing.**

Every one of the six needs produced that finding independently, and it survives the rewrite. What changes is what follows from it. Under a capital screen, "an interest benefits from the failure continuing" was a reason to walk away — money cannot move an entrenched interest. Under three instruments it is the routing rule: an entrenched interest with a known technical remedy is precisely the object activism and institution-building exist to move. The same sentence, the opposite conclusion.

The rewrite adds a second finding the old file could not see, because it needed a gate neither of its tables had:

**In tier 1, the presence of an organised affected constituency and the presence of a payer are anti-correlated.** The three rows the capital screen demoted to the bottom — silicosis, manual scavenging, cool roofs — are exactly the rows with real, decades-old, affected-led organisations behind them. The row it promoted to the top, data-centre water, has no affected party at all, because the harm has not yet landed. This is not two rankings to be averaged. It is a structural property of the tier, and it is the reason a single-instrument screen was always going to mis-sort it.

## 2. The shape of the failure

Four claims that hold across all six files, none of which is obvious from the death tolls.

**The toll is chronic, not catastrophic.** Famine, cholera epidemic, exposure death — the acute forms that built the institutions — have largely been beaten. What remains is a chronic band: malnutrition rather than famine, ambient PM2.5 rather than a smog event, sleep loss rather than exposure death. This band is larger than the acute one ever was, and it kills slowly enough that no single death is attributable to it. Chronic failure produces no event, and institutions respond to events.

**The failure sorts by class, caste and identity. Not one of the six is randomly distributed.** Not a single failure mode found in tier 1 lands on the population at large. Air pollution loads onto informal industrial workers; heat onto tin-roof dwellings with 8% AC penetration; sanitation onto one caste by assignment; sleep onto gig and night-shift labour; displacement onto Adivasis (40%) and Dalits (20%). The consequence is structural, not rhetorical: **tier-1 failure in India is not a resource-availability problem, it is an allocation problem** — which means the causes sit in tiers 3–4 (caste, class, power) and a fix confined to tier 1 will keep getting overwritten from above.

**The harm is measured badly or not at all, and the absence is systematic rather than incidental.** It recurred in four of six files: no lead surveillance against 275 million exposed children, no asbestos registry, under 1% silicosis detection against ~52 million exposed, no persons-per-room distribution for urban India, no slum enumeration since Census 2011. Absence of measurement is a finding, not a gap in the research.

**Every failure has a bearer; almost none has a claimant.** New in this version, and the sharpest thing the leader gate produced. The harmed party is identifiable in all six needs — that is claim two. But across the eighteen shortlist rows in §5, only four carry a durable organisation of the harmed themselves. Three more have a constituency that is organised *against* the remedy. The rest have professional advocacy, or nothing. A harm with a bearer and no claimant is the tier's default state, and it is why so much of tier 1 reads as neglected rather than contested.

## 3. Mechanisms

Not themes — mechanisms, each found in three or more files. This is the transferable content of the tier. The instrument column is new: it names which of the three legs can actually move each mechanism, and it is the operational half of the rewrite.

| Mechanism | What it does | Clearest instance | Instrument |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aggregation masks failure** | The unit of reporting is larger than the unit of harm, so the average is compliant while the parts are not | National groundwater 60.6% "safe" vs Punjab >100% extraction; Mumbai city AQI mean vs Malad W at 194 | Institution (the reporting unit needs an owner); enterprise if the harmed can pay |
| **Spend mismatched to source** | The response is funded and executed, against the wrong cause | 68% of ₹9,929 cr clean-air spend on road dust; mortality is combustion PM2.5 | Institution (redirect the spend) or enterprise (serve the real source) |
| **Instrument keyed to the wrong object** | The remedy attaches to a thing adjacent to the harm, so a class of victims is excluded by design, not by failure | Displacement compensation keyed to *land title*; the harm is to *shelter*, so the landless get nothing | **Enterprise, directly** — an unserved object exists |
| **Authority mismatched to harm** | The body with power does not contain the source, or *is* the source | Byrnihat's airshed spans a state line; government agencies hold >30% of encroached water-body area | **Institution-building first** — the object needs an owner before either other leg has anything to attach to |
| **Primary vs derivative burden** | The visible cause is not the load-bearing one, so effort routes to the minority of the problem | 55% of acute food insecurity is conflict-driven, but chronic diet failure is larger and independent | Institution or enterprise |
| **Solution at hand, blocked** | Technically settled, economically cheap, politically stuck | Asbestos: the fix is not using it; ~68 countries have full bans, India bans only mining (1986) | **Activism → institution.** Political by construction; capital cannot move it |
| **Compensation substitutes for counting** | The state builds the payout layer without the detection layer, so liability stays bounded by the size of the search | Rajasthan silicosis: 22,000+ certified and ₹250 cr paid, against 3M+ mining workers — under 1% detection | Enterprise where a buyer exists; activism where the missing denominator is the contested thing |

The seventh is the sharpened form of the measurement finding in §2 and the more useful one, because it names an *action* the state took rather than an absence. It recurs three times: silicosis (pays reliably, searches barely), manual scavenging (Supreme Court compensation mandated since 1993, 714 of 766 districts self-declaring themselves free), asbestos (no registry at all). Paying for a harm is politically cheaper than measuring it, and it caps the bill by leaving the denominator unknown.

**The instrument column reverses the old ranking, and that reversal is the point.** *Solution at hand, blocked* ranked last on the capital screen and is the only mechanism that satisfies the frontier criterion — solution at hand, blocker identifiable and movable — by definition. Both readings were correct under their own instrument count. The error was the instrument count, not the criterion.

Two secondary patterns worth carrying: **within-tier loops** (providing shelter degrades thermoregulation via tin/asbestos roofs, and construction degrades air and climate at 37% of global emissions), and **the second half never built** (collection exists, treatment does not — 61% official vs ~28% independent sewage treatment, so pathogens are relocated rather than removed).

### The toxic-exposure class

Asbestos, lead and silica are written across four files as three unrelated entries. They are one mechanism with three carriers, and the repetition is the finding:

| | Latency | Registry | Status elsewhere | How it reaches the poor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Asbestos** | 20–40 yrs | None | Banned in ~68 countries | Cement roofing sold as affordable housing |
| **Lead** | Lifetime, cognitive | None | Petrol/paint controlled globally | Spice adulteration, backyard battery smelting, old paint |
| **Silica** | 10–20 yrs | Detection <1% | Controlled by enforced workplace limits | Quarrying, stone cutting, construction |

Each is already solved technically, banned or controlled elsewhere, invisible in official statistics because nobody counts, and **installed into poor households as a development product**. Three independent instances of one mechanism is a stronger frontier signal than any single row of the shortlist, because it says the failure is structural to how India handles slow toxicology rather than specific to one material.

### Instrument keyed to the wrong object: the movement is not exempt

Mechanism 3 was originally read as a state phenomenon, then extended in the commercial pass to consumer markets — mattresses sold to households whose sleep interference is already low, air conditioning at ~8% penetration, purifiers to the least-exposed, OTC painkillers for a disability nobody asks about. The leader gate adds a third domain: **the same mechanism operates on advocacy.**

Warrior Moms — 1,500+ members across 13 states, founded by five Delhi mothers — is genuinely affected-led, and it represents urban middle-class parents rather than the informal industrial workers who carry the load at Byrnihat and Malad W. The claimant is adjacent to the harm in exactly the way a compensation instrument keyed to land title is adjacent to a harm against shelter. This is a *unit mismatch* rather than a failure of legitimacy (see §4), and naming it that way matters: the fix is a central representation unit for an airshed-scale harm, not a criticism of the mothers.

The mechanism describes any remedy that attaches adjacent to the harm. The state has no monopoly on aiming badly, and neither does capital.

### When a mandate constitutes a market

**Reframed in this version.** Under the capital screen this table was a base rate for market entry. Under three instruments it is something more useful: **the empirically-grounded handoff from leg 2 to leg 3.** Institution-building writes an obligation; enterprise fills the market the obligation creates. Six instances against one clean negative control is the only quantified inter-leg sequence the tier contains.

It is **not an eighth mechanism** and does not belong in the table above, because the seven describe how a *failure* is produced and this describes how a *remedy industry* is produced.

| Obligation | Industry it constituted |
|---|---|
| CPCB Red-category consent / **ZLD** | Ion Exchange, Thermax, VA Tech Wabag, L&T, Gradiant, Veolia |
| **CEMS**, 17 industry categories (2014), CSIR-NPL certifying from 2019 | certified continuous-monitoring instrument sector |
| **EPR, plastics** | Banyan Nation (~₹100 cr revenue, **80% EPR-driven**), Lucro; compliance market **$1.5B (2025) → $5B (2030)** |
| **Biomedical Waste Rules** | 200+ CBWTFs; **$2.32B (2024) → $3.53B (2030)** |
| **7% biomass co-firing** within 300 km of Delhi (FY25–26) | Punjab pelletisation, 16 plants operating with 21 more expected |
| **₹66.17/litre guaranteed ethanol procurement** | Praj Industries built ~65% of India's ethanol plants |

And the negative control in the same tier: **CGWA groundwater-extraction permits exist and manufactured nothing** — no metering-as-a-service, no compliance-audit sector, nothing.

**The discriminator appears to be countability.** ZLD, CEMS and biomedical waste all attach to a finite set of licensable, inspectable, permit-dependent facilities; extraction is diffuse across millions of tubewells, so there is no inspectable object for a compliance industry to serve. The corollary for §5 is direct: where a harm has a countable set of responsible facilities and no obligation attached, **writing the obligation is the whole leg-2 intervention** — leg 3 follows on a base rate this table gives you rather than on hope.

**Mechanism 7 gains a commercial corollary.** Rajasthan's silicosis scheme does not only cap liability — the cap is *why no detection market formed*. A compensation ceiling is not a market signal, so compensation substituting for counting also suppresses the industry that would do the counting. The absence of a blood-lead testing market against 275 million exposed children is the same shape.

### Won the law, lost the execution

**New in this version**, and kept separate from the seven for the same reason the mandate table is: the seven describe how a failure is produced, and this describes how a *won remedy* fails afterwards. It is the leg-2 → leg-3 handoff breaking, and it is the dominant failure mode of activism in tier 1. Four of four rows where an affected-led movement actually moved the blocker show it:

| Win | What was won | Where it stalled |
|---|---|---|
| **Manual scavenging** | 1993 and 2013 Acts, co-drafted by SKA; 2014 Supreme Court judgment | 714 of 766 districts self-declare themselves free |
| **Silicosis** | Rajasthan Pneumoconiosis Policy 2019 — pension irrespective of income, DMF-funded corpus | **365 of 2,200 registered death cases aided (16.6%)**; detection still under 1% |
| **Gig work** | Rajasthan Platform Based Gig Workers Act 2023, first in India, welfare board with worker representation; Karnataka ordinance 2025 | Under High Court challenge by Swiggy, Zepto and IAMAI (June 2026) |
| **Cool roofs** | Demonstrated product, financing model, and 20,000+ homes (Mahila Housing Trust) | 20,000 against tens of millions; no building or rental code provision |

Two things follow. First, **the binding constraint in tier 1 is more often execution of a won remedy than winning it** — which is a different and less romantic problem than the one activism is usually organised for. Second, this is the specific gap a catalyst is positioned to hold: the movement that won the law is rarely the actor that can build the delivery layer, and neither leg-holder can make the handoff judgment for themselves.

## 4. Instrument and representation — how a row gets routed

The rewrite's structural addition. Every shortlist row now carries **two independent assignments**, and conflating them was the flaw in both of the old tables.

**Which leg** — activism, political institution-building, or social enterprise — is given by the mechanism, per the instrument column in §3.

**Which representation unit** is a separate question, and the discriminator is not the size of the harm. **The representation unit has to match the perception unit of the harm:** can the person bearing it perceive it, attribute it, and act against the party causing it?

| Representation unit | Condition | Tier-1 instances |
|---|---|---|
| **Local affected** | Harm concentrated, perceived by the sufferer, counterparty local and nameable | Manual scavenging (SKA), silicosis (a mine), cool roofs (a roof), gig fatigue (a platform's local fleet) |
| **Central organisation** | Harm latent, invisible or statistical — attribution is a specialist function the sufferer cannot perform | Lead (invisible, and the affected are children), asbestos (20–40 yr latency), AMR (no individual sufferer), air source-apportionment (the harm unit is an airshed) |
| **Enterprise** | Sufferer can perceive the harm *and* transact against it, or can be made able to | Cool roofs, fleet fatigue, data-centre water |
| **Central, at a named legitimacy cost** | Sufferer perceives the harm but **benefits from its cause** | Western Ghats slope zoning, farm power, ethanol feedstock |

Three consequences.

- **"No local leader" is a routing signal, not a disqualifier.** This is the same correction the three-instrument amendment made to the criterion one level up: what read as a reason to drop a row is a statement about which unit fits. Lead is not a dead row for lacking an affected-led movement — i-LEAP, Toxics Link (working on lead since 2005), Pure Earth India and Vital Strategies are the *correct* representation for a harm no sufferer can perceive. The live question is whether they are adequate, not whether they are local.
- **The two assignments are independent.** Asbestos is activism-leg with a central unit. Cool roofs is enterprise-leg with a local unit. Faecal sludge is institution-leg with no unit at any level.
- **Row four is the honest problem, and it needs stating rather than assuming away.** Where the affected are organised against the remedy, central representation is the only available route and it *costs* legitimacy. This is the one cell where the catalyst model runs against its own founding principle — that a protest must be led by the affected — and the cost has to be carried explicitly in the file. Pretending otherwise is how well-intentioned conservation gets done *to* the people of Wayanad.

**The unit can also be wrong in the other direction.** Warrior Moms is local representation of an airshed-scale harm (§3). A unit mismatch is not a smaller problem than a missing unit; it produces confident advocacy aimed at the wrong object.

**Five reads.**

- **The top five rows are the tier's target class, and four of them were at or near the bottom of the old capital ranking.** They share a shape: a known cheap fix, a single identifiable interest holding the blocker, and an existing organisation of the affected that has already moved the blocker once. What they need is not a movement and not an invention — it is the leg-2 → leg-3 handoff in §3 that none of them completed.
- **Silicosis and cool roofs were both mis-specified by the payer screen, in the same direction.** Silicosis was scored "no payer" when the DMF-funded pneumoconiosis corpus exists; cool roofs was scored a split-incentive problem when Mahila Housing Trust has financed 20,000 owner-occupied roofs through its own credit cooperative. In both cases the affected-led organisation had already solved the payer question and the screen could not see it, because it was looking for a venture-financed customer.
- **Asbestos is the cleanest diagnosis in the tier now.** A central representation unit exists, it is the correct unit for a 20–40 year latency, it has been campaigning since 2000 alongside the India Asbestos Victims Association, and there is still no ban. With representation eliminated as the explanation, the blocker is isolated to the import trade and nothing is left to attribute it to.
- **Data-centre water splits.** It was the top capital pick and it fails the representation gate outright — no affected party, because the harm has not yet landed. Under three instruments that is not a contradiction: it routes to institution → enterprise and never to activism, and it remains the only row in tier 1 where prevention rather than mitigation is still available.
- **Rental discrimination is now the tier's clearest structural void, and slope-failure zoning its hardest problem.** Rental has no law, no compliance market, 2% of urban tenancies formally registered, and no organisation making the demand — nothing to route to on any leg, which makes constructing a claimant the entire first step. Slope-failure zoning has the opposite pathology: the Western Ghats ESA is at its **seventh draft notification (27 July 2026) with the expert committee's deadline pushed to 2027**, opposed by Kerala, Karnataka and Goa, and the local opposition is plantation farmers and Wayanad/Idukki tourism — the population the landslides kill. Every other row can be worked with the affected. This one cannot, and that is the finding.

**Slope-failure zoning also inverts the tier's measurement finding.** Every other entry is blocked partly by not knowing: no registry, no surveillance, no disclosure, aggregated away. Here the detection layer is built, published and good — GSI's 4.3 lakh km² susceptibility map, ISRO's 80,000-slide atlas — and the harm continues because nothing binds a construction approval to it. It is the tier's proof that measurement is necessary and not sufficient, and a caution against reading "absence of measurement is a finding" as "measurement is the fix."

### Where writing an obligation is the whole leg-2 intervention

The most directly actionable output. Each has a countable set of responsible parties, a technically settled remedy, and no obligation attached — the exact configuration that produced six industries in §3:

- **Aquifer-draw disclosure for data centres.** Countable operators, existing sensing vendors (FluxGen).
- **Faecal sludge management.** The plastic/septage comparison is decisive: EPR created a $1.5B compliance market for a smaller harm; septage has a larger one and no instrument.
- **Blood-lead screening**, against 275 million exposed children, and **silicosis detection**, against ~52 million exposed workers with under 1% found. Both require unbundling detection from the compensation scheme that currently caps it.
- **A cool-roof provision in building or rental code**, which converts the split incentive in the rented stock into an obligation on the party that owns the roof. Note that this is needed only for tenancies — MHT has already closed the owner-occupied case commercially.
- **Duty-hour limits for commercial fleets**, on the aviation and mining precedent, which is what would price the liability that a fatigue product needs in order to have a buyer.

The caution attached to all five: an obligation only constitutes a market where the responsible parties are countable and inspectable. CGWA extraction permits are the standing proof that writing one against a diffuse population produces nothing.

## 6. Cross-need nodes

Three policy objects each produce failures in more than one tier-1 need. This matters more for choosing work than the need-by-need list, because a single intervention at a node propagates across needs while a need-level fix does not.

**Farm power tariffs and scheduling → water + sleep.** Free and subsidised tube-well electricity drives groundwater over-extraction (`02`); the same supply being scheduled *at night*, because non-agricultural users hold daytime priority, forces night irrigation and destroys farmers' sleep (`06`). One instrument, two needs — and one fix. Gujarat's Jyotirgram Yojana separated agricultural from domestic feeders in 2003–06 for ~US$290 million, cut groundwater withdrawal ~37%, halved farm power subsidies, and gave tubewell owners eight daytime hours on a pre-announced schedule. **The circadian benefit was an uncosted byproduct nobody claimed.** Solar agricultural feeders extend the same design. This is the highest-multiplier lever identified in the tier — and, note, it was delivered administratively with **no movement behind it at all**, which is why it sits in row four of §4's representation table rather than row one.

**Construction → air + water + shelter + sleep + food.** Sand mining degrades rivers and aquifer recharge; dust and silica load the air; asbestos-cement roofing and lead paint enter the housing stock; displacement destroys shelter; heat islands and night construction noise degrade sleep; land conversion takes cropland and lakes. Construction is 37% of global emissions and India is the second-largest cement producer. No single regulator holds this object, which is why it appears in five files as five problems.

**Ethanol blending targets → water + food.** The E20 target was met in 2025 and E27 is scheduled for 2030. On water (`02`) it commissions a demand class into Maharashtra, UP and Karnataka, where sugarcane already takes ~70% of state irrigation demand off ~6% of cultivable area, and adds 8–15 litres of spent wash per litre produced. On food (`01`) it routes ~12.7 Mt of FCI rice and ~7 Mt of maize a year into fuel, turning India into a net maize importer in 2024 and moving maize from ~₹15,000 to ~₹25,000/tonne against a feed sector that takes 60–70% of the crop. One instrument set — the EBP target schedule, the FCI allocation SOP, and MSP procurement — held by MoPNG, DFPD and FCI.

Three properties make it the most instructive node in the tier. **It was created on purpose**, on a published schedule, which is the opposite of every other entry here: this is not a failure that accumulated while nobody watched but one being provisioned forward, so the counterfactual is unusually clean. **It runs against another arm of the same state** — ethanol gives paddy and maize a second guaranteed buyer while the millet and pulse push tries to move land the other way. And **its central quantity is contested by system boundary rather than by measurement**: the water cost of a litre of ethanol is quoted at 3–5 litres, ~2,469, or ~10,790 depending on whether you count the distillery, the crop, or the rainfall, with each figure advanced by a party it favours and no agency publishing the blue-water number that would settle it. That is a distinct failure mode from the tier's usual measurement absence, and worth naming as its own: **not unmeasured, but measured to three different boundaries by interested parties.** Expect it wherever a harm is quantifiable in principle and the accounting frame is unregulated.

### Climate change: cross-cutting, but not a node

A **node** as defined above is a *policy object* — an instrument some identifiable body controls, so that one intervention on it propagates across needs. Farm power and construction qualify. Climate does not: it is a **threat multiplier** operating on the substrate itself, and no body inside India holds it.

| Need | Climate route | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Air** | Heat accelerates photochemical ozone formation; fire seasons lengthen, so 20th-century regulatory gains are partly given back | `03:76` |
| **Water** | HKH glaciers projected to lose up to 80% of volume by 2100; flood-drought whiplash against infrastructure sized for a stabler rainfall regime; GLOFs | `02:92,135,140` |
| **Food** | March 2022 heat cut wheat yields 10–15% (forecast 110 → 99 MMT); elevated CO2 reduces grain zinc ~9–10%, iron ~5–8%, protein ~6–8% (Myers et al., 2014) | `01:122,126` |
| **Shelter** | Heat load rising against 8% AC penetration and roofs that run 4–6°C hotter indoors | `04:112` |
| **Sleep** | Above 25°C outdoor night temperature the share sleeping under seven hours rises ~3.5%; +1°C ≈ three extra insufficient nights per 100 people per month (Minor et al., 2022) | `06:200` |

Four things follow that no single file states.

- **Climate is the only tier-1 threat that is simultaneously a cause and a consequence of another tier-1 failure.** Combustion PM2.5 kills directly and warms; construction is 37% of global emissions and degrades four other needs; irrigation pumping to compensate for erratic rainfall draws the aquifer that erratic rainfall no longer recharges. These are loops, not chains, and every other entry in this tier is a chain.
- **It inverts the tier's central finding.** §1 says tier 1 fails where a known solution meets an interest that benefits from the failure. Climate has the known solution and the identified blocker, but the blocker is neither national nor movable at the scale this project operates at. It is the one entry where "solution at hand, blocker identifiable and movable" splits: the first two hold, the third does not — and unlike the politically-blocked rows, the three-instrument amendment does **not** rescue it, because no leg reaches a blocker held outside the jurisdiction.
- **The remedy class it attracts is the visible one.** Tree planting and smog towers are to climate and air what road dust is to PM2.5 — see the vegetation section in `03-air.md`. Global land sink absorption fell to near zero in 2023 and 2024 while restoration was being promoted hardest, and the whole global tree-restoration potential is worth roughly ten years of emissions, once. Adaptation entries in §5 (cool roofs, work-rest rules, feeder separation) survive this test; mitigation-by-vegetation does not.
- **Which is why it is absent from §5 rather than at the top of it.** The shortlist filters on *actionable by a small team in India*. Climate fails that filter while being the largest force in the tier — the honest form of that statement is that §5 is a list of things one can work on, not a ranking of what matters.

### Overcrowding: cross-need, and owned by nobody

A third category, distinct from both of the above. Household density is not a policy object anyone controls (so not a node) and not an external force acting on the substrate (so not a multiplier). It is a **shared condition produced by failures in three needs at once and owned by none of them.**

The route runs through TB, where India carries 25% of the global burden. Decomposition of NFHS-4 and NFHS-5 attributes **~10% and 8.2% of the wealth-based disparity in TB prevalence to overcrowding**, with house type a further 8.9% and 9.7%; among the poorest quintile the population-attributable fractions are **34.2% for low BMI and 28.5% for indoor air pollution**. Those three inputs sit in three different files — undernutrition in `01`, household solid fuel in `03`, the room itself in `04:116` — and TB control is run as a fourth programme, on drugs and diagnostics, with no housing determinant entering any target.

- **The component that is falling is the one being measured.** TB incidence fell 21% from 2015 to 2024, faster than the global rate, on detection and treatment. The environmental determinants were not part of that effort and there is no evidence they moved. A programme can post a real, verifiable win while the substrate producing the disease is untouched — the leaded-petrol pattern in §8, arriving early rather than after the win.
- **Density has no denominator.** No slum enumeration since Census 2011 (65.5 million), no published persons-per-room distribution for urban India, and NSS's ~46 m² mean dwelling area averages a one-room slum unit together with a bungalow. Every other measurement absence in this tier is of a *harm*; this is an absence of the *exposure*, which is worse, because no intervention on it could be evaluated even if one were attempted.

Expect this category — cross-need, no owner, no denominator — to be more common in tier 2 than in tier 1, since safety failures are less physically localised than physiological ones.

**Method note:** these nodes were only visible after all six files existed. None is discoverable from within a single need. The register of nodes should be maintained at the tier-set level rather than per tier.

## 7. The latent set

Bills already incurred, none yet visible in mortality data. They score highest on irreversibility and lowest on political salience, which is the worst available combination and the one where early work is worth most.

- **Asbestos** — import of chrysotile remains legal; mesothelioma latency is 20–40 years; no surveillance, so the arrival will be invisible until it isn't.
- **Lead** — the harm is already in the children. 275 million with elevated blood lead, ~4 IQ points lost on average, cognitive damage that does not reverse. Unlike the others this bill is not arriving later; it has been paid and the country has not noticed, because the loss is distributed as slightly lower attainment across a generation rather than as identifiable cases. **It is also the tier's clearest case of a harm whose bearers cannot possibly be its claimants** — they are children, and the injury is imperceptible.
- **Silicosis** — ~52 million exposed, 10–20 year latency, detection under 1% of the exposed workforce.
- **Seismic exposure** — ~60% of the landmass, unreinforced masonry stock, IS 4326/13828 unenforced. The shelter analogue of asbestos latency.
- **Aquifer depletion** — the only tier-1 failure with a genuinely depletable stock rather than a flow.
- **Data-centre water demand** — the one latent entry where the harm has *not* yet been incurred. 1.5 GW of capacity now, 8–10 GW by 2030, ~75% of it in water-stressed regions, ~80% of cooling water evaporated, and no agency requiring disclosure of aquifer draw. Every other item here is a bill already run up and awaiting presentation; this one is still being run up, which makes it the only place in tier 1 where prevention rather than mitigation is still available. That is a stronger reason to work on it than its current size.

**The latent set is where the representation gate bites hardest.** A harm nobody has felt yet cannot have an affected-led claimant by construction, which is why every row in this section routes to a central unit or to enterprise, and none to local activism. Latency and local representation are structurally incompatible, and that is a general result rather than a tier-1 accident.

## 8. What worked

An earlier draft of this file claimed Odisha was the tier's only positive control. That was wrong in a way that changed the conclusion — it made the tier read as near-uniformly bleak when it isn't. Every file now carries a `### Where it worked` section.

**State programmes.**

| Win | Measured change | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| **Odisha cyclone shelters** (1999–2018) | Fatalities per million affected **779.3 → 3.55** | Tier-1 mortality is a state-capacity variable, not an income variable |
| **Leaded petrol phase-out** (completed 2000) | Child blood lead **18.1 → 12.1 µg/dL**; urban air Pb 1.6 → 0.2 µg/m³ | A single substitution, measurable across air, water and blood |
| **Bihar PDS reform** | Leakage **68.7% → 19.2%** | Sen's distributional failure responds to plumbing |
| **Jyotirgram Yojana** (2003–06) | Groundwater withdrawal **−37%**; subsidies halved; daytime farm power | One instrument can fix two needs at once — with no movement behind it |
| **Polio-free certification** (2014) | ~50% of world cases (2009) → zero | Indian state capacity executes at national scale on an unambiguous target |
| **TB incidence** (2015–2024) | **−21%**, faster than global | Partial, and worth stating precisely — burden is still the world's largest |
| **Salt iodisation** | >90% household coverage | Universal carriers beat behaviour change |

**Activism and institution-building.** Absent entirely from the first version of this file, which is why that version could not tell whether leg 1 works on tier-1 needs. It does.

| Win | Affected-led? | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **MKSS → RTI Act 2005** | **Yes** — workers and peasants of central Rajasthan, est. 1990 | A local struggle over unpaid wages and muster rolls became *jan sunwai* public hearings and then a national transparency law |
| **Right to Food Campaign → PUCL v UoI** (W.P. 196/2001) | Coalition of NGOs, academics and unions — not affected-led in the SKA sense | 28 Nov 2001 interim order: right to food read into Art 21; cooked mid-day meals in **all** primary schools within six months; ICDS expansion; continuous SC commissioner monitoring; → NFSA 2013 |
| **SKA → 1993 and 2013 Acts** | **Yes** — Dalit sanitation workers, est. 1994, deliberately unregistered as a movement rather than an organisation | Co-drafted the 2013 Act; 2003 PIL → 2014 Supreme Court judgment |
| **MLPC + affected workers → Rajasthan Pneumoconiosis Policy 2019** | Partly — NGO-convened, worker-based | Pension irrespective of income, DMF-funded corpus, livelihood provisions for patients' families |
| **IFAT + TGPWU → Rajasthan Act 2023** | **Yes** — union federation, 22 states, est. Dec 2019 | First Indian law for platform workers; welfare board with worker representation; Karnataka ordinance 2025 |
| **APFAMGS** (2003, farmer-managed groundwater) | **Yes** — farmer hydrological monitoring and crop water budgeting | Reduced abstraction observed — **but the reality-check literature holds that attribution to the project is unclear and effects on water-level decline limited and localised.** Recorded as a partial control with the disagreement intact, not as a win |

**MKSS is the template.** A locally-rooted struggle over a concrete local grievance became a national institution, which is exactly the leg-1 → leg-2 handoff the catalyst model describes, executed without a catalyst. It is the single most instructive entry in this file for the question of what this project is trying to do.

**Non-state wins that reach the bottom quintile.**

| Win | Reach | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| **Amul / GCMMF** | ~80–100M farmer families; **~80% of the consumer rupee returned to producers** against 35–40% in Western dairy | Producer-owned cooperative |
| **Sulabh** | **~15M users/day at ₹1–2 per use**, ~₹550M annual revenue, cross-subsidised | Nonprofit / social enterprise, municipal land |
| **SEWA parametric heat cover** (Ahmedabad, 2023) | 21,000 women across seven informal trades; payout at two consecutive days ≥43.72°C | Philanthropically backed insurance pilot |
| **Mahila Housing Trust cool roofs** | **20,000+ homes**; ModRoof financed at $1,100–1,400 per house; 3–8°C indoor reduction (figures vary by source) | Member-owned, women organised into Community Action Groups, financed through MHT's own credit cooperative |

**The central commercial finding of the rewrite: the affected-led organisation is itself the mechanism that makes a non-paying beneficiary payable.** `../../../CLAUDE.md` calls this the third path and describes it as a business-model frontier. Tier 1's evidence is that in every instance it was not spotted by an allocator and handed to a founder — it was produced by the constituency organising first. Mahila Housing Trust wrote a $1,100 housing loan to slum women whom the purifier and mattress industries do not serve at all; Amul returns 80% of the consumer rupee to producers by owning the margin; SEWA made an exposed population the beneficiary of a priced instrument. None is venture-financed and none of the dense venture-financed categories found across the six files — snacking D2C, purifiers, co-living, mattresses — reaches this population.

That reverses the sequence the old §4b implied. **"Cannot pay" in tier 1 has consistently meant "is not organised."** Amul, Sulabh, SEWA and MHT are four existence proofs and all four are small relative to the need; they do not settle the question, and the tier should not be written as if they do.

**Two readings to carry.** First, §5 should be read as **neglected, not intractable** — the mechanism of change in nearly every row above is administrative or organisational rather than technological. Second, the most instructive win is still the leaded-petrol one, because of how it failed afterwards: removing the largest single source produced a large measured decline, the effort stopped, the residual sources were never touched, and 275 million children still carry elevated lead. **A win against the visible source became the reason to stop looking.** Watch for that anywhere a headline number has improved — and note that it is the same shape as *won the law, lost the execution* in §3, one layer out.

## 9. What this tier does not contain

Stated explicitly, because the absences are findings.

- **No knowledge frontier except AMR — and the conclusion drawn from that in the previous version is withdrawn.** Seventeen of eighteen shortlist rows are implementation problems, and the old file concluded that this was mostly an artifact of method and that **"tier 1 is a poor hunting ground; tiers 2–5 are likely better."** That verdict does not survive the rewrite, because it was produced by a screen that ranked politically-blocked entries lowest — it partly manufactured the finding it reported. "Implementation frontier" is a verdict about *capital's* reach, not about the problem: an implementation frontier with a political blocker is dead to capital and live to activism → institution. Re-scored under three instruments, tier 1's five strongest rows are all implementation frontiers with movable blockers and existing local representation, which is the best available configuration rather than the worst. **What remains true of the method** is the bias itself: the institutional-source preference selects state-generated evidence, the seven mechanisms are all mechanisms of *institutional* failure with none for "nobody built it," and a national unit of analysis has the state as its only plausible owner. That bias is real and needs the commercial and representation passes to counteract it. What does not follow from it is that the tier is a poor hunting ground.
- **No entry where the payer and the beneficiary are the same person outside the top quintile, absent an organisation of the affected.** With the four wins in §8 as the exceptions, and the exceptions are the finding: every one of them constructed the payer through cooperative, union or nonprofit ownership. Across six files, every dense commercial category serves an urban discretionary consumer, a farmer buying inputs on subsidy, a facility under a compliance mandate, or a government tender. Not one serves the exposed population as a paying customer without that intermediate structure.
- **No problem solvable from inside tier 1.** The causes route upward into caste, class and power. Any intervention that stays within the tier is treating a symptom of a tier-3/4 structure.
- **No propagation need — scoped out deliberately, not overlooked.** `../../tier-taxonomy.md` derives the hierarchy from *persist/propagate*, and all six tier-1 needs are persist. Obstetric survival (MMR 88/100k, ~22,500 maternal deaths a year, led by haemorrhage and compounded by the rising anaemia in `01-food.md`) is physiological and tier-1 in character, but the failure mode is **absence of care at the moment of risk** rather than absence of a substrate. Filed in `tier2-safety/05-health-safety-net.md`. The distinction to preserve: birth is not the threat; the inability to survive a known and time-bounded medical event is.
- **No entry whose blocker is a security guarantee.** Manipur is in `04:104` — 58,821 displaced on government data, 281 relief camps still open in 2026, a ₹523 crore rehabilitation package, ~3,000 prefabricated houses built, and people who cannot go back because their villages are unsafe and their houses are held by armed groups. Absent from §5 for the same reason climate is: solution known, blocker identified, blocker not movable by any of the three legs at the scale available. Worth naming as a *category*, because the state's response — routing conflict rehabilitation through PMAY-G, a rural ownership-housing scheme — is the tier's most literal instance of building the thing you know how to build when the missing input is something else entirely.
- **No row where representation is absent at every level, except one.** Rental discrimination. Faecal sludge and AMR are close, and both have at least a professional or technical community to route through. Rental has no law, no market, and no claimant, which makes it the tier's only entry where the first step is constructing a constituency rather than working with one.
- **No problem the researcher personally faces — and the three-instrument frame partly answers this rather than restating it.** Tier 1 is met for anyone in a position to read this. The food loop closed because it could be run on oneself; none of these rows can be. The old version flagged this as an unsolved requirement for "short distance to the source of truth." Under the catalyst model the distance is meant to be closed by the leg-holder rather than by the catalyst: local leadership is not a nice-to-have but the specific mechanism that supplies the lived proximity the catalyst lacks. That reframes the requirement into a selection test — **prefer rows where a durable affected-led organisation already exists**, because they are the rows where the missing input is already present in someone else. The top five rows of §5 all pass that test. It does not dissolve the problem for the central-unit rows, where the proximity genuinely is not available to anyone.

## 10. Carried forward

- **Score every tier-2 row on both assignments from the start** — leg *and* representation unit (§4) — rather than deriving a shortlist and re-scoring it twice, which is what tier 1 did and what cost it two rewrites.
- **Run the commercial-landscape pass before the shortlist, not after.** In tier 1 it was run last and changed the ranking; run first it would have changed what got researched. Note also that `### Where it worked` must stop meaning "state programmes that worked" — three non-state wins and six activism wins were invisible until the passes went looking, and the activism set was missed twice.
- **Look for the affected-led organisation as a source, not only as a gate.** MLPC on silicosis and MHT on cool roofs both held facts — a DMF-funded corpus, a working $1,100–1,400 roof loan — that the institutional sources did not surface and that changed a row's classification. The organisations of the harmed are a research input, and tier 1 treated them only as a scoring criterion.
- **Distinguish nodes from multipliers in every later tier.** A node is a policy object someone controls (farm power, construction); a multiplier is a force acting on the substrate that no one in the jurisdiction controls (climate). Both cross needs; only the first is a place to intervene. A third category appeared late — the **shared condition with no owner** (overcrowding), produced by several needs jointly and controlled by none, which has no denominator and is the hardest of the three. Expect tier 2 to have at least one multiplier of its own — the candidate to watch is demographic and employment structure — and more shared conditions than tier 1 had.
- **Three hypotheses to test against tier 2.** (1) *Payer identity*: private capital is dense wherever the harmed party is also a paying customer and absent wherever it isn't — held in all six tier-1 files. (2) *Capital follows the mandate, not the harm*: six obligations each constituted an industry against one that did not, with countability of the responsible parties as the discriminator (§3). (3) **New — organisation precedes payment**: where a non-paying beneficiary became payable in tier 1, an organisation of the affected built the payment mechanism, and no allocator did. Tier 2 should be a harder test of all three, since safety failures have more identifiable counterparties — employers, insurers, lenders, platforms — than physiological ones do.
- **Test whether the leader/payer anti-correlation in §1 is a tier-1 property or a general one.** If it holds in tier 2, it is the most consequential structural finding of the project so far, because it means the two screens this project has used are systematically pointing in opposite directions and neither can be run alone.
- **Watch for *won the law, lost the execution* (§3).** Tier 2's counterparties are more likely to be employers and platforms than the state, so expect the pattern to appear as a won rule with no enforcement capacity rather than a won scheme with no disbursal. The Karnataka gig Act under industry challenge is the transitional case.
- Carry into tier 2: the seven mechanisms with their instrument assignments, the three cross-need nodes, the latent/active split, the representation-unit table, and the mandatory `### Where it worked` section.
- `tier2-safety/05-health-safety-net.md` holds a carry-forward note on obstetric survival, routed out of tier 1 deliberately.
- Tier 2 files still use their own H2 titles; normalise them when that tier is processed, not before.
