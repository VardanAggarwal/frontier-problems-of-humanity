# In-Group vs. Out-Group Standing — Whole Group's Rank Against Rival Groups (Tier 4: Esteem)

> Rank held collectively — a group's standing against rivals, absorbed by every member.

Tier definition: rank gates resource/mating access. Here rank is held collectively — a nation, empire, or civilization's relative standing against rival groups, not any one individual's. Threats to group standing (defeat, subjugation, humiliation) are absorbed by every member and often outlive the generation that suffered them.

## How the threat manifested

**National humiliation feeding revanchism**

- 1919 (Germany): The Treaty of Versailles imposed Article 231 ("war guilt clause"), stripped Germany of 13% of its territory and all colonies, capped its army at 100,000, and set reparations later fixed at 132 billion gold marks. German nationalists branded it a "Diktat" and the "stab in the back" myth (Dolchstoßlegende) claimed the army was undefeated but betrayed at home. Hitler's 1923 Beer Hall Putsch and later Nazi platform explicitly campaigned on reversing Versailles's status humiliation (Ian Kershaw, *Hitler: 1889-1936*, 1998).
- 1839-1949 (China): The First (1839-42) and Second (1856-60) Opium Wars, the "unequal treaties" (Treaty of Nanking 1842, ceding Hong Kong), extraterritoriality for foreign powers, and the 1900 Boxer Protocol indemnity are retrospectively bundled into the "Century of Humiliation" (bainian guochi) narrative, formalized as history-curriculum doctrine after 1991 and actively invoked in CCP legitimacy discourse and territorial claims (William Callahan, *China: The Pessoptimist Nation*, 2010).
- 1895 (China/Japan): The Treaty of Shimonoseki ending the First Sino-Japanese War forced China to cede Taiwan and recognize Korean "independence" (from Chinese suzerainty), a defeat by a nation China had long considered a tributary inferior — amplifying humiliation beyond material loss.

**Military defeat collapsing group standing**

- 1945 (Japan): Unconditional surrender (Aug 15, 1945) after atomic bombings, Allied occupation under MacArthur (1945-52), and Emperor Hirohito's renunciation of divinity (Jan 1, 1946 "Humanity Declaration") collapsed a status built on decades of imperial expansion (1895 Taiwan, 1910 Korea annexation, 1931 Manchuria).
- 1940 (France): The June 1940 armistice, German occupation, and the Vichy collaborationist government produced a durable national trauma; de Gaulle's post-war insistence on France's "great power" seat (permanent UNSC membership, independent nuclear deterrent by 1960) is documented by historians as a direct compensatory response to 1940 (Robert Paxton, *Vichy France*, 1972).
- 1905 (Russia): Defeat by Japan in the Russo-Japanese War — the first modern defeat of a European power by an Asian one — shocked Russian elite status assumptions and contributed to the 1905 Revolution.
- 1967 & 1973 (Egypt/Arab states): The Six-Day War's rapid Arab defeat (June 1967) was a collective pan-Arab humiliation (loss of Sinai, Golan, West Bank in six days); Egypt's partial recovery of standing via the 1973 Yom Kippur War's initial crossing of the Suez Canal was explicitly framed by Sadat as restoring Arab honor regardless of the war's ultimate military outcome.

**Colonized peoples' group-standing subordination as ideology**

- 1899 (Britain/US): Rudyard Kipling's poem "The White Man's Burden" (published in *McClure's Magazine*, Feb 1899, urging US annexation of the Philippines) articulated an explicit civilizational hierarchy casting colonized peoples as "half-devil and half-child," providing moral cover for colonial rule as tutelary rather than exploitative.
- 1884-85 (Berlin Conference): European powers partitioned Africa among themselves without African representation, codifying in the General Act a "civilized nations" framework (the "standard of civilization") that formally excluded African and Asian polities from equal sovereign standing in international law (Gerrit Gong, *The Standard of "Civilization" in International Society*, 1984).
- 1858-1947 (British India): The "civilizing mission" ideology following the 1857 Rebellion (Government of India Act 1858 transferring rule to the Crown) institutionalized racial hierarchy in the Indian Civil Service, clubs, and railway carriages barring Indians from spaces reserved for Europeans.

**Cold War status competition as group-standing rivalry**

- 1957 (USSR/US): Sputnik 1's launch (Oct 4, 1957) triggered the "Sputnik crisis" — a documented shock to American group-standing, driving the 1958 National Defense Education Act and NASA's creation, on the premise that Soviet technological superiority signaled ideological/civilizational superiority.
- 1961-1969 (US/USSR): Kennedy's May 1961 "we choose to go to the Moon" speech to Congress explicitly framed Apollo as a status contest ("we intend to win"), culminating in the July 1969 Moon landing as a scripted American recovery of group standing after Sputnik and Gagarin (1961).
- 1955-1991 (global): Superpower proxy competition for allegiance of newly independent states (aid, arms, prestige projects like Aswan Dam, stadiums, "friendship" universities) functioned as a continuous status-recruitment contest between capitalist and communist blocs for group-standing supremacy.

**Post-colonial status anxiety and assertion**

- 1955 (Indonesia): The Bandung Conference (April 18-24, 1955), convened by Sukarno, Nehru, Nasser and others, brought 29 Asian and African states together explicitly to assert collective standing independent of both former colonizers and Cold War blocs — Sukarno's opening address called colonialism "the ugly face of narrow-minded prejudice."
- 1961 (Yugoslavia): The Belgrade Conference founded the Non-Aligned Movement, formalizing Bandung's status-assertion logic into a standing bloc of 120+ states by the 2020s, explicitly rejecting subordination to either superpower's hierarchy.

## How humanity evolved to deal with the threat

**Formalized great-power standing recognition**

- 1945 (UN): The UN Charter's Article 23 created five permanent Security Council seats (US, UK, France, USSR/Russia, China) with veto power, institutionalizing recognized great-power status as a permanent structural feature rather than leaving it to contest resolved by war.
- 1975-2008 (G7/G20): The G6/G7 (founded 1975) and later G20 (1999, elevated to leader-level 2008) created standing-recognition clubs — membership itself functions as a certified marker of major-economy status, giving rising powers (China, India, Brazil) a non-military standing ladder to climb.

**Post-defeat rehabilitation frameworks**

- 1947-1952 (US/Western Europe): The Marshall Plan ($13.3 billion, 1948-52) explicitly rebuilt West German industrial capacity and reintegrated West Germany into European economic structures rather than repeating Versailles-style punitive extraction — George Marshall's June 1947 Harvard speech and later historiography (Tony Judt, *Postwar*, 2005) frame this as a deliberate lesson learned from Versailles's failure to produce durable peace.
- 1949-1955 (West Germany): NATO membership (1955) and founding of the European Coal and Steel Community (1951, with France) rehabilitated German standing as a partner rather than a permanently subjugated pariah.
- 1945-1952 (Japan): The US occupation (1945-52) combined demilitarization with a new constitution (1947) and rapid economic reconstruction; the 1951 San Francisco Peace Treaty restored Japanese sovereignty, and by the 1964 Tokyo Olympics Japan re-entered the international stage as a recognized, non-humiliated power.

**International sporting competition as peaceful status outlet**

- 1896 onward (Olympics): The modern Olympic Games (revived by Pierre de Coubertin, Athens 1896) created a recurring, rule-bound arena for national status competition via medal counts, explicitly designed to channel inter-national rivalry away from war.
- 1930 onward (FIFA World Cup): The World Cup (first held Uruguay 1930) similarly functions as a quadrennial global status tournament; national narratives around specific results (e.g., West Germany's 1954 "Miracle of Bern" win, widely credited with restoring post-war German national self-regard) are documented as symbolic status-repair events.

**Diplomatic protocol and mutual-recognition norms**

- 1961 (Vienna Convention): The Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations codified reciprocal protocol (precedence, immunities, ambassadorial rank) as a standardized grammar for signaling mutual respect between states of unequal power, reducing status friction in routine interaction.

**Post-colonial reparations and apology diplomacy**

- 1951 (Japan/Southeast Asia): The San Francisco Treaty and subsequent bilateral reparations agreements (e.g., 1965 Japan-South Korea treaty, $800 million in grants/loans) attempted material and symbolic standing-restoration for occupied nations, though contested as insufficient by victims' groups.
- 1995 (Japan): PM Tomiichi Murayama's official statement (Aug 15, 1995) apologized for Japan's "colonial rule and aggression," a template later governments partially reaffirmed and partially walked back — illustrating that apology diplomacy is contested and incomplete rather than a closed transaction.
- 2021-2023 (Europe/Africa): Germany's formal 2021 recognition of the 1904-08 Herero/Nama genocide in Namibia and France's 2021 acknowledgment of complicity in the Rwandan genocide reflect a growing, still-incomplete pattern of former colonial/occupying powers using formal apology as standing-restoration diplomacy for colonized groups.

## Note on the pattern

Group-standing threats recur on generational timescales and rarely resolve cleanly: Versailles's punitive model demonstrably produced worse outcomes than Marshall Plan-style rehabilitation, a lesson institutionalized after 1945 but re-litigated in every post-conflict settlement since (Iraq 2003, Afghanistan 2021). Sporting and diplomatic institutions provide low-stakes outlets, but material humiliation (colonization, unequal treaties, defeat) leaves standing grievances — China's Century of Humiliation narrative, still politically load-bearing in 2026 — that outlast the generation that experienced them by a century or more.

## Where this fails today

**US-China great-power status rivalry**

- 2022-2025 (US export controls): The Biden administration's Oct 2022 semiconductor export controls (barring advanced chip and chip-equipment sales to China) were expanded in Oct 2023 and again in Dec 2024, explicitly framed by US officials as preserving US technological primacy; China's Ministry of Commerce called the measures "tech hegemony" and retaliated with its own Dec 2024 export bans on gallium, germanium, and antimony (dual-use minerals) to the US.
- 2018-2025 (trade war): Trump-era tariffs beginning July 2018 escalated through 2025, with the Trump administration's April 2025 "Liberation Day" tariffs raising average US tariffs on Chinese goods above 100% in some categories before partial de-escalation; both sides' rhetoric consistently frames the dispute as a contest over which nation sets global economic rules rather than a narrow trade dispute.
- 2022-2026 (Taiwan): Speaker Nancy Pelosi's Aug 2-3, 2022 visit to Taiwan triggered the largest PLA military exercises around the island to date; China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs called it a violation of China's "sovereignty and territorial integrity," and PLA large-scale drills around Taiwan recurred in April 2023, May 2024, and Oct 2024, each explicitly tied by Chinese state media to reasserting China's status as the determinant of Taiwan's fate against perceived US interference.

**Russia's invasion of Ukraine as great-power restoration**

- Feb 21, 2022: Putin's televised address preceding the invasion argued Ukraine was "not even a state" and cast NATO's post-Cold War expansion as a humiliation of Russia that had to be reversed; his July 2021 essay "On the Historical Unity of Russians and Ukrainians" laid the ideological groundwork.
- Feb 2007 (Munich Security Conference): Putin's speech condemning a "unipolar world" and NATO expansion as broken promises is widely cited (e.g., by the Council on Foreign Relations) as the clearest early statement of Russia's grievance that its post-Soviet decline was treated as permanent subordination rather than temporary weakness.
- 2022-2024: Russian state messaging around the war consistently invoked restoring Russia's rightful "great power" (velikaya derzhava) status, alongside Western sanctions packages (over 21,000 individual/entity sanctions by 2024 per the EU) explicitly designed to diminish that same status.

**Global South collective-standing assertion**

- Aug 22-24, 2023 (BRICS Johannesburg summit): BRICS admitted Saudi Arabia, Iran, UAE, Egypt, Ethiopia, and Argentina (Argentina later declined) effective Jan 1, 2024, expanding the bloc to represent roughly 45% of world population and, per Goldman Sachs estimates, over a third of global GDP (PPP) — explicitly pitched by South African President Ramaphosa as building "a more balanced global order."
- 2023-2025 (de-dollarization): Russia, China, and India settled a growing share of bilateral trade in rubles/yuan/rupees after 2022 sanctions; the Jan 2025 BRICS presidency statement and repeated Putin remarks (e.g., Oct 2024 Kazan summit) framed reduced dollar dependence as ending "weaponization" of Western-controlled financial infrastructure — though no common BRICS currency has launched as of mid-2026.
- Jan 1, 2024 onward: BRICS expansion to ten members (adding Indonesia in Jan 2025) is widely analyzed (e.g., Council on Foreign Relations, Chatham House commentary) as status signaling — collective bargaining leverage against the G7 — more than functional economic integration.

**Nationalist rhetoric tied to humiliation narratives**

- 2014-2026 (India): Hindu nationalist discourse under the BJP repeatedly invokes "a thousand years of foreign subjugation" (Mughal and British rule) as justification for renaming colonial-era landmarks (e.g., Rajpath to Kartavya Path, 2022) and for a more assertive foreign policy posture; PM Modi's Aug 2022 Independence Day speech called for shedding a "slave mentality."
- 2018-2026 (Turkey): President Erdoğan's rhetoric around restoring Ottoman-era regional standing ("neo-Ottomanism") intensified after the 2020 Hagia Sophia reconversion to a mosque, explicitly framed as reversing a 1934 secularizing "humiliation."
- 2022-2026 (China): Xi Jinping's repeated invocation of the "great rejuvenation of the Chinese nation" (zhonghua minzu weida fuxing) — reaffirmed at the Oct 2022 20th Party Congress and Oct 2025 20th Central Committee plenums — explicitly ties domestic legitimacy and territorial claims (Taiwan, South China Sea) to reversing the Century of Humiliation.
